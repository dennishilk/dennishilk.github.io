DSKY command card & keyboard

VERB is the action; NOUN selects the data. Only the commands below are implemented. These displays use model data and simplified scaling.

  • V16 N60 E — monitor horizontal speed, vertical speed, altitude
  • V06 N60 E — read once; KEY REL returns to live values
  • V16 N36 E — simulation elapsed hours, minutes, seconds
  • V35 E — lamp test; RSET ends it
  • V37 E 66 E — take manual control below 150 m; museum restriction

Focus the keypad, then type V, N, digits, Enter (or E), Backspace (CLR), Escape (KEY REL), R (RSET). Tab and Space also operate each key. Signs and PRO are physical keys but rejected where no signed data/proceed request is supported. RSET resets lights; the flight RESTART button resets the scenario.

N60 field order follows Luminary099: VHORIZ, HDOTDISP, HCALC. Speeds here are tenths of ft/s; altitude is whole ft and saturates at 99999. N36 uses elapsed model time, not a historical GET clock.

Flight checklist · read before your first landing

START begins the clock. Set hover thrust to stop accelerating downward; existing descent does not stop immediately. Add thrust briefly to reduce the descent rate, then ease back. Tilt left/right to change horizontal acceleration; level out and counter-tilt to brake. Sliders and target selection take over manual control.

Success limits: vertical speed ≤ 2.5 m/s, horizontal speed ≤ 1.5 m/s, tilt ≤ 8°, within 40 m of the selected site. These are museum training limits, not certified LM operating limits. Outside the envelope the attempt fails. Try guided mode to watch a complete model descent; you may take over at any time.

Within the flight console: Up/Down change thrust by 1%, Left/Right change tilt by 1°, Space pauses/resumes. Shortcuts do not intercept selects, buttons or sliders. Hidden tabs and the computer view pause the flight; return and resume explicitly. Restart restores the selected scenario. No audio, uploads or external runtime services.

    02 / INSIDE THE APOLLO COMPUTER

    The computer that knew what mattered.

    The flight is paused while you explore. The original code remains below; return to Land the Eagle to resume.

    36,864words of fixed rope memory

    The program was encoded by routing wires through or around magnetic cores. Rope is read-only storage, not the working memory.

    2,048words of erasable memory

    Ferrite-core working storage held changing navigation and program data. A word carried 15 data bits and a parity bit.

    VERB / NOUNDISPLAY AND KEYBOARD

    The DSKY was the crew interface, not the computer itself. The AGC connected inertial navigation, radar, guidance calculations and control outputs.

    Explore the executive alarms ↓ · History, people and limits ↓

    1201 / 1202 · FAULT TOLERANCE

    An alarm. A restart.
    A mission that continued.

    The executive could not allocate another work area. 1202 meant no core sets; 1201 meant no VAC areas for interpretive jobs. Restart protection restored essential work instead of trying to finish every queued task.

    Unexpected rendezvous-radar counter activity stole computer cycles. Steve Bales and Jack Garman helped Houston assess the alarms. This was not a computer that simply ignored errors.

    ENGINEERING, HISTORY & HONEST LIMITS

    Small computer. Extraordinary responsibility.

    Built by people

    Eagle carried a Block II Apollo Guidance Computer. MIT’s Instrumentation Laboratory developed the Apollo guidance system under Charles Stark Draper. Raytheon manufactured the AGC hardware. Margaret Hamilton led the onboard flight software division; Hal Laning’s executive and restart design made scheduling and recovery central to the system. Don Eyles worked on lunar landing software. The achievement belonged to a much larger team, including the people who fabricated and tested the rope modules.

    P63 → P64 → P66

    P63 braked the spacecraft during powered descent. P64 approached the landing site after pitchover made it visible. P66 allowed the commander to control the descent rate and attitude with computer assistance. It was not simply flying with the computer switched off. Our model changes phase at 1,800 m and 150 m; these are educational thresholds, not Luminary’s original transition logic.

    What really runs here?

    Independent JavaScript flight physics and a bounded DSKY interpreter. Original Luminary 1A build 099 / LMY99 revision 001 source is displayed locally, never executed. The source view does not follow an instruction pointer. Virtual AGC’s GPL emulator is linked as further reading and is not bundled. A CPU alone would still need spacecraft sensor, I/O and navigation models.

    The physical model

    A 2D local plane with constant lunar gravity (1.62 m/s²), 45,040 N maximum thrust, 311 s specific impulse and variable mass. Fuel flow is thrust ÷ (specific impulse × standard gravity). At 60 fixed steps per simulated second we integrate thrust, tilt, velocity and position. Dry mass is 6,800 kg; initial fuel is 600 kg for training or 4,500 kg for the guided scenario. These are chosen scenario values, not a reconstruction of Eagle’s full mass history.

    A view, not a surveyed landing map

    The procedural craters, shadow and cockpit view are original illustrations. Collision occurs on a flat datum; craters have no collision geometry. There is no orbit, 6-axis attitude dynamics, radar error model, propellant slosh, real RCS or ascent/abort simulation. Throttle is continuous from 0–100%; the real descent engine had operating restrictions. Guided mode is a feedback controller, not a recorded trajectory.

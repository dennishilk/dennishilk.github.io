OPNsense at the edge

The D525 appliance has one job: be the router. It handles WAN/LAN routing, VLANs, fixed DHCP assignments for known devices, firewalling and Unbound DNS. Inbound home port forwarding is closed.

Guest means guest

The guest WLAN gets Internet and nothing else. Dynamic DHCP, no internal reachability, no capture, no AdGuard and no extra surveillance layer.

Three UniFi APs

Three identical long-range UniFi access points cover bedroom, living room and workshop. The bedroom AP is wired; the others mesh wirelessly. Their PoE injectors are intentionally outside the UPS.

VLANs by purpose, not paranoia

The network separates normal/server infrastructure, the Windows 98 Retro VLAN, WLAN/IoT, laptops, QNAP, individual Supermicro roles and guests. Cthulhu can reach all administrative surfaces. Ordinary clients cannot.

The X1 and Toshiba share a laptop network but are client-isolated. Each sees only its own QNAP share. The workshop PC cannot reach the homelab. The retro machine can keep being 1998 without being trusted as 2026.

The 1G switch is still enough

The HP ProCurve remains the central VLAN switch because bulk transfers can bypass it on dedicated links. It is factory fanless; a tiny Noctua was added anyway because “can't hurt” is also a valid cooling policy.

One deliberately separate Wi-Fi

A Starlink Mesh Mini in the bedroom broadcasts its own Starlink WLAN outside OPNsense and the homelab. Its glamorous primary client is an eight-or-nine-year-old Blu-ray/DVD player used for Netflix and Prime. It is not on the UPS, so during a power cut that branch simply disappears first.

FICTION NOTICE

The Lost Administrator is a work of fiction. Its characters, companies, organizations and events are fictional. Any resemblance to actual persons, living or dead, or to real companies or organizations is purely coincidental. Real technologies, products and historical references may appear within a fictional context.

The Lost Administrator is an interactive fictional experience built around the workstation of Debian system administrator Michael Weber.

Rather than following a traditional game structure, visitors explore the digital environment he left behind: emails, tickets, scripts, photographs, notes, calendars and terminal history.

At first, these fragments reveal an ordinary professional and personal life. Gradually, they begin to form a much larger story.

01 / STORY

The Novel

The Lost Administrator is a novel currently in development. The interactive workstation brings part of its fictional world into the browser by recreating the working environment of Debian system administrator Michael Weber.

Some of its contents connect directly to the novel, while others simply reflect the emails, files, routines and personal history that have accumulated throughout Michael’s working life.

Readers can explore beyond the pages of the novel. Visitors unfamiliar with the story can experience the workstation as a self-contained digital environment.

THE NOVEL IS CURRENTLY IN DEVELOPMENT

02 / ENVIRONMENT

Interactive Workstation

The first version of Michael Weber’s Debian workstation is available to explore. It recreates the working environment from the novel and will continue to grow alongside the project.

Visitors can examine emails, tickets, scripts, notes, files, terminal history and other traces accumulated throughout Michael’s working life. Not every item is a clue, and there is no score or fixed puzzle path to complete.

It is a place to explore and understand, not a challenge to beat.

03 / DETAIL

A Lived-In System

Michael’s workstation is designed to feel like a real system that has been used for years rather than a collection of obvious story clues.

Projects, logs, Git repositories, Bash history, notes, calendars, tickets, photographs and personal documents gradually form a picture of his professional routines, relationships and interests.

Some details may connect to the novel. Others exist simply because they belong in Michael’s life.

04 / APPROACH

Grounded Storytelling

The Lost Administrator begins with ordinary people, familiar routines and believable technology. The larger story develops gradually from a world that already feels real and inhabited.

The workstation follows the same principle. Not every email, file or terminal command exists to advance the plot. Many details simply document everyday work, relationships, interests and unfinished tasks.

When meaningful connections do emerge, they should feel discovered rather than deliberately placed for the visitor.

05 / PRIVACY

Browser-Only Simulation

Michael’s workstation is a fictional simulation that runs entirely inside the visitor’s browser. It is not connected to a real Debian system, company network or remote server.

Commands and actions affect only the simulated environment. The workstation cannot access files, programs or terminal sessions on the visitor’s computer.

Progress and local changes remain within that visitor’s browser, creating an independent version of the workstation for each person.

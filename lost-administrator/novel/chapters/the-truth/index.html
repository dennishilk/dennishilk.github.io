CHAPTER 3

The Truth

Friday, July 31, 2026

04:32 PM

Henry stood in Frank’s office with his phone in his hand.

The old laptop rested on the cabinet beside the desk.

Closed again.

Silent.

Next to it lay Michael’s Leatherman.

The narrow braided band Emma had tied through the lanyard ring rested against the dark surface beneath it.

Henry had seen Michael use the tool hundreds of times.

Opening packages.

Cutting cable ties.

Tightening screws that were never supposed to have worked loose.

Michael carried it every day.

He did not leave it on desks.

He did not forget it in rooms.

He certainly did not leave it beside an old powered-off laptop and disappear.

Behind Henry, Frank remained near the cleared desk.

He did not repeat himself.

He did not need to.

The truth.

Henry found Sarah’s number and pressed Call.

The line rang once.

“Did you find him?”

Sarah’s voice came immediately.

Henry looked at the Leatherman again.

“No.”

For a moment, neither of them spoke.

Through the phone, Henry could hear children in the background.

Paper moving across a table.

One voice saying something too quietly to understand.

A brief laugh.

Normal sounds from a house that did not yet know what had happened.

Sarah’s voice became quieter.

“What do you mean, no?”

“We searched the entire building.”

“And?”

Henry swallowed.

“He isn’t here.”

The children continued talking somewhere behind her.

Sarah said nothing.

Henry waited.

“Where’s his car?” she asked.

“Still outside.”

“His phone?”

“At his desk.”

Another silence.

Longer this time.

“Then where is he?”

Henry looked through Frank’s open doorway toward the hall.

“I don’t know.”

“What did you find?”

“The old archive room was unlocked.”

“Was Michael there?”

“No.”

Henry kept his eyes on the Leatherman.

“The desk lamp was on. There was an old laptop on the desk.”

He paused.

“And his Leatherman.”

Sarah answered immediately.

“His Leatherman?”

“Yes.”

“He never leaves that behind.”

“I know.”

“Was the room disturbed?”

“No.”

“No damage?”

“Nothing we could see.”

“Have you called the police?”

Henry glanced toward Frank.

“Not yet.”

“Why not?”

“Because we were still checking.”

Sarah took a breath.

The background voices had grown quieter.

“Then check once more,” she said. “But don’t wait after that.”

“I won’t.”

“Call me back.”

“I will.”

Henry ended the call.

Frank looked at him.

“What did she say?”

“To check once more.”

Frank gave a small nod.

“Then we check once more.”

They went downstairs together.

Brenda stood behind the reception desk watching them approach.

She had stopped pretending to work through the stack of forms in front of her.

“Sarah?” she asked.

Henry nodded.

“She knows we haven’t found him.”

Brenda looked through the entrance windows toward the parking lot.

Michael’s car remained exactly where it had been all afternoon.

Frank opened the front door.

Heat pressed against them the moment they stepped outside.

The sun had moved lower but had lost none of its strength.

They crossed the parking lot without speaking.

Michael’s car stood straight between the painted lines.

Driver’s door closed.

Windows up.

Nothing visible on the seats except the ordinary things that had been there before.

Frank tried the driver’s door.

Locked.

Henry checked the passenger side.

Locked.

He looked through the rear window.

No movement.

No damage.

No keys.

Frank walked once around the car.

The tires were intact.

The trunk was closed.

Nothing lay beneath it.

Nothing lay between it and the next vehicle.

They returned to the building.

Brenda was already standing.

“Everyone checks again,” Frank said. “Every office. Every restroom. Every storage room. Nobody assumes someone else already looked.”

The request moved through Chesapeake without needing explanation.

Chairs rolled back.

Doors opened.

Employees checked beneath desks, behind cabinets, inside empty conference rooms and along the outer hallways.

Henry went through the server room again.

Frank checked the executive floor.

Brenda opened the supply rooms near reception.

Steve walked through the workshop and loading area.

The custodian returned to the lower corridor and checked every space between the archive shelving and the rear wall.

They called Michael’s name.

Again.

And again.

No answer came.

Ten minutes later, the employees gathered near reception one by one.

Each shook their head.

No one had found him.

No one had seen him after the custodian watched him walk toward the archive room.

No one remembered an exterior door opening.

No one had an explanation.

Henry took out his phone.

This time, he did not look at Frank first.

He made the call.

“Port St. Lucie Police Department. What is the address of your emergency?”

“Chesapeake Signal Tech. Five-nine-zero-four Commerce Signal Parkway.”

“What happened?”

Henry looked through the windows at Michael’s car.

“One of our employees is missing.”

“All right. I’m sending an officer to you now. Stay on the line with me.”

Henry heard typing.

“What makes you believe he’s missing?”

“His car is still here. The doors are locked. His phone is at his desk. His computer is still running, and we found something he carries every day in another room.”

“When was he last seen?”

“At approximately one forty-seven this afternoon.”

“Has anyone seen him leave the building?”

“No.”

“Could he have left without being noticed?”

“I don’t know.”

Henry forced himself to stay precise.

“But he was supposed to pick up his twelve-year-old daughter at three-oh-five. He didn’t arrive, and he didn’t call.”

The typing stopped for a second.

“Do you know of any medical condition that could cause confusion or disorientation?”

“Not that I know of.”

“Any reason he might have left voluntarily?”

“No.”

“An officer should be there in approximately ten minutes. Don’t move or touch anything else you found.”

Henry looked toward Frank’s office upstairs.

“We won’t.”

“Is there any reason to believe he may be in immediate danger?”

Henry looked at the empty workstation across the office.

“I don’t know.”

Then he added the only thing he knew with certainty.

“But this isn’t something Michael would do.”

The call ended.

Henry immediately called Sarah again.

She answered before the second ring.

“They’re sending someone,” he said.

“How long?”

“About ten minutes.”

“Call me the moment they know anything.”

“I will.”

Henry lowered the phone.

At the Vance house, Sarah remained in the hallway for a moment before returning to the dining room.

Emma looked up immediately.

“Did they find him?”

Sarah kept her voice steady.

“Not yet. They’re still looking at the office.”

Emma studied her face.

Sarah knew she had not believed the whole answer.

Laura Vance saw it too.

She crossed the kitchen and quietly touched Sarah’s arm.

Sarah followed her a few steps away from the children.

“The police are going there,” Sarah said under her breath.

Laura’s expression changed.

“Does Emma know?”

“No.”

Sarah looked back toward her daughter.

“Not yet.”

Blue light moved across the reception windows.

Not flashing quickly.

Not accompanied by a siren.

Only a slow reflection as the patrol car turned into the employee lot and stopped near the entrance.

Brenda stood.

Henry was already moving toward the foyer.

Frank came down the stairs behind him.

“He was supposed to pick up his daughter at three-oh-five. He didn’t arrive. He didn’t call.”

“For Michael?”

“Completely.”

“More than twenty years.”

“No.”

“And he wouldn’t leave Emma waiting.”

“Show me his workstation.”

The terminal waiting on the monitor.

The open drawer.

Michael’s phone near the back.

The small empty metal tin.

The new USB drive beside it.

The empty chair.

“Has anything here been moved?”

Henry nodded.

“I called Michael’s phone. It started vibrating inside the drawer. I picked it up to end the call and put it back.”

“Anything else?”

“The laptop and his Leatherman were brought upstairs from the archive room.”

Daniels turned toward Frank.

“Who authorized that?”

“I did.”

“Why?”

“I didn’t know we were dealing with a possible crime scene. I wanted them secured where nobody else could touch them.”

Daniels made another note.

“From this point forward, nobody touches anything unless I tell them to.”

Frank nodded.

Daniels leaned slightly toward the monitor.

Last login: Fri Jul 31 17:41:26 UTC 2026 on tty1

m.weber@workstation:~$

“Is this exactly how you found it?”

“Yes,” Henry said.

Daniels read the login line again.

“So Michael logged in at one forty-one?”

“His account did.”

Daniels looked at him.

“What’s the difference?”

“The system recorded a login using Michael’s credentials. It doesn’t record who entered them.”

“Not this way. tty1 is the local console. Someone was physically at this workstation.”

“But you can’t prove it was Michael.”

“No.”

“Steve saw Michael sitting here around that time. So it was probably him. But the login record itself only proves that his account was used.”

“Who else knows his password?”

“Nobody should.”

“Could you access the account?”

“I could reset the password as an administrator. I couldn’t tell you what his current password is.”

“Could Mr. Kessler?”

“No.”

Daniels looked at Frank.

Frank shook his head.

“No.”

Daniels photographed the screen from several angles.

“Nobody uses this computer. Nobody disconnects it, logs out or types anything.”

He looked toward the open drawer.

“Is that the device someone saw him connect?”

Henry followed his gaze to the USB drive.

“I don’t know.”

“Who saw it?”

“Steve Harper.”

Daniels stepped away from the desk.

“I’ll speak to him next.”

The others remained out of hearing range.

Full name.

Date of birth.

Address.

Telephone number.

Position at Chesapeake.

Then he turned to the afternoon.

“What did you see Michael do?”

“He came back into the office, sat down and connected something to the computer.”

“I couldn’t tell. Something small. It might have been a USB drive.”

“Did you see it clearly enough to identify it?”

“No.”

“What did Michael say?”

“I asked what it was. He said, ‘Just checking something quickly.’”

“And then?”

Steve looked down at his hands.

“I turned toward Henry. I started talking about a project.”

“When did Michael leave the office?”

“I don’t know.”

“You were in the same room?”

“Yes.”

“No.”

“I wasn’t watching him.”

“Did he take the object with him?”

“I don’t know.”

“Is that what you saw?”

Steve studied it.

“It could be.”

“Can you identify it with certainty?”

“No.”

“Did Michael appear nervous?”

“No.”

“Angry?”

“No.”

“Frightened?”

“No. He seemed completely normal.”

Daniels closed that page of the notebook.

Brenda was next.

They spoke at the reception desk while Henry and the others waited in the technical office.

“Around one-thirty. He walked outside toward his car.”

“Did he drive away?”

“No. He came back a few minutes later.”

“How did he seem?”

“Completely normal.”

“He looked at me and nodded.”

“Something small. In one hand.”

“No. I couldn’t see it clearly enough.”

“Did you see him leave the building again?”

“No.”

“No. I was working. I only know that I didn’t see him leave.”

Daniels did not offer promises he could not keep.

“We’re circulating his photograph and description. We’ll check nearby roads, businesses, hospitals and available camera footage.”

“Do you want us to come?”

“Not yet. Stay where you are with Emma. I’ll tell you immediately if that changes.”

Sarah ended the call.

For several seconds, she remained alone in the hallway.

Then she opened the door.

Emma was already standing.

She had heard enough changes in Sarah’s voice to know that something had shifted.

“Mom, what’s happening?”

Sarah crossed the room toward her.

“They still haven’t found Dad at the office.”

Emma frowned.

“What do you mean, found him?”

Sarah held her daughter’s gaze.

“Nobody knows where he is right now. The police are helping them look.”

Emma stared at her.

“The police?!”

Her voice rose sharply.

Chloe stopped moving.

Laura appeared in the kitchen doorway.

Sarah stepped closer.

“They’re helping because nobody at the office knows where Dad went.”

Emma shook her head.

“Dad wouldn’t just go somewhere.”

“I know.”

“Did he leave his phone?”

Sarah did not answer quickly enough.

Emma understood.

“He did?”

“Yes.”

“And his car?”

“It’s still there.”

Emma’s face changed.

Not into tears.

Not yet.

Into certainty.

“Then something happened to him.”

“We don’t know that.”

“But you think it did.”

Sarah felt Laura watching from the doorway.

“I think something is wrong.”

Emma turned immediately toward the garage.

“I’m going.”

Sarah followed her.

“Going where?”

“To Dad’s office.”

Emma reached for the bicycle helmet she had left near the door.

“Emma, no.”

“It’s not far.”

“You are not riding anywhere.”

“Then drive me!”

Chloe came into the hallway behind them.

Laura moved from the kitchen.

Daniel Vance appeared from the rear of the house, drawn by the raised voices.

Emma pulled at the garage-door handle.

Sarah placed her hand over it.

Not hard.

Enough.

“Move,” Emma said.

“No.”

“They might need me!”

“The police are already there.”

“They don’t know Dad!”

The words landed with the force of truth.

The police did not know what Michael kept in which pocket.

They did not know what time he considered late.

They did not know which promises he treated like appointments and which appointments he treated like promises.

Chloe stepped closer.

“Emma, please stay.”

“He could be waiting for us.”

Laura remained beside Sarah without crowding Emma.

Daniel stood far enough back not to block her in.

No one tried to tell her everything would be fine.

Sarah kept her hand on the garage door.

“Officer Daniels told us to stay here.”

“I don’t care.”

“I do.”

“He’s my dad.”

“I know.”

“Then do something!”

Sarah took the words without defending herself.

Then she made the only promise she could make.

“The moment Officer Daniels says we should come, we get in the car and go. Immediately.”

Emma held the helmet against her chest.

“You promise?”

“I promise.”

“You won’t make me stay here?”

“Not if the police tell us to come.”

Emma looked at the garage door.

Then at Sarah.

Slowly, she placed the helmet on the narrow shelf beside the entrance.

Close enough to grab without thinking.

Sarah pointed toward the phone in Emma’s hand.

“Keep it with you. Volume all the way up. If Dad calls, texts or anything changes, you tell me immediately.”

Emma nodded.

“And you do not leave this house alone.”

Another nod.

Smaller.

“What if he needs me?”

Sarah looked at her daughter.

“Then we’ll be ready.”

Emma turned away before anyone could see her face clearly.

Chloe followed her toward the bedroom.

Laura waited until the door closed behind them.

“What are you going to do?” she asked.

Sarah looked at the silent phone in her hand.

“Wait for the police to call.”

She hated the answer as soon as she said it.

06:18 PM

In Chloe’s room, Emma sat on the floor beside Byte’s smaller transport cage.

Byte had come with her that morning because the weekend was supposed to be spent at Michael’s house.

His food was packed.

His bedding was familiar.

His cage waited beside Emma as though the plan had not changed.

Chloe sat quietly on the edge of the bed.

Emma watched Byte move beneath the bedding.

“He’s okay,” she said.

Byte pushed a small ridge of material toward one corner.

“Dad is always on time.”

Emma checked her phone.

No message.

No missed call.

“Maybe something broke at work.”

She looked through the cage bars.

“A server or a printer or something.”

Byte stopped moving for a moment.

Emma rubbed her thumb along the edge of the phone case.

“He said three-oh-five.”

Her voice became quieter.

“He doesn’t forget three-oh-five.”

She rested one finger against the bars.

“It’s going to be okay, Byte.”

The sentence sounded less certain when spoken aloud.

Emma tried again.

“He always fixes things.”

Chloe slid down from the bed and sat beside her on the floor.

She did not say Michael would come back.

She did not say the police would find him soon.

She only said the thing she could promise.

“I’ll wait with you.”

Emma nodded without looking at her.

She placed her phone on the floor between them.

Display upward.

Volume at maximum.

Byte began digging again.

Outside the room, the adults spoke in voices too low for the children to hear.

At Chesapeake Signal Tech, the search moved beyond the building.

Daniels stood with Henry near reception.

“If Michael left on foot, where would he normally go?”

Henry looked through the glass doors at the industrial road beyond the parking lot.

“Nowhere.”

Daniels waited.

“There’s nothing nearby he would walk to,” Henry said. “No restaurant. No store. No park. Michael drives everywhere from here.”

It did not prove he had not left on foot.

It only removed another ordinary reason for doing so.

Daniels requested checks of nearby roads, hospitals and traffic contacts.

Officers began contacting the surrounding businesses.

Had anyone seen Michael walking?

Had anyone noticed an unusual vehicle?

Did any exterior camera cover the access road between 1:30 and 3:15 PM?

Most answers were immediate.

No.

No one remembered him.

No one had noticed anything unusual.

One company two lots down had an exterior camera aimed across its loading entrance.

The camera did not show Chesapeake’s front doors.

It did not show the employee parking lot.

At the edge of the frame, however, it captured part of the shared access road.

The manager with access to the recording system was not on site.

The employee speaking with Daniels explained that the system began a new recording cycle on Monday morning.

Anything not preserved before then would be overwritten.

Daniels gave a clear instruction.

“Preserve everything from one-thirty onward. Don’t wait until Monday.”

The employee confirmed that the relevant sequence would be copied and protected.

No one viewed it that evening.

Not yet.

Daniels returned to Henry.

“Does Michael have any enemies?”

Henry stared at him.

“Michael?”

“Anyone who threatened him. A former employee. An angry customer. Someone with a reason to hurt him.”

Henry considered every argument he could remember.

Every frustrated user.

Every customer whose emergency had been caused by something they had done themselves.

Every employee Michael had corrected without humiliating.

“Not that I know of.”

Daniels turned toward Frank.

“And professionally?”

Frank folded his arms.

“No one I would call an enemy.”

“Any disputes?”

“Customers complain. Former employees leave angry. That happens in every company.”

“Did any of them threaten Michael specifically?”

“Not that I’m aware of.”

“Was he working with anything sensitive?”

“Michael had access to our servers, customer backups and archived records. Most of it was routine technical work.”

Daniels looked toward Michael’s desk.

“I need a list of every customer account, internal investigation, employee dispute and archived record he accessed or worked on during the last thirty days.”

Frank nodded.

“I’ll have the list prepared.”

“Do not alter or remove anything while preparing it.”

“Understood.”

“I also need a complete list of everything Michael was scheduled to do today.”

Frank looked toward Henry.

“Assigned work, completed work, open tickets?”

“Everything. Keep the categories separate. I need to know what the records confirm, not what anyone assumes he did.”

Daniels pointed toward Michael’s workstation.

“Do not use that computer to prepare the list.”

“We won’t.”

Henry and Frank used another workstation.

They reviewed the ticket system, the shared schedule and the system records without entering Michael’s account.

Planned work.

Completed work.

Open tasks.

The overnight reports he had reviewed.

The archive threshold he had closed.

The parser he had corrected.

The failed drive he had entered into the intake log.

The backup verification completed at 1:26 PM.

The final calendar entry.

3:05 PM — PICK UP EMMA — VANCE HOUSE

The entry remained there as though the afternoon had simply failed to continue.

Daniels watched Henry separate fact from inference.

“What was he scheduled to do in the archive room today?”

“Nothing,” Frank said.

Daniels looked toward the lower floor.

“Was he authorized to be there?”

“Yes. He had his own key and unrestricted access whenever his work required it.”

Henry added:

“He might have gone down to check an old backup, retrieve a part or look something up. He wouldn’t need to announce it.”

“Do you know why he went there today?”

“No.”

“Can anyone tell whether anything is missing from the room?”

Henry and Frank looked at each other.

Frank answered first.

“Not without comparing the records.”

“And the items that were never individually inventoried?”

Henry looked toward the hallway.

“Michael would know.”

Again, the answer led back to the man who was not there.

Daniels asked Brenda and Henry for the clothing Michael had worn that morning.

Brenda remembered first.

“Dark blue Chesapeake Signal Tech polo. Dark jeans. Brown work shoes.”

“Belt?”

“Black.”

Henry added:

“The shirt was untucked.”

Daniels looked up.

“You’re certain?”

“Completely. Michael has refused to tuck that shirt into his pants for as long as I’ve known him.”

For a fraction of a second, Henry’s voice carried something almost familiar.

Then it was gone.

Daniels transmitted the description.

Missing adult male.

Current photograph available.

Dark blue company polo worn untucked.

Dark jeans.

Brown work shoes.

Black belt.

No vehicle.

Possibly on foot.

May be carrying car keys and a slim dark wallet.

Does not have his phone.

Daniels finished the transmission and looked at Henry.

“All right. We’re expanding the search.”

“What does that mean?”

“Nearby roads, businesses, hospitals, traffic contacts and every available recording we can preserve.”

Daniels looked toward the employees waiting in the office.

“This is no longer just your company looking for an employee.”

Henry held his gaze.

“You’re treating him as missing?”

“Yes.”

The word settled across the room.

Official now.

Recorded.

Beyond Chesapeake.

Missing.

Daniels called Sarah again.

She answered on the first ring.

“Mrs. Weber, we’ve completed the initial searches of the company property and Michael’s residence.”

“And?”

“We haven’t found him.”

Sarah looked toward Chloe’s closed bedroom door.

“What happens now?”

“We’re expanding the search. Nearby roads, businesses, hospitals and available camera footage.”

“Do you want us to come?”

“Not yet. Stay where you are with Emma. I’ll tell you immediately if that changes.”

“All right.”

Sarah ended the call and went upstairs.

The bedroom door opened before she reached it.

Emma stood there with her phone in her hand.

“Are we going?”

“Not yet.”

“Did they find him?”

Sarah shook her head.

“No.”

Emma looked past her toward the stairs.

“Did they check Dad’s house?”

Sarah hesitated only briefly.

“Yes.”

“And he wasn’t there?”

“No.”

Emma lowered herself back to the floor beside Byte’s cage.

Chloe remained close beside her.

“Then where is he?”

Sarah had already asked Henry the same question.

She still had no answer.

07:42 PM

Additional officers arrived at Chesapeake as daylight began to soften outside the windows.

Daniels briefed them before anyone touched the scene.

He listed every known change.

Michael’s phone had been lifted once to stop the call, then returned to the drawer.

Henry had opened the ThinkPad T43 and pressed the space bar.

The custodian had carried the laptop from the archive room to Frank’s office.

Henry had carried the Leatherman.

Frank had authorized both movements.

The workstation, USB drive and empty metal tin had otherwise remained untouched.

The archive room had been entered by Henry and the custodian before police arrived.

No detail was omitted because it was inconvenient.

The T43 and the Leatherman were photographed in Frank’s office exactly where they had been placed.

The faded Chesapeake inventory label on the ThinkPad was recorded.

The old IBM logo remained visible on the lid.

Under Daniels’s direction, an officer wearing gloves carried the laptop downstairs.

Another carried the Leatherman separately.

A clean evidence sheet was placed on the unused side of Michael’s desk.

The ThinkPad was set on one side.

The Leatherman on the other.

They were not returned to the drawer.

They were not mixed with the phone, USB drive or parts tin.

Each item received its own marker and its own photographs.

The USB drive remained where it had been found until it too was documented and secured separately.

The phone was photographed in the drawer.

The jumpers from the lower hallway were collected after their positions had been measured and recorded.

The shredder in Frank’s office was photographed from every side.

Its collection bin had not been emptied.

The paper strips inside were secured together for later examination.

No one knew whether they mattered.

No one was willing to assume they did not.

Michael’s workstation remained powered on.

The screen was photographed again.

The visible ports and connected cables were documented.

No one typed a command.

No one logged out.

No one unplugged it.

The cursor continued blinking beneath the prompt.

Frank closed Chesapeake Signal Tech for the rest of the day.

Brenda contacted the few customers still expecting responses and told them only that the office had closed because of an unexpected emergency.

She did not explain whose emergency.

One by one, the remaining employees were allowed to leave after their information had been confirmed.

They were instructed to remain reachable.

They were asked not to compare statements or discuss details of the search.

Steve packed his tablet without opening the presentation that had occupied him for most of the day.

He looked once toward Michael’s desk before leaving.

Brenda collected her handbag from beneath reception.

The ceramic honey pot remained beside the visitor forms.

She turned off the reception lamp, then turned it back on when an officer told her the foyer still needed to be photographed.

Frank remained until Daniels told him there was nothing further he could do that evening.

Henry stayed at his desk.

“You can go home, Mr. Sullivan,” Daniels said.

“I’m staying.”

Daniels studied him for a moment.

“Then stay out of the secured areas and let us work.”

Henry nodded.

He sat down at his own workstation but did not wake the screen.

Across from him, Michael’s chair remained empty.

Beyond it, officers moved carefully through the space where an ordinary Friday had ended.

10:48 PM

At the Vance house, nobody suggested that Sarah and Emma should leave.

The weekend bag remained near the front hallway.

Emma’s bicycle helmet remained on the shelf beside the garage door.

Byte’s transport cage stayed in Chloe’s room.

Laura prepared food that almost no one ate.

Daniel checked the doors twice, not because he expected anything outside, but because it was something he could do.

Sarah kept her phone on the table in front of her.

Every vibration made her reach for it.

Most were messages from people who had not yet learned what questions not to ask.

None were from Michael.

Upstairs, Emma and Chloe remained awake long after the house had grown quiet.

They did not talk much.

Emma checked her phone until the screen dimmed.

Then touched it to wake it again.

No call.

No message.

Eventually, exhaustion did what reassurance could not.

Chloe fell asleep first.

Emma remained awake beside her, listening to Byte move through the bedding.

Her phone rested near her hand.

Volume at maximum.

At some point, her eyes closed too.

Downstairs, Sarah sat with Laura and Daniel in the darkened kitchen.

No one told the others to go to bed.

No one knew what morning would change.

At Chesapeake Signal Tech, Michael’s workstation was still running.

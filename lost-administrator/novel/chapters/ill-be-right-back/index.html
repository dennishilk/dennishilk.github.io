CHAPTER 2

I’ll Be Right Back

Friday, July 31, 2026

01:30 PM

The front doors opened as Michael approached.

Warm Florida air replaced the steady cool of the building almost immediately.

He stepped outside and crossed the employee parking lot.

From behind the reception desk, Brenda Montgomery saw him pass the windows.

Car keys in hand.

Nothing unusual.

She returned her attention to the forms in front of her.

A short time later, movement beyond the glass caught her eye again.

Michael was walking back toward the entrance.

He carried something small in one hand.

Brenda could not see what it was.

Michael opened the door and stepped back into the reception area.

Their eyes met.

He gave her a small, friendly nod.

Brenda returned it.

Then Michael continued toward the technical office.

He did not appear hurried.

He did not appear distracted.

He looked exactly as he had when he left.

01:41 PM

Michael returned to his desk.

Henry was still working through the open maintenance queue.

Steve had moved one of the diagrams in his presentation from the left side of the slide to the right and was now considering moving it back.

Neither of them looked up immediately.

Michael sat down.

The terminal waited on the screen in front of him.

He connected the small object to the workstation.

Steve noticed the movement.

“What’s that?”

Michael kept his eyes on the screen.

“Just checking something quickly.”

Steve watched for another second.

Then he turned toward Henry.

“Hey, Henry—did you look at my new project yet? Turned out pretty good, right?”

Henry continued typing.

“I haven’t even opened the email.”

Steve leaned one hand against the divider.

“That’s fine. I can show you the important parts.”

Michael remained at the terminal.

01:47 PM

Michael left the technical office a few minutes later.

No one stopped him.

In the lower part of the building, the custodian stood beneath an open ceiling fixture, replacing a burned-out light bulb.

He heard footsteps on the stairs and looked up.

Michael was coming down.

“Afternoon,” the custodian said.

Michael gave him a brief nod.

“Afternoon.”

He continued along the hallway toward the old archive and backup room.

The custodian returned his attention to the light fixture.

A moment later, the new bulb came on.

He did not see Michael come back.

03:05 PM

Emma looked through the front window.

The space beside the curb was empty.

Behind her, several newly printed pages of Major Tom covered the Vances’ dining table. Chloe was adding color to one of the panels while Laura worked in the kitchen.

Emma checked the time on her phone.

3:05 PM.

Michael was not there.

She waited another few seconds.

Then she looked through the window again.

No familiar car turned onto Sandhill Heron Way.

Chloe glanced up from the table.

“Is that your dad?”

Emma shook her head.

“No.”

She unlocked her phone and opened their conversation.

Are you here?

The message showed as sent.

Emma remained near the window.

A minute passed.

No reply.

She pressed Michael’s name and called him.

The phone rang.

And rang.

Then the call ended without an answer.

Emma waited a few seconds and tried again.

The phone rang until the call ended a second time.

Still no answer.

Emma lowered the phone.

Chloe had stopped coloring.

“He’s probably just late,” she said.

Emma looked at the time on her phone again.

Michael was never just late.

A few minutes later, Laura Vance came into the dining room.

She looked from Emma to the phone in her hand.

“Still nothing?”

Emma shook her head.

Laura glanced toward the window.

“Call your mom.”

Emma opened Sarah’s contact and pressed Call.

Sarah answered almost immediately.

“Hey, sweetheart.”

“Dad isn’t here.”

A short pause followed.

“What do you mean?”

“He said three-oh-five.”

Sarah knew what that meant.

“Did you call him?”

“Twice. He didn’t answer.”

Sarah kept her voice calm.

“Okay. Stay there with Laura and Chloe.”

Emma looked through the window again.

“Are you coming?”

“I’m going to make a few calls first. Then I’ll come get you.”

“Okay.”

“Stay inside. I’ll call you back.”

Emma ended the call.

At home, Sarah was already searching for Michael’s office number.

There were times when Michael disappeared into a computer problem and stopped noticing everything around him.

A server.

A backup.

Something that should have taken five minutes and somehow became an hour.

For one brief moment, Sarah allowed herself to believe that was all this was.

Then she looked at the time on her phone.

Michael might forget lunch.

He might forget to answer his phone.

He would not forget Emma.

Sarah found Michael’s direct number and called.

The line rang several times.

No answer.

She ended the call and tried again.

Still nothing.

Michael kept the office phone close enough to reach without standing.

If he was at his desk, he should have heard it.

Sarah opened the company website and called the main number.

Brenda answered on the second ring.

“Chesapeake Signal Tech, this is Brenda.”

“Hi, Brenda. It’s Sarah Weber.”

Brenda straightened slightly behind the reception desk.

“Hi, Sarah.”

“Is Michael there?”

Brenda looked toward the technical office.

His desk was visible from where she sat.

The chair was empty.

“I haven’t seen him for a little while.”

“He was supposed to pick Emma up at three-oh-five.”

Brenda looked at the clock.

“Oh.”

“He isn’t answering his phone or his direct line.”

“I’ll check.”

Brenda stood and carried the receiver with her as far as the cord allowed.

“Henry?”

Henry looked up from his monitor.

“What’s wrong?”

“Sarah’s on the phone. She can’t reach Michael.”

Henry glanced toward Michael’s desk.

Only then did the empty chair seem unusual.

“I’ll find him,” he said.

Brenda returned to the phone.

“Henry’s checking now.”

Sarah looked at the time again.

“Please tell him to call me as soon as he knows where Michael is.”

“I will.”

Henry was already walking toward Michael’s workstation.

03:18 PM

Henry reached Michael’s desk.

The chair was pushed back slightly.

His coffee mug was gone, already placed in the dishwasher earlier that afternoon.

The workstation was still running.

Both monitors were awake, but only one displayed anything.

A terminal.

Nothing else.

No ticket system.

No email.

No open files.

Just the login message and a waiting prompt.

Henry looked at the time in the corner of the screen.

Then at the empty chair.

He did not touch the keyboard.

“Michael?”

No answer came from the office.

Steve looked over the divider.

“He’s not back?”

Henry shook his head.

Brenda was still holding the call at reception.

Henry picked up the phone on Michael’s desk and pressed the line button.

“Sarah?”

“Did you find him?”

“Not yet.”

Henry looked at the terminal again.

“His computer is still running. The screen says when he last logged in.”

“What time?”

“About one forty-one.”

“And nobody’s seen him since?”

“I’m checking.”

Sarah was silent for a moment.

“Henry, he was supposed to pick up Emma.”

“I know.”

Henry looked beneath the desk.

Then toward the hallway.

“I’m going to call his cell.”

Henry placed Sarah on hold and selected Michael’s number on a second line.

The call connected.

For a second, nothing happened.

Then a faint vibration came from somewhere close by.

Henry lowered the receiver and listened.

The sound came again.

Muted.

Inside the desk.

He pulled the already open drawer farther toward him.

Michael’s phone lay near the back, vibrating against the wood.

Beside it sat a small metal tin.

Empty.

A USB drive rested next to it.

Henry picked up Michael’s phone and ended the call.

He placed it back in the drawer where he had found it.

Sarah was still waiting on the line.

“His phone is here,” Henry said.

“At his desk?”

“In the drawer.”

Sarah did not answer immediately.

Henry looked at the other objects again.

The tin normally held screws, adapters and whatever small parts Michael did not want disappearing into the office.

Now there was nothing inside it.

The USB drive looked new.

Too new.

Henry had the vague impression that he had seen Michael holding one like it before.

Maybe the same one.

Maybe not.

He left both where they were.

From Michael’s desk, the employee parking lot was visible through the window.

Henry stepped closer to the glass.

Michael’s car stood in its usual space.

Straight between the lines.

Unchanged.

“His car is still here too,” Henry said.

“Then he has to be somewhere in the building.”

“I’m going to look again.”

Sarah took a breath.

“I’m going to Emma.”

“Okay.”

“Call me the second you find him.”

“I will.”

Sarah ended the call.

Henry placed the receiver back onto Michael’s desk.

The terminal continued waiting.

The phone remained in the open drawer.

And Michael’s car remained exactly where he had left it.

Henry checked the server room first.

The door was closed but unlocked.

He opened it and stepped inside.

Fans filled the room with their steady mechanical hum.

Status lights blinked across the racks.

“Michael?”

No answer.

Henry checked behind the open cabinet doors and along the narrow space between the racks.

Empty.

He moved on to the restrooms.

Then the surrounding offices.

The workshop.

The break room.

Each place offered the same result.

No Michael.

Henry returned to the main hallway and checked the exterior doors.

Outside, the afternoon heat had settled over the building.

He walked around the employee entrance and along the side of the property, looking between the parked vehicles and toward the service area.

Michael’s car remained in its usual space.

The driver’s door was closed.

No one stood near it.

Henry went back inside.

He had now checked every place Michael normally went during the day.

The server room.

The offices.

The workshop.

The break room.

Outside.

Michael was nowhere.

Only then did Henry head upstairs to Frank Kessler’s office.

Frank sat behind his desk on the upper floor.

A large stack of documents covered one side of the desk. He was reading through the top page when Henry appeared in the doorway.

Frank looked up.

“What is it?”

“I can’t find Michael.”

Frank placed the page onto the stack.

“What do you mean, you can’t find him?”

“He isn’t at his desk. His phone is in the drawer. His car is still outside.”

Frank looked past Henry toward the clock mounted on the wall.

“Hm.”

He considered the time for a moment.

“Strange.”

Henry waited.

Frank leaned back slightly.

“Well, he has to be somewhere.”

“I’ve checked the server room, the restrooms, the offices, the workshop, the break room and outside.”

“And?”

“Nothing.”

Frank’s expression changed only slightly.

“When and where was he last seen?”

Henry opened his mouth.

Then stopped.

“I saw him at his desk around noon.”

“Around noon?”

“I didn’t look at the time.”

Frank glanced at the clock again.

“Find out.”

Henry nodded.

“Ask Montgomery. Ask Steve. Ask anyone who might have seen him after lunch.”

Henry turned back toward the doorway.

“And Sullivan?”

Henry looked over his shoulder.

“Start with facts.”

Henry continued downstairs.

For the first time, finding Michael was no longer only a matter of checking rooms.

They needed a timeline.

Henry returned to reception.

Brenda looked up from the forms on her desk.

“What did Frank say?”

“He wants to know when Michael was last seen.”

Brenda glanced toward the technical office.

“I saw him go outside.”

“When?”

She looked at the clock, reconstructing the afternoon from the calls and deliveries that had passed through reception.

“Around one-thirty.”

“Did he leave?”

“He went to his car.”

Henry stopped.

“You saw him at the car?”

“I saw him walk across the parking lot. Then he came back a few minutes later.”

“Was he carrying anything?”

Brenda hesitated.

“Something small.”

“What kind of thing?”

“I don’t know.”

“A box?”

“Maybe.”

“A drive?”

“I couldn’t see it clearly.”

Henry waited.

Brenda looked through the front windows toward Michael’s car.

“He had it in one hand. That’s all I remember.”

“How did he seem?”

“Normal.”

“Hurried?”

“No.”

“Upset?”

“No. He looked at me when he came back in.”

“What did he say?”

“Nothing. He nodded.”

“And after that?”

“He went toward the office.”

“Did you see him leave again?”

Brenda shook her head.

“No.”

Henry wrote the time down.

1:30 PM.

Michael had gone to his car.

Michael had returned.

Whatever happened had happened after that.

Henry found Steve at his desk.

The presentation was still open on the third monitor.

“What’s going on?” Steve asked.

“When did you last see Michael?”

Steve glanced toward the empty workstation.

“After lunch.”

“Can you be more specific?”

Steve thought for a moment.

“He came back from outside.”

“From his car?”

“I don’t know. He came into the office and sat down.”

“Was he carrying anything?”

“Something small.”

Henry looked at him.

“Brenda said the same thing.”

Steve turned slightly in his chair.

“He plugged it into the computer.”

“What was it?”

“I didn’t see.”

“A USB drive?”

“Maybe.”

“What did Michael say?”

“I asked what it was.”

“And?”

“He said, ‘Just checking something quickly.’”

Henry waited.

Steve looked back at the presentation on his monitor.

Then his expression changed.

“Oh.”

“What?”

“I started talking about the prototype.”

Henry stared at him.

“You asked Michael what he was doing, and then you started talking about your project?”

“I thought he was busy.”

“You always think people are busy when they stop listening to you.”

“That isn’t fair.”

Henry continued looking at him.

Steve lowered his voice.

“I asked whether you had seen the email.”

Henry remembered the moment.

The tablet.

The presentation.

Steve waiting for an answer.

I haven’t even opened the email.

He had been looking at his own screen.

Not at Michael.

“When did Michael leave the office?” Henry asked.

Steve shook his head.

“I don’t know.”

“You didn’t see him go?”

“No.”

Henry wrote down what Steve had told him.

Michael had returned.

He had connected something to the workstation.

And a few minutes later, no one in the technical office had been watching him.

Henry found the custodian in the maintenance closet near the rear stairwell.

A replacement bulb and the empty cardboard sleeve from the old one rested on the workbench beside him.

“Did you see Michael this afternoon?”

The custodian looked up.

“Yes.”

“When?”

“Quarter to two. Maybe a little after.”

“Where?”

“Downstairs.”

Henry glanced toward the stairwell.

“What was he doing?”

“Walking.”

“Which way?”

The custodian pointed along the lower hallway.

“Toward the old archive room.”

“The backup room?”

“That one.”

“Did he say anything?”

“Just afternoon.”

“How did he seem?”

The custodian considered the question.

“Normal.”

“Was he carrying anything?”

“I didn’t notice.”

Henry looked toward the hallway again.

“You were changing the light?”

The custodian nodded.

“Bulb burned out near the bottom of the stairs.”

“And you stayed down here afterward?”

“For a while.”

“Did you see Michael come back?”

“No.”

Henry stopped writing.

“You’re sure?”

“I would have seen him pass.”

The old archive and backup room stood at the far end of the lower hallway.

It was rarely used now.

Most of the records stored inside had been moved to newer systems years earlier. What remained was kept for retention requirements, old customer accounts and hardware nobody had been willing to discard.

The door was normally locked.

Henry looked at the custodian.

“Do you have the key?”

“Yes.”

They walked down the hallway together.

The new bulb cast a clean white circle across the floor behind them.

At the end of the corridor, Henry reached for the archive-room handle before the custodian could unlock it.

The handle turned.

The door opened.

Neither of them spoke.

The room was unlocked.

Henry pushed the door wider.

The archive and backup room looked almost exactly as it always did.

Metal shelving lined the walls.

Boxes of historical records sat beneath handwritten labels.

Old backup equipment occupied a rack near the rear of the room, powered down and gathering dust between the vents.

Nothing appeared disturbed.

A desk stood against the far wall.

Its lamp was on.

An old laptop sat beneath the light with the screen closed.

Beside it lay a Leatherman.

Henry stopped.

He knew it immediately.

Michael normally carried it in his pocket at work.

A narrow braided band was tied through the small lanyard ring, faded slightly from use.

Emma had made it for him.

There was no mistaking whose it was.

The custodian looked around the room.

“Michael?”

Silence.

Henry stepped closer to the desk.

He left the Leatherman where it was and lifted the laptop screen.

Black.

He pressed the space bar once.

Nothing happened.

No status light.

No fan.

No response.

The laptop was completely powered off.

Henry looked behind the desk.

Then beneath it.

The custodian checked between the shelving units and along the back wall.

Michael was not there.

The desk lamp continued to burn.

His Leatherman remained beside the dead laptop.

And the room offered no explanation for either of them.

Henry left the laptop open.

He did not touch the Leatherman.

“Stay here for a moment,” he told the custodian.

Henry stepped back into the hallway and walked toward the stairs.

On the way through the technical office, he passed Michael’s workstation again.

The terminal still waited on the screen.

The drawer remained open.

Michael’s phone lay inside.

Through the window beyond the desk, his car was still visible in the employee parking lot.

Same space.

Same position.

Henry continued upstairs.

The sound reached him before he entered Frank’s office.

Paper moving through a shredder.

Frank stood beside the machine, feeding several sheets into the slot.

A large portion of the stack that had covered his desk earlier was gone.

Henry stopped in the doorway.

“We found something.”

Frank pressed the pause button.

The shredder stopped with several inches of paper still visible above the blades.

He did not pull them out.

“What?”

“The old archive room was unlocked.”

Frank waited.

“The desk lamp was on. There’s an old laptop on the desk. It’s completely powered off.”

“And Michael?”

“Not there.”

Henry looked at the sheets held inside the shredder.

“His Leatherman is beside the laptop.”

Frank’s expression did not change.

“The one he always carries?”

“Yes.”

Frank gave a small nod.

“Mhm. One moment.”

He pressed the button again.

The paper disappeared into the machine.

Then Frank picked up the remaining sheets from his desk and fed them through as well.

Henry stood without speaking until the final page was gone.

The shredder fell silent.

Frank turned toward him.

“Now start again.”

He gestured toward the chair opposite his desk.

“From the beginning. Exactly.”

Henry sat down.

Frank remained standing beside the shredder.

“Emma was supposed to be picked up at three-oh-five,” Henry began. “Michael never arrived.”

Frank said nothing.

“Sarah called his direct line. Then she called reception. Brenda brought me in.”

“And Michael’s desk?”

“Empty. His workstation was still running.”

“What was open?”

“A terminal. Nothing else.”

“No email?”

“No.”

“Ticket system?”

“No.”

“Files?”

“Nothing.”

Frank folded his arms.

Henry continued.

“His last login was at one forty-one. I didn’t touch the keyboard.”

“Good.”

“I called his phone. It was in the open desk drawer.”

“Anything else in the drawer?”

Henry hesitated for only a moment.

“A small tin. Empty. And a USB drive.”

Frank looked at him.

“What kind of drive?”

“I don’t know. It looked new.”

“Was it connected to the workstation?”

“No. It was in the drawer.”

Frank gave a slight nod.

“Continue.”

“Michael’s car is still outside. I checked the server room, the restrooms, the offices, the workshop, the break room and the exterior.”

“And found nothing.”

“Nothing.”

Henry shifted slightly in the chair.

“Brenda saw him go to his car around one-thirty. He came back carrying something small.”

“She saw what it was?”

“No.”

“His condition?”

“Normal. Not hurried. Not upset.”

Frank waited.

“Steve saw him return to his desk,” Henry said. “Michael connected something to the workstation.”

“The USB drive?”

“Steve couldn’t identify it.”

“What did Michael say?”

“‘Just checking something quickly.’”

“And then?”

“Steve started talking about his prototype.”

Frank’s expression remained unreadable.

Henry continued.

“The custodian saw Michael downstairs around one forty-seven. Michael was walking toward the old archive and backup room.”

“Did the custodian see him leave?”

“No.”

“The room?”

“Unlocked.”

Frank’s eyes narrowed slightly.

“The lamp was on. The laptop was powered off. Michael’s Leatherman was beside it.”

“And Michael?”

Henry looked directly at him.

“Gone.”

The office remained quiet.

Below them, the normal sounds of Chesapeake Signal Tech continued through the building.

A telephone rang.

Someone crossed the hallway.

A printer began feeding paper.

Frank looked toward the empty space on his desk where the documents had been.

Then back at Henry.

“Go to Michael’s workstation.”

Henry stood.

“I’ll meet you there in ten or fifteen minutes.”

“What are you going to do?”

Frank began gathering the remaining folders into a single stack.

“Make sure nothing else gets overlooked.”

Henry waited for more.

None came.

He left the office and closed the door behind him.

Frank closed the office door after Henry left.

The latch settled quietly into place.

For several seconds, he remained beside the desk.

Then he gathered the folders and papers still lying across its surface.

One stack.

Then another.

He straightened the edges and cleared them away until the desk was almost empty.

The shredder stood silent beside him.

No telephone call followed.

No voice carried through the closed door.

Downstairs, Henry returned to Michael’s workstation.

The terminal was unchanged.

Michael’s phone remained in the open drawer.

His car remained visible through the window.

Henry checked the time.

Then he waited.

Ten minutes passed.

Then twelve.

The office continued around him, quieter now than it had been before.

The people around him knew Michael still had not been found.

They simply did not yet know what that meant.

After nearly fifteen minutes, Frank appeared at the entrance to the technical office.

He walked directly toward Michael’s desk.

Frank stopped beside Michael’s chair.

For several seconds, he said nothing.

His eyes moved across the empty desk.

The open drawer.

The phone inside it.

The terminal waiting on the monitor.

Frank leaned slightly closer to the screen.

“You haven’t touched it?”

“No.”

“Good.”

He looked toward the window.

Michael’s car remained in the same space.

Frank straightened.

“Show me the server room.”

Henry led him through the hallway.

The server-room door opened onto the same steady hum Henry had heard earlier.

Fans.

Cooling systems.

Status lights.

Frank checked the narrow spaces between the racks.

Henry opened the storage cabinet at the rear of the room.

Nothing.

No Michael.

They returned to the hallway.

Frank looked toward the staircase leading down to the archive room.

“Bring the laptop to my office.”

Henry hesitated.

“And the Leatherman?”

“That too.”

Frank glanced toward the employees watching from their desks.

“Carefully.”

The custodian carried the powered-off laptop upstairs.

Henry followed with Michael’s Leatherman.

The braided band Emma had tied to it rested against his palm.

Frank cleared a space on the cabinet beside his desk.

The laptop was placed there.

The Leatherman beside it.

Neither object was bagged.

Nothing was labeled.

There was no evidence log.

Not yet.

Frank looked at Henry.

“Until we know what happened, nobody touches either of them.”

Henry nodded.

“What about the workstation?”

“Leave it exactly as it is.”

“And Sarah?”

Frank looked toward the closed office door.

“Call her.”

Henry did not move immediately.

“What do I tell her?”

Frank’s answer did not come immediately.

“The truth.”

Henry looked back toward the hallway.

They had searched Michael’s desk.

The server room.

The offices.

The workshop.

The break room.

The exterior.

They had followed the final confirmed path to the old archive and backup room.

His phone was still inside the building.

His car was still outside.

His car keys were nowhere they had looked.

His Leatherman was upstairs in Frank’s office.

Michael was nowhere.

Henry reached for his phone.

For the first time, there was no other room left to check.

Michael Weber was missing.

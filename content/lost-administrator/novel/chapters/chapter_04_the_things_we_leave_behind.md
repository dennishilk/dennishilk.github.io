# Chapter 04 — The Things We Leave Behind

*Saturday, August 1, 2026*

**07:18 AM**

Emma opened her eyes.

For one quiet moment, she did not know where she was.

The ceiling was not hers.

Neither was the room.

Chloe slept beside her, one arm hanging over the edge of the bed. Morning light pressed through the curtains. Somewhere outside, birds called from the trees as though this were an ordinary Saturday.

Then Emma saw Byte’s small travel cage beside Chloe’s desk.

She remembered.

Dad still hadn’t called.

The thought settled against her chest with all the weight it had carried the night before.

Byte scratched softly beneath the bedding.

Emma turned toward him.

“Morning, Byte.”

He continued digging as though nothing in the world had changed.

Emma wished she could do the same.

She reached for her phone on the nightstand.

No missed calls.

No messages.

The lock screen showed only the time.

07:18 AM.

She stared at it, waiting for something to appear.

Nothing did.

For several seconds she kept the phone in her hand anyway, as though holding it made the distance between her and Michael smaller.

Then she heard voices downstairs.

Quiet.

Careful.

The house was already awake.

Emma slipped out of bed without waking Chloe.

---

The smell of coffee reached her before she reached the bottom of the stairs.

Laura stood near the kitchen counter. Daniel sat at the table with a mug between his hands.

Sarah sat beside him.

She was still wearing the clothes she had worn the day before. Her hair had been pulled into a quick ponytail. She looked as though she had slept even less than Emma.

A cup of coffee stood in front of her, almost untouched.

Her phone lay beside it.

She looked at the screen.

Not because it had made a sound.

Because she had looked at it every few seconds since waking.

Then she noticed Emma.

A small smile appeared.

“Good morning, sweetheart.”

Emma stopped on the last stair.

Her eyes moved immediately to Sarah’s phone.

“Dad called?”

The kitchen became still.

Sarah shook her head.

“No, sweetheart.”

Emma had known the answer before asking.

She still needed to hear it.

She crossed the room and sat beside her mother.

Not across from her.

Beside her.

Their shoulders touched.

For a few seconds, neither of them spoke.

---

Laura placed a plate on the table.

“I made toast.”

Emma looked at it.

“I’m not hungry.”

“You don’t have to finish it.”

Laura set down only one slice.

“And I can make hot chocolate.”

Yesterday, Emma would have said yes immediately.

Today she only nodded.

Laura turned toward the stove.

The local radio played quietly from the counter.

Weather.

Traffic.

A baseball score from the night before.

Everything sounded impossibly normal.

Laura reached over and lowered the volume.

No one commented.

Sarah nudged the plate a little closer to Emma.

“You should try.”

Emma broke a small piece from the edge of the toast.

It took her a long time to eat it.

Nobody praised her.

Nobody asked her to take another bite.

They had all noticed.

That was enough.

---

Laura poured hot chocolate into two mugs.

She placed one in front of Emma and held out the other.

“Would you take this upstairs for Chloe?”

Emma wrapped both hands around the warm mug.

“Okay.”

The small task felt strangely useful.

She carried the second mug upstairs.

---

Chloe had begun to move beneath the blanket.

Emma placed the mug on the nightstand.

“Your mom made hot chocolate.”

Chloe opened her eyes.

“Morning.”

“Morning.”

For a second, Chloe looked as though she had forgotten too.

Then she saw Emma’s face.

“Oh.”

She sat up.

“I’m sorry.”

Emma shook her head.

“You don’t have to keep saying that.”

Chloe nodded.

She moved a little closer to the wall, making room beside her.

Emma sat down.

Neither girl spoke.

They drank their hot chocolate and listened to Byte moving through the bedding.

After a while Chloe looked at Emma’s phone.

“Nothing?”

Emma shook her head.

“No.”

Chloe did not tell her Michael would call soon.

She only stayed beside her.

---

The doorbell rang downstairs.

Daniel glanced toward the front window.

“That’ll be Leo.”

He opened the door.

A father from the neighborhood stood outside with Leo and a small overnight bag.

“Morning.”

“Morning. Thanks again for having him.”

“Of course.”

Leo waved toward the car.

“Bye!”

The other father returned the wave and drove away.

Leo stepped into the house.

“Morning!”

He stopped.

Sarah was there.

Emma was there.

Nobody smiled the way they normally did.

Laura crossed to him and crouched beside him.

“Come here for a second.”

Leo looked from her to Emma.

“What’s wrong?”

Laura kept her voice gentle.

“Emma’s dad didn’t come home yesterday.”

“The police are looking for him.”

Leo frowned.

“Is he lost?”

Laura paused.

“We don’t know yet.”

Leo considered that answer.

Then he walked over to Emma.

“Hi.”

“Hi.”

His eyes moved to Byte’s travel cage.

“Did Byte sleep here too?”

Emma nodded.

“He did.”

Leo crouched beside the cage.

“Did he get breakfast?”

“Not yet.”

“We should feed him.”

Emma opened the little container while Leo held it steady.

Byte appeared almost immediately.

Leo watched him collect the first piece of food.

“He doesn’t know anything happened.”

“No,” Emma said.

Leo thought about that.

“That’s probably nice.”

For the first time that morning, Emma smiled.

Only a little.

But it was real.

---

While the children remained near Byte’s cage, Daniel looked toward the bright morning beyond the kitchen windows.

“The girls shouldn’t spend the whole day waiting.”

Sarah looked at him.

Daniel continued carefully.

“What if we take the kids to the beach for a few hours?”

“Nothing big. We can walk, collect shells, swim if they feel like it.”

“And come straight back if they don’t,” Laura added.

Sarah looked toward Emma.

“I don’t want her to think we’re trying to make her forget.”

“We’re not,” Laura said.

“We’re just letting her breathe.”

Sarah lowered her eyes to the coffee she still had not finished.

After a moment, she nodded.

“If she wants to go.”

Daniel turned toward the children.

“What do you think?”

Leo raised his hand immediately.

“Beach.”

Chloe looked at Emma.

“You don’t have to.”

Emma stared through the window at the clear August sky.

“Dad likes the ocean.”

Sarah looked at her.

Emma continued quietly.

“He always says the ocean never hurries.”

Michael had said it when she became impatient.

When something refused to work.

When they sat beside the water and she wanted the next wave to arrive faster.

Sarah knew the sentence.

“He does.”

Emma looked toward Chloe.

“I’ll go.”

Laura folded another towel and looked at Sarah.

“You can both stay here again tonight.”

Emma glanced at Chloe.

Then she nodded.

“I’d like that.”

Sarah gave Laura a grateful look.

“Thank you.”

---

Laura began collecting towels.

Daniel carried a cooler from the pantry.

Sarah stood and reached for her handbag.

“I need to stop at the police department first.”

Emma looked up.

“And after that?”

“I’ll ask them if I can go to Dad’s house.”

Sarah waited until Emma met her eyes.

“I’ll bring your clothes.”

“Your sketchbook.”

“Your colored pencils.”

Emma glanced toward Byte’s travel cage.

“His big cage too?”

“Of course.”

“And my blue hoodie.”

Sarah knew the one she meant.

“The one from Dad’s closet?”

Emma nodded.

“It still smells like him.”

Sarah swallowed.

“I’ll bring it.”

She started to turn away.

“Mom?”

Sarah stopped.

“Could you check on Grissom?”

“The aquarium?”

Emma nodded.

“Dad always feeds him in the morning.”

“I will.”

“And the filter?”

“I’ll check that too.”

Emma seemed a little less tense.

“Thank you.”

---

Sarah knelt and pulled her into a hug.

Emma held on longer than she normally would.

“I’ll be back later,” Sarah said.

“Keep your phone with you.”

“I will.”

“If Dad calls—”

“I’ll answer on the first ring.”

Sarah managed a small smile.

“I know you will.”

She stood.

Daniel lifted his own keys from the counter.

“We’ll keep our phones with us. If anything changes, we turn around immediately.”

Sarah nodded.

“Thank you.”

Laura touched her arm.

“You don’t have to thank us.”

“We’re family.”

Sarah looked at her for a moment.

Then she nodded.

---

Warm air entered when the front door opened.

Outside, someone was mowing a lawn.

A dog barked farther down the street.

The neighborhood had begun another ordinary Saturday.

Sarah watched Emma climb into the Vances’ SUV beside Chloe and Leo.

She waited until Daniel had closed the rear door.

Then she got into her own car.

The radio remained off.

She connected her phone to the hands-free system before leaving the driveway.

Not because she wanted to make a call.

Because she could not risk missing one.

---

The drive to the Port St. Lucie Police Department felt strangely familiar.

Traffic moved as it always did on a Saturday morning.

People stopped for coffee.

Families loaded groceries into SUVs.

Someone walked a golden retriever across a parking lot.

The world had continued.

As though yesterday had happened to someone else.

Sarah hated it.

---

She turned into the visitor lot and stopped in front of a plain blue-and-white sign.

**VISITOR PARKING**

For one absurd second, the certainty of the sign felt like a relief.

At least she knew where to put the car.

She switched off the engine.

Both hands remained on the steering wheel.

She looked toward the entrance.

Yesterday, Michael had been late.

Then unreachable.

Then missing.

Today she had come to a police station because her husband had vanished.

Sarah exhaled slowly.

Then opened the door.

---

The automatic glass doors admitted her into cold, conditioned air.

Phones rang somewhere beyond the reception desk.

A printer worked behind a partition.

People waited in molded chairs with folders, envelopes and tired expressions.

For everyone else, the building was simply doing what it did every day.

Sarah approached the desk.

“Good morning. How can I help you?”

“My name is Sarah Weber.”

She hesitated.

“I’m here about Michael Weber.”

The receptionist’s expression changed without becoming dramatic.

“One moment, Mrs. Weber.”

She checked the screen and picked up the telephone.

“Officer Daniels is expecting you.”

Sarah did not sit.

A door opened a short time later.

Daniels stepped into the waiting area.

She recognized him first by his voice.

“Mrs. Weber.”

“Officer Daniels.”

He came forward and offered his hand.

“I’m sorry we’re meeting like this.”

“So am I.”

He held the door open.

“Come with me.”

---

They walked along a quiet corridor.

At the end, Daniels opened a small meeting room.

A plain table.

Four chairs.

A notepad.

A bottle of water.

Nothing about it looked important.

Sarah stopped at the threshold anyway.

It felt like being called from a waiting room.

The moment before something became official.

Daniels did not hurry her.

“After you.”

Sarah took one breath and entered.

---

Daniels waited until she sat before taking the chair across from her.

“Before we begin, how are you holding up?”

Sarah gave a brief, exhausted smile.

“I don’t know.”

He nodded.

The answer was enough.

“I know we spoke several times yesterday. I want to go over where things stand and answer what I can.”

“Please don’t guess.”

“I won’t.”

Daniels opened his notebook.

“We completed another review of the searches and statements overnight.”

“The last confirmed sequence remains the same.”

“Michael returned from the parking lot. He used his workstation. A few minutes later, he was seen walking toward the archive room.”

“No confirmed sighting after that.”

Sarah looked down at her hands.

“So nothing changed.”

“No.”

---

Daniels continued carefully.

“We have no evidence that Michael has been harmed.”

Sarah looked up.

He did not soften the next sentence.

“And we have no evidence that he’s safe.”

The room remained quiet.

“So we’re working with facts.”

Sarah nodded.

“He’s officially missing?”

“Yes.”

The single word settled between them.

---

Daniels explained what had continued through the night.

Patrol units had Michael’s photograph and description.

Hospitals had been checked.

Nearby roads and businesses were being covered.

Available surveillance recordings were being preserved.

Chesapeake Signal Tech remained closed. Michael’s workstation and the old archive room were secured while the evidence inside was documented and reviewed.

The officers had not stopped because Friday had ended.

“What happens now?” Sarah asked.

“We continue.”

“You don’t stop because it’s Saturday.”

“No.”

“Missing-person cases don’t wait until Monday.”

Sarah breathed out slowly.

It helped to hear him say it.

---

“Should I be doing something?”

“Yes.”

She straightened.

“Take care of Emma.”

Sarah looked surprised.

Daniels continued.

“We’ll handle the search. She needs someone she trusts to keep the rest of the world from falling apart.”

Sarah glanced toward the silent phone beside her hand.

“That doesn’t feel like enough.”

“It is.”

---

She told him she intended to stop at Michael’s house.

“Emma needs clothes. Her drawing things. A larger cage for her hamster.”

Daniels made a note.

“And I promised her I’d feed Grissom.”

“Grissom?”

“The fish.”

Daniels nodded once.

“The initial walk-through is complete. The house was not secured as a scene.”

“You may go inside.”

“If you notice anything unusual, don’t move it. Step outside and call us.”

“I understand.”

---

When the conversation ended, Daniels walked her back toward reception.

Before they reached the door, he stopped.

“One more thing.”

He removed a plain business card and handed it to her.

His name.

Port St. Lucie Police Department.

A direct extension and department email address.

“If you remember anything, no matter how small, call that number.”

“A sentence. A place. Something he mentioned once and you dismissed at the time.”

“People rarely remember everything while sitting in a room like this.”

Sarah looked at the card.

“When do they remember?”

“Driving. Making coffee. Folding laundry.”

He gave a faint, tired smile.

“Memory doesn’t work on our schedule.”

Sarah placed the card inside her wallet.

“Thank you.”

“If anything changes, we’ll call you immediately.”

She nodded.

“I hope it’s soon.”

“So do I.”

---

Sarah stepped back into the Florida heat.

This time, she knew where she was going next.

Michael’s house.

---

The drive to Copperleaf Loop took less than twenty minutes.

Sarah had made it countless times before.

Some weekends to pick Emma up.

Some to drop her off.

The route had become part of the rhythm of their shared life with Emma.

Today was different.

She wasn’t coming to visit.

She was coming because Michael wasn’t there.

---

The neighborhood looked exactly as it always had.

Freshly cut lawns.

Palm trees swaying gently in the humid morning breeze.

A man washing his pickup truck two houses down.

Someone walking a dog.

Nobody looked twice at Sarah’s car.

Nobody knew.

---

She turned into the driveway.

The garage door remained closed.

The front porch looked exactly as Michael always kept it.

Clean.

Simple.

Ordinary.

Sarah switched off the engine.

For a moment she simply looked at the house.

Yesterday morning Michael had walked out through that front door.

Coffee finished.

Toast eaten.

Aquarium checked.

Emma’s pickup already somewhere in the back of his mind.

He had expected to come home again.

So had everyone else.

---

She picked up her purse and stepped out of the car.

The officers had returned the spare key to its usual place.

Sarah crossed to the garage keypad.

One-zero-one-three.

The door began to rise.

She kept her attention on the workbench.

Not the boxes.

Not Emma’s bicycle.

Not yet.

The black magnetic key box remained beneath the rear leg, exactly where she had told Daniels it would be.

Sarah removed the key, closed the garage again and walked around to the front door.

Every step felt familiar.

And completely wrong.

---

The key slid into the lock without resistance.

She turned it once.

The deadbolt clicked.

Sarah rested her hand on the doorknob.

For a second...

...she couldn’t move.

Then she opened the door.

---

The house welcomed her with silence.

Not an uncomfortable silence.

Just...

...an empty one.

She stepped inside.

Almost automatically she spoke.

“Michael?”

The word echoed softly through the hallway.

No answer came.

Of course none did.

Even so...

...she had called his name.

Because that was what she had always done when she entered the house and thought he might be home.

---

She closed the door behind her.

Almost immediately her eyes wandered toward the small wooden bowl beside the entrance.

A few coins.

A shopping-cart token.

Nothing else.

Sarah stopped.

Every evening...

...Michael came home, reached into his left pocket and placed his Leatherman there before doing anything else.

It had become such a normal part of the house that she had stopped noticing it years ago.

Now she noticed only its absence.

She didn’t have to wonder where it was.

She already knew.

Michael had never brought it home.

---

She let her fingertips rest briefly against the edge of the bowl.

Then quietly whispered,

“You really never made it home.”

---

She took a slow breath.

There were things Emma had asked her to do.

She focused on those.

One thing at a time.

---

She walked toward the living room.

The aquarium hummed softly.

The familiar sound filled the otherwise silent house.

Grissom appeared from behind one of the plants almost immediately.

He swam toward the front glass.

Waiting.

Sarah smiled sadly.

“Hey, buddy.”

Grissom circled once.

Then waited again.

Probably expecting breakfast.

Probably expecting Michael.

Sarah picked up the small food container from the shelf beside the aquarium.

Exactly where Michael always kept it.

She sprinkled a little food across the surface.

Grissom darted upward immediately.

Tiny ripples spread across the water.

Sarah rested one hand on the cabinet beneath the aquarium.

“Emma was worried about you.”

She watched the little fish happily chasing breakfast.

“I told her I’d check on you.”

The filter continued its quiet, steady hum.

The water remained crystal clear.

Everything worked exactly as Michael had left it.

She smiled.

“You checked everything before you left...”

“...didn’t you?”

Of course he had.

Michael always did.

---

She reached into her purse.

Took out her phone.

Raised it toward the aquarium.

*Click.*

Just one picture.

Nothing artistic.

Nothing carefully framed.

Just Grissom swimming peacefully through the plants.

Sarah looked at the photograph for a second.

Then slipped the phone back into her purse.

Not because Emma had asked for proof.

But because she knew her daughter.

Sooner or later Emma would ask,

“How’s Grissom?”

Now Sarah wouldn’t have to tell her.

She could simply show her.

---

She stood there another few moments.

Listening to the soft bubbling of the filter.

For the first time since yesterday...

...something inside the house still sounded alive.

---

Before going upstairs, Sarah crossed into the kitchen.

The counter was clean.

The coffee machine was off.

Nothing had been left waiting in the sink.

Michael had expected to return that evening, but he had still left the room in order.

Sarah lowered the dishwasher door.

Inside stood the plate from his breakfast and the mug he had used that morning.

**Everything is fine.**

The little cartoon dog sat smiling in a room surrounded by flames.

Sarah stared at it.

“Your sense of humor was always terrible.”

A small laugh escaped before she could stop it.

Then the sound disappeared into the empty kitchen.

She closed the dishwasher without removing anything.

---

There were still promises to keep.

Sarah quietly climbed the stairs.

---

The second floor was exactly as quiet as the first.

Sunlight slipped through the hallway window, painting narrow rectangles across the wooden floor.

Sarah climbed the last step slowly.

She already knew what Emma had asked her to bring.

Still...

...her feet carried her somewhere else first.

---

She stopped in front of Emma’s bedroom.

The door stood half open.

Sarah gently pushed it farther.

The room looked as though Emma had only stepped out for a few minutes.

The bed was neatly made.

A sketchbook rested on the desk.

Colored pencils lay beside an unfinished drawing.

Sarah smiled.

Emma had inherited that from Michael.

One project was never quite finished before the next one began.

---

She opened the wardrobe and packed several T-shirts.

Jeans.

Underwear.

Pajamas.

Enough for another night with the Vances.

Then she placed the sketchbook and the plastic case of colored pencils on top.

Near the wall stood Byte’s larger cage.

His wheel.

Water bottle.

The little shelter he preferred to sleep beneath.

Sarah detached the pieces that needed to be carried separately and set everything beside the bedroom door.

Emma had asked for ordinary things.

Clothes.

Pencils.

A home for her hamster.

Sarah could give her those.

---

She looked around the room one last time.

Everything waited for Emma to return.

She lifted the travel bag and carried it into the hallway.

Only one thing remained.

The blue hoodie.

She stopped halfway down the hallway.

At the opposite end stood Michael’s office.

The door remained slightly open.

Just as it almost always had.

Michael never liked closing it completely.

Years ago Sarah had once asked why.

He had smiled.

“If the door’s open...”

“...Emma knows she can always come in.”

“Even while you’re working?”

“Especially then.”

Sarah smiled faintly at the memory.

The same door still stood open.

But beyond it...

...there was no keyboard.

No quiet typing.

No music.

No voice saying,

“Come in.”

She rested two fingers lightly against the door.

Took a slow breath.

Then pushed it open.

---

The room looked exactly as Sarah remembered.

Not spotless.

Not messy.

Simply...

...lived in.

A place where things were constantly being repaired, tested or improved.

The faint smell of electronics lingered in the air.

Coffee.

Paper.

Warm computer hardware.

It smelled unmistakably like Michael.

---

Her eyes wandered across the room.

Plastic storage boxes lined one shelf.

Each carried a handwritten label.

**Network Cables**

**Power Supplies**

**Adapters**

**Serial**

Every box looked organized.

Every box probably made perfect sense to Michael.

Sarah smiled.

He had never thrown anything away if he thought there was even the slightest chance it might be useful someday.

“Just in case.”

He had said those words more times than she could count.

---

Her attention shifted to the workbench beneath the window.

There, resting on an antistatic mat, lay a familiar laptop.

Emma’s ThinkPad T480.

The bottom cover had already been removed.

Tiny screws rested neatly inside a magnetic parts tray.

Beside them...

...a brand-new keyboard still sealed inside an antistatic bag.

Sarah immediately remembered Emma talking excitedly about it only a few days earlier.

“Dad found a backlit keyboard for it.”

“He says I won’t need the desk lamp just to see the keys anymore.”

Michael had already started the upgrade.

He had simply...

...never finished it.

Sarah carefully looked over the workbench.

She touched nothing.

Everything remained exactly where Michael had left it.

As though he had only stepped away for a moment.

---

A bookshelf stood against the opposite wall.

Technical manuals.

Networking books.

Linux.

Debian.

Some looked almost new.

Others had worn corners and broken spines from years of use.

One bright box caught Sarah’s attention.

**Windows 98 Second Edition.**

She blinked.

Walked a little closer.

Picked up the box.

“...Why on earth...”

“...do you still have this?”

She smiled despite herself.

She didn’t actually expect an answer.

She already knew one would have existed.

Michael always had a reason.

She returned the box to its place.

---

Something else caught her eye.

A tiny black USB device with a short antenna.

She laughed quietly.

“The radio thing.”

Years ago Michael had spent nearly an entire weekend enthusiastically explaining everything it could receive.

Weather stations.

Aircraft.

Satellites.

Amateur radio.

Signals she hadn’t even known existed.

After about ten minutes...

...she had stopped understanding.

Michael hadn’t.

He had happily continued for another hour.

The little receiver still rested exactly where he had left it.

---

She slowly looked around the room again.

Old hard drives.

Network switches.

Cables disappearing behind furniture.

Half-finished notes.

Little projects everywhere.

Sarah probably understood half of what she was looking at.

Maybe less.

But she recognized every single object.

Not because she knew what it did.

Because it belonged to Michael.

Michael always had little projects.

And over the years Sarah had learned one simple truth.

If it had a cable...

...he probably knew exactly what it did.

---

For a moment, Sarah remained in the office.

Every unfinished project seemed to assume Michael would return and complete it.

She moved nothing else.

Then she stepped back into the hallway and left the door exactly as she had found it.

---

The travel bag waited beside the doorway.

Sarah picked it up and crossed to Michael’s bedroom.

The blue hoodie was still in his wardrobe.

The door slid open with the same soft sound it always had.

Everything inside was neatly arranged.

Shirts.

Polos.

Jeans.

Jackets.

Michael had never been obsessed with order.

But he always knew where everything belonged.

Sarah smiled faintly.

“Some things never change.”

Her eyes found the blue hoodie immediately.

Emma had been right.

Toward the back.

Exactly where Michael always hung it.

Sarah lifted it from the hanger.

For a long moment...

...she simply held it.

Almost without thinking, she brought it closer.

It still smelled like him.

Not aftershave.

Not cologne.

Just...

...Michael.

She closed her eyes.

Only for a second.

Then she folded the hoodie carefully.

Not perfectly.

Just enough that Emma could unfold it again without losing the familiar scent.

She placed it on top of the travel bag.

Not underneath the clothes.

Not squeezed between the jeans.

Right on top.

The first thing Emma would see.

Sarah closed the wardrobe and left the room without changing anything else.

---

She carried the travel bag downstairs.

Then she returned for Byte’s larger cage and set it beside the front door.

Before stepping outside, she locked the front door with a single turn.

Almost ready to leave.

Then something occurred to her.

The garage.

Emma’s bicycle.

Without really thinking about it, Sarah stepped outside again.

She crossed the driveway toward the keypad.

She entered the same four digits again.

A soft beep.

The garage door slowly began to rise.

---

Nothing appeared unusual.

On one side stood Michael’s neatly stacked boxes.

Computer parts.

Cables.

Old hardware.

Things he always insisted might become useful again.

On the other stood Emma’s bicycle.

The frame still carried the shallow dent from the afternoon Michael had backed over it.

Sarah smiled immediately.

She remembered the phone call.

Michael had sounded less alarmed than embarrassed.

“Don’t laugh.”

Sarah had laughed before he could continue.

“What did you do?”

A long sigh.

“Emma left her bike in the driveway.”

“I backed over it.”

Sarah laughed harder.

“Seriously?”

“I didn’t see it.”

“It was just there.”

She could picture the expression on his face.

That mixture of annoyance and reluctant amusement he wore whenever something ridiculous happened to him.

“I knew calling you first was a mistake.”

“Oh, I’m definitely telling Emma.”

“I was hoping you wouldn’t.”

“You knew that was never going to happen.”

Emma had told the story differently from that day forward.

“Dad crashed into my bike.”

Michael had defended himself every time.

“Your bike attacked my car.”

Sarah rested her hand on the handlebar.

“She never let you forget it.”

Her eyes moved to the boxes.

Michael had talked for months about clearing enough space to park inside the garage again.

Next weekend.

Always next weekend.

“You’ve been saying that for months.”

She shook her head with a small smile.

Then she lifted Byte’s cage into the car.

Before leaving, she returned the house key to the black magnetic box beneath the workbench.

She pressed the wall control and waited until the garage door had closed completely.

---

Sarah stood for another moment in the driveway.

The garage door settled quietly into place behind her.

Everything was locked.

Everything that belonged to Michael was still waiting where he had left it.

Except Michael.

She picked up the travel bag once more.

Opened the car door.

Placed it carefully on the passenger seat.

The blue hoodie remained on top.

Exactly where Emma would see it first.

Sarah looked at the house one final time.

Then started the engine.

---

She didn’t immediately turn toward the Vances’ neighborhood.

At the next intersection she hesitated.

Her right hand rested on the turn signal.

Instead...

...she drove straight ahead.

---

A few minutes later the familiar building appeared between the palm trees.

**Chesapeake Signal Tech.**

The parking lot entrance stood closed.

The reception windows reflected the bright morning sun.

Weekend silence surrounded the entire property.

Sarah slowed almost without realizing it.

Then she saw it.

Michael’s car.

Still standing exactly where Henry had said it was.

Unmoved.

Waiting.

She took her foot off the accelerator.

For a long moment she simply looked through the fence.

The car appeared completely ordinary.

As though Michael might walk through the front door at any second.

Unlock it.

Start the engine.

Drive home.

But the front entrance remained closed.

No one came outside.

Sarah knew she couldn’t simply enter the property.

Yesterday it had become a police scene.

She stayed in her car.

---

She continued driving.

Around the block.

Past the loading dock.

Through the small industrial streets behind the building.

There was no real destination.

No real reason.

She simply couldn’t bring herself to leave.

Every now and then the company building appeared again between warehouses and parked delivery trucks.

Each time...

...she searched for something.

Someone.

Anything.

She didn’t even know what.

---

Eventually she stopped at a quiet intersection.

Both hands rested on the steering wheel.

She looked back toward the building one last time.

“Come on, Michael...”

“...where did you go?”

Only silence answered.

---

Her phone vibrated.

The sudden sound made her jump.

She grabbed it almost instantly.

A WhatsApp notification.

**Laura**

> **We’re on our way home with the kids.**
>
> **No rush. Drive safely. ❤️**

A small smile returned.

Laura hadn’t asked whether there was news.

She already knew Sarah would have called if there had been.

Sarah quickly typed a reply.

> **Thank you.**
>
> **I’ll be there in about twenty minutes.**
>
> **I got everything Emma asked for.**

She pressed Send.

Placed the phone back on the passenger seat beside the travel bag.

Then looked once more toward Chesapeake Signal Tech.

Toward Michael’s car.

Still waiting.

Just like everyone else.

Sarah started the engine.

This time...

...she turned toward Sandhill Heron Way.

---

Daniel was unloading damp towels from the back of the SUV when Sarah turned into the driveway.

Chloe carried a small plastic bucket filled with shells.

Leo held one large shell to his ear and insisted he could hear the ocean even from the front lawn.

Emma noticed Sarah’s car first.

She was already moving before Sarah had fully stopped.

“You went?”

Sarah nodded.

“I did.”

Emma did not look at the travel bag.

She did not look at the larger cage on the back seat.

“Did you check on Grissom?”

Sarah smiled.

“I promised you I would.”

She opened the photograph on her phone and handed it to Emma.

---

Emma studied the picture.

Grissom swam peacefully between the plants.

The water was clear.

The filter was running.

Everything looked exactly as it should.

A tiny smile appeared.

“He looks okay.”

“He is.”

“You fed him?”

“He seemed very happy about it.”

Emma looked at the picture once more before returning the phone.

“Thanks.”

One simple word.

Sarah knew how much it meant.

---

Only then did Emma notice the travel bag.

“My hoodie?”

“It’s on top.”

Emma opened the bag.

The blue hoodie was exactly where Sarah had placed it.

She lifted it out carefully and pressed it against herself.

For several seconds she said nothing.

Then she looked up.

“You remembered.”

“I promised.”

Daniel carried Byte’s larger cage toward the house.

Leo followed beside him.

“He’s going to like this better.”

Emma nodded.

“He will.”

Laura picked up the travel bag.

“I’ll take the rest upstairs.”

Sarah brushed a loose strand of hair behind Emma’s ear.

The hoodie remained held tightly between them.

---

The afternoon slowly settled into something almost normal.

Almost.

Emma and Leo set up Byte’s larger cage in Chloe’s room.

Fresh bedding.

His wheel.

The little shelter from home.

When they opened the travel cage, Byte moved into the larger space without hesitation and immediately began rearranging everything they had just placed inside.

Leo watched through the bars.

“He doesn’t like where we put it.”

“He never does,” Emma said.

For a moment, caring for Byte was the only problem in the room.

---

Soon afterward, the children went outside.

The swimming pool shimmered beneath the Florida sun.

Laura carried a tray onto the patio.

Six tall glasses.

One for each of them.

Fresh lemonade.

Slices of lemon floated beside sprigs of mint from the garden.

---

Leo jumped into the pool first.

A huge splash soaked half the patio.

“Dad!”

Daniel laughed.

“I saw that coming.”

Chloe followed him into the water.

Emma sat quietly at the edge of the pool.

Her feet rested beneath the surface.

The blue hoodie remained neatly folded on a lounge chair beside her.

Not far away.

Close enough that she could see it.

---

Leo paddled toward her.

“Come on!”

Emma smiled faintly.

“In a minute.”

He accepted the answer and splashed away again.

Daniel tossed an inflatable volleyball into the pool.

Within minutes, Leo and Chloe were laughing as though nothing in the world could ever go wrong.

Emma watched them.

Then she stood.

She moved the hoodie a little farther from the water.

Only then did she dive in.

Not dramatically.

Not excitedly.

Just quietly.

Chloe smiled the moment Emma surfaced.

“I knew you’d come.”

Emma smiled back.

“So did I.”

---

Sarah watched from the patio.

For a few moments, Emma looked like an ordinary twelve-year-old girl again.

Not the daughter of a missing father.

Just Emma.

Swimming with her best friend.

Sarah silently thanked Chloe for that.

---

Laura sat beside her with a glass of lemonade.

For several minutes, neither woman spoke.

They watched the children.

Eventually Laura said,

“I’m glad she laughed.”

Sarah kept her eyes on the pool.

“So am I.”

---

Her phone rested on the table.

Face up.

Volume at maximum.

Every few minutes, her eyes moved toward it.

Nothing.

No missed call.

No message.

She turned it face down.

Forced herself not to look.

Thirty seconds later, she turned it over again.

Laura noticed.

She said nothing.

She understood.

---

The smell of grilled burgers slowly filled the backyard.

Leo stood beside the grill wearing an oversized apron that reached almost to his ankles.

Daniel carefully handed him a spatula.

“Ready?”

Leo nodded enthusiastically.

“I was born ready.”

Daniel laughed.

“We’ll see.”

A moment later Leo attempted to flip the first burger.

It landed slightly crooked.

He looked at Daniel.

“...I meant to do that.”

“I’m sure you did.”

---

The afternoon gave way to dinner without anyone really noticing.

The conversation remained light.

School.

Summer.

Movies.

Leo proudly described every detail of the sleepover he had just returned from.

Emma listened.

Sometimes she even smiled.

Not often.

But enough.

---

After they finished eating, Laura carried the plates inside.

Emma and Chloe disappeared upstairs together.

The Major Tom pages still lay spread across Chloe’s desk.

This time they didn’t print anything.

They simply drew.

Planets.

Little robots.

A rocket that somehow ended up wearing sunglasses.

Occasionally one of them laughed.

Occasionally neither of them spoke for several minutes.

Both seemed equally important.

---

Downstairs the television played quietly in the background.

Some Saturday evening game show.

Nobody really watched it.

It simply filled the silence.

Sometimes silence became louder than television.

---

Leo insisted he was not tired.

“I’m not tired.”

Five minutes later he had fallen asleep across one end of the couch.

One arm hanging toward the floor.

Still holding half a hamburger bun.

Daniel smiled.

“I think I lost this round.”

He gently lifted Leo into his arms.

The little boy didn’t even wake up.

---

Later that evening Sarah quietly climbed the stairs.

The hallway lights remained dim.

She gently pushed Chloe’s bedroom door open.

The girls were asleep.

Colored pencils still lay scattered across the desk.

Several finished drawings covered the surface.

Byte slept curled beneath fresh bedding inside his larger cage.

The blue hoodie rested beside her pillow.

One sleeve was tucked beneath her hand.

Almost as though she had fallen asleep holding it.

Sarah smiled sadly.

“Good night, sweetheart.”

She quietly pulled the blanket a little higher over Emma’s shoulder.

Then she gently closed the door again.

---

Back downstairs the television was still running.

Laura had already gone to bed.

Daniel switched the volume even lower.

Sarah stood for a moment beside the guest-room door.

Her phone rested in her hand.

She looked at the display one last time.

No missed calls.

No new messages.

She placed it carefully on the nightstand beside the bed.

Not on silent.

Never on silent.

Within arm’s reach.

Just in case.

She turned off the bedside lamp.

Darkness settled across the room.

Outside...

...Florida’s summer night continued exactly as every other summer night had before it.

Inside...

...everyone slept.

Or tried to.

Morning would come whether answers did or not.

For now...

...there was nothing left to do except wait.

# Chapter 05 — The Passenger

*Sunday, August 2, 2026*

**07:40 AM**

Sarah’s phone vibrated.

The sound cut through the silence of the room.

Her eyes opened immediately.

For one confused moment, she stared motionless into the dim guest room.

Then the phone vibrated again on the wooden nightstand.

Sarah reached for it so quickly that her hand struck the glass of water beside the bed.

The glass rocked.

Water spilled across the nightstand.

She did not notice.

The screen lit her face.

**Officer Andrew Daniels**

Sarah stopped breathing.

She answered before the phone could vibrate a third time.

“Hello?”

Her voice was rough with sleep.

Daniels spoke calmly.

“Mrs. Weber?”

“I’m here.”

Sarah pushed herself upright.

The blanket slid into her lap.

“What happened?”

A brief pause.

Not long.

Less than a second.

And still long enough for every possible answer to pass through her mind.

A hospital.

A roadside.

A body.

Michael standing somewhere without his phone, asking why everyone had been so worried.

“Officer Daniels?”

“We reviewed the recording from the neighboring business.”

Sarah gripped the phone more tightly.

The room seemed to shrink around her.

“And?”

“I’d like you to come to the station.”

“No.”

Daniels fell silent.

Sarah swung her legs out of bed.

“No. Don’t do that.”

“Mrs. Weber—”

“Don’t call me at seven forty on a Sunday morning and ask me to come somewhere without telling me why.”

Her feet touched the floor.

The spilled water had begun to drip from the nightstand.

She did not look at it.

“Did you find Michael?”

“No.”

The answer came immediately.

Clear.

Sarah closed her eyes.

Relief came first.

Sharp and immediate.

Then guilt followed.

Because *no* meant he was still missing.

“No,” Daniels repeated. “We haven’t found him.”

Sarah lowered her voice.

“Is he dead?”

“We have no evidence of that.”

“Then tell me what you found.”

Daniels exhaled quietly.

“We recovered usable footage from the camera overlooking the access road behind Chesapeake Signal Tech.”

Sarah stood.

For a moment, the room shifted.

She steadied herself against the nightstand.

“The camera from the business two properties away?”

“Yes.”

“You said it doesn’t show the building.”

“It doesn’t.”

“The parking lot?”

“No.”

“Then what does it show?”

“The shared access road.”

Sarah waited.

Daniels did not continue immediately.

Her own heartbeat filled the silence.

“What was on it?”

“At 2:03:17 Friday afternoon, the camera recorded a vehicle leaving the access road.”

Sarah frowned.

“Cars leave there all day.”

“Yes.”

“Then why are you calling me?”

“Because there’s someone in the passenger seat.”

Sarah said nothing.

The sentence reached her.

But for a moment, she did not understand it.

“Someone?”

“An adult male.”

Sarah’s fingers tightened around the phone.

“Is it Michael?”

Daniels did not answer immediately.

“Officer Daniels.”

“We can’t say with certainty.”

Sarah walked to the window and pulled the curtain aside.

Outside, the street lay still in the pale morning light.

No cars moved.

No one was there.

“How can you not know whether it’s Michael?”

“The camera is too far away. The image was compressed, and the windshield reflects part of the sky.”

“But you think it’s him.”

Again, that brief pause.

This time, Sarah understood what it meant.

“Yes,” Daniels said.

The word was barely louder than a breath.

Sarah did not sit down again.

She could not have.

“Why?”

“The person is approximately Michael’s height and build. The shape of his head and hair are consistent. He’s also wearing a dark blue top.”

Sarah saw Michael in her mind.

The Chesapeake polo.

Dark jeans.

Brown work shoes.

The clothes he had worn to work Friday morning.

The clothes Henry had described.

The clothes he wore in the parking-lot footage.

“The company shirt.”

“Possibly.”

“Is there a clear image of his face?”

“No.”

“But you can see what he’s wearing?”

“We can distinguish colors and general outlines. No reliable details.”

Sarah pressed her free hand against her forehead.

“And the vehicle?”

“A large white SUV.”

“What kind?”

“Our analysts believe it may be a GMC Yukon.”

“May be?”

“The grille, headlights and body shape are consistent. But we can’t make a definitive identification there either.”

Sarah closed her eyes.

A white SUV.

Michael in the passenger seat.

Someone else driving.

“The license plate?”

“The camera captures the vehicle only from the front and slightly from the side.”

“Then you should be able to see the plate.”

“There wasn’t one on the front.”

Sarah opened her eyes again.

“And the rear?”

“The vehicle leaves the camera’s field of view before the back becomes visible.”

“So you have nothing.”

Daniels remained calm.

“We have a vehicle.”

“Without a front plate.”

“We have an approximate make and model.”

“Approximate.”

“And we have a possible sighting of your husband at a time when no one previously knew where he was.”

Sarah paced slowly across the room.

Three steps to the door.

Three back to the window.

“When?”

“At 2:03:17 Friday afternoon.”

Sarah stopped.

Just after two.

Less than half an hour after Brenda had seen Michael return from the parking lot.

Only minutes after the local login.

After Steve’s observation.

After Michael had walked toward the old archive room.

“Was he conscious?”

The question left her mouth before she knew she was going to ask it.

Daniels was silent for a moment.

“We can’t determine that from the footage.”

“Was he sitting upright?”

“Yes.”

“Did he move?”

“Not clearly enough for us to say with confidence.”

“Was he looking out the window?”

“His head appears to be turned slightly toward the driver.”

“Appears?”

“I can’t promise you more than that.”

Sarah turned toward the door.

Her hand was already on the handle.

“Who’s driving?”

“That isn’t visible either.”

“A man or a woman?”

“Unclear.”

“Anything?”

“The person behind the wheel is mostly obscured by reflections and the vehicle frame.”

Sarah opened the door.

The hallway lay quiet before her.

She spoke more softly.

“Is Michael going willingly?”

“We don’t know.”

“Can you see a weapon?”

“No.”

“Is he restrained?”

“Not that we can tell.”

“Is he trying to open the door?”

“No.”

“Then he might have gotten in willingly.”

“That’s possible.”

“Or someone forced him.”

“That’s possible too.”

Sarah rested her forehead against the doorframe.

“You keep telling me only what’s possible.”

“Because right now, that’s all I can honestly tell you.”

A door clicked softly downstairs.

Then footsteps.

Slow.

Barefoot.

Sarah looked down the stairs.

No one appeared.

Not yet.

“Why do you want me to come to the station?”

“We’d like you to review the footage.”

Sarah straightened.

“Why me?”

“You know the way Michael moves.”

“The way he moves?”

“How he sits. How he holds his head. His posture. Things that may stand out to someone who has known him for years more than they would to us.”

Sarah looked back into the guest room.

The bed.

The overturned glass.

The water dripping slowly onto the floor.

A few minutes earlier, she had been asleep.

Now she was expected to decide from a blurred image whether the man inside a stranger’s vehicle was her husband.

“What happens if I say it’s him?”

“We’ll continue to treat the footage as a probable sighting.”

“Continue?”

“We aren’t waiting for your assessment. The search for the vehicle is already underway.”

Sarah held her breath.

“Since when?”

“Since we reviewed the footage.”

“Are you looking for every white Yukon in Port St. Lucie?”

“For vehicles consistent with the image, the time and the area. We’re also checking additional cameras along possible routes.”

“Which routes?”

“That depends on which direction the vehicle went after leaving the access road.”

“And you don’t know.”

“Not yet.”

The words remained between them.

Not yet.

Since Friday, Sarah’s entire life had been made of those two words.

Not found yet.

Not known yet.

Not certain yet.

Not dead yet.

Not home yet.

A small figure appeared at the top of the stairs.

Emma.

She wore Michael’s blue hoodie over her pajamas.

The sleeves were too long and covered most of her hands.

Her hair stood up on one side.

She looked at Sarah.

Then at the phone.

Her face changed immediately.

“Mom?”

Sarah closed her eyes.

Only for a fraction of a second.

“I have to go,” she said quietly.

“Come to the station,” Daniels said. “As soon as you can.”

“I’m coming.”

“Drive carefully.”

Sarah ended the call.

Emma still stood on the landing.

“Was that Officer Daniels?”

Sarah slowly lowered the phone.

“Yes.”

Emma came down one step.

“Did they find Dad?”

“No.”

The word struck both of them.

Emma stopped.

“Then what?”

Sarah looked at her daughter.

At the hoodie.

The sleeves that were too long.

The small piece of Michael Emma had wrapped around herself.

She wanted to say there was nothing certain.

That the footage was poor.

That the police could be wrong.

That the person in the passenger seat could be anyone.

But Emma would hear the lie.

She always did.

Sarah walked to the stairs.

“They found a recording.”

“Of Dad?”

“Maybe.”

One word.

It was enough.

Emma hurried down the remaining steps.

“What does maybe mean?”

Sarah placed both hands on her shoulders.

“There’s a white vehicle in the recording.”

“What kind of vehicle?”

“A large SUV.”

“And Dad is inside?”

“There’s someone in the passenger seat who looks like him.”

Emma stared at her.

“But they don’t know if it’s him.”

“No.”

“Why not?”

“The camera was too far away.”

Emma looked at the phone in Sarah’s hand.

“Can I see it?”

“Officer Daniels wants me to come to the station to look at it.”

“Then I’m coming with you.”

Sarah did not answer immediately.

Emma pulled the hoodie more tightly around herself.

“Mom.”

“I don’t know if that’s a good idea.”

“It’s Dad.”

“That’s exactly why.”

“I know him too.”

“Emma—”

“Maybe you won’t recognize him.”

Sarah felt the sentence hit her.

Emma noticed immediately.

“I didn’t mean it like that.”

“I know.”

“But I know how he sits in a car.”

Her words came faster.

“He never rolls the window all the way down when the air conditioning is on. And when he talks to someone, he doesn’t just turn his head. He always turns his shoulder too, because he says otherwise his neck—”

Her voice broke off.

Sarah knew exactly what Michael said.

That his neck was no longer twenty.

Emma swallowed.

“I could help.”

Sarah pulled her close.

Emma remained stiff.

Only for a moment.

Then she wrapped her arms around her mother.

“Please let me come.”

Sarah held her.

Over Emma’s shoulder, she looked toward the bright morning beyond the window.

A Sunday was beginning outside.

Somewhere on a secured recording, a white SUV was leaving an access road.

And maybe Michael was inside.

Not vanished.

Not simply gone.

Taken.

Or leaving with someone.

Sarah did not know which possibility was worse.

“We’ll talk to Laura and Daniel first,” she said.

Emma pulled away.

“That isn’t a no.”

Sarah looked at her.

“No.”

Emma nodded.

For the moment, that was enough.

Upstairs, Byte began running in his wheel.

The steady sound drifted through the open bedroom door.

Sarah looked at her phone again.

**07:48 AM**

Eight minutes.

That was all it had taken.

Sunday had brought its first answer.

And a hundred new questions with it.

---

Sarah and Emma were still standing at the foot of the stairs when the Vances’ bedroom door opened.

Laura appeared in the hallway.

She wore a robe and had tied her hair back loosely.

Her gaze went first to Emma.

Then to Sarah’s phone.

“Was that the police?”

Sarah nodded.

Daniel stepped into the hallway behind Laura.

“Did they find Michael?”

“No.”

Sarah looked at Emma.

“But they may have a recording of him.”

Laura slowly came down the stairs.

“May have?”

“A camera behind Chesapeake recorded a white SUV. There’s a man in the passenger seat who looks like Michael.”

Daniel stopped on the last step.

“How much like him?”

“Similar height and build. The shape of his head and hair are consistent. He’s also wearing a dark blue top.”

“And his face?”

Sarah shook her head.

“Not clear enough.”

Laura raised a hand to her mouth.

“Oh God.”

Emma pulled the sleeves of the hoodie over her hands.

“Officer Daniels wants Mom to look at the recording.”

“We’re leaving in a few minutes,” Sarah said.

Emma held her mother’s gaze.

“We are.”

Laura understood immediately.

She looked at Sarah.

“Are you taking her?”

“I don’t know yet.”

“I want to go,” Emma said.

Daniel came the rest of the way down the stairs.

“Maybe Sarah should look at it alone first.”

Emma turned toward him.

“Why?”

Daniel searched for the right words.

“Because we don’t know what’s on it.”

“Dad is sitting in a car.”

“Maybe.”

“And if it is him, I might recognize him.”

No one answered immediately.

The quiet hum of the refrigerator came from the kitchen.

Emma looked from one adult to the next.

“You said yesterday that we’d all go together if the police needed us.”

Sarah felt her stomach tighten.

She had promised exactly that.

Laura looked at her.

Not pressing.

Only reminding her.

Sarah crouched in front of Emma.

“If you come with us, you may hear things that frighten you.”

“I’m already frightened.”

“And it may not be Dad at all.”

“Then I want to know that too.”

Sarah held her gaze.

Emma did not look brave.

Not unshakable.

She was afraid.

But she still did not want to be left behind.

Sarah brushed a strand of hair from her face.

“You stay with me the entire time.”

Emma nodded immediately.

“And if Officer Daniels says there’s something you shouldn’t see, we don’t argue.”

A brief hesitation.

Then Emma nodded again.

“Okay.”

Sarah stood.

Laura placed a hand on her arm.

“I’ll make you something to take with you.”

“I can’t eat.”

“Then you’ll take it anyway.”

Daniel reached for his car keys.

“I’ll drive.”

Sarah shook her head.

“You don’t have to—”

“I know.”

He glanced at Emma.

“But neither of you should have to drive right now.”

Sarah wanted to object.

Then she looked at her hand.

It was trembling.

Only slightly.

But enough.

She nodded.

“Thank you.”

Upstairs, Byte began running in his wheel again.

Emma looked toward the stairs.

“I need to give him water.”

“Go,” Sarah said.

“We leave in ten minutes.”

Emma ran upstairs.

The blue hoodie hung far too large on her.

Sarah watched her go.

Then she looked at Daniel.

“If that really is Michael…”

Daniel waited.

Sarah did not finish the sentence.

She did not need to.

---

Ten minutes later, Daniel had the SUV waiting in the driveway.

Laura had placed two bottles of water and a small paper bag on the back seat.

“Just in case,” she said.

Sarah nodded.

Emma was already buckled in behind the passenger seat.

She still wore Michael’s blue hoodie over her pajamas. She had only pulled on a pair of jeans beneath it.

Laura leaned through the open door.

“Call as soon as you can.”

“I will,” Sarah said.

Laura looked at Emma.

“And you stay with your mom.”

Emma nodded.

“I promise.”

Daniel started the engine.

Sarah pulled the door closed.

---

The roads were almost empty.

Sunday morning.

Closed stores.

Vacant parking lots.

A woman jogged along the roadside wearing headphones.

At a gas station, a man stood beside his pickup wiping the windshield clean.

Sarah noticed every white vehicle.

A delivery van.

A small SUV.

An old Chevrolet with a rusted tailgate.

Each time, her head lifted.

Each time, she knew immediately it was not the one.

Emma sat quietly in the back seat.

After a few minutes, she asked:

“Did Dad know anyone with a white Yukon?”

Sarah turned halfway toward her.

“Not that I know of.”

“Maybe a customer?”

“Maybe.”

“Or someone from work?”

Sarah faced forward again.

“The police will check.”

Emma pulled the sleeves farther over her hands.

“Frank has a big car.”

Daniel glanced briefly into the rearview mirror.

So did Sarah.

“What kind?” she asked.

Emma shrugged.

“I don’t know. Dark. Not white.”

“Then it isn’t the vehicle in the recording.”

Emma nodded.

But Sarah could tell she was already thinking beyond that.

Every name.

Every car.

Every person Michael knew.

Everything could suddenly matter.

“We’re not going to guess,” Sarah said gently.

Emma looked out the window.

“Dad always says guessing is just a hypothesis without enough data.”

For a moment, no one spoke.

Then Daniel said quietly:

“That sounds like him.”

Emma pressed her forehead to the glass.

“Yeah.”

---

When the police station came into view, Sarah sat a little straighter.

Daniel turned into the visitor parking lot.

“Do you want me to come inside?”

Sarah looked at Emma.

Then at Daniel.

“As far as reception.”

He nodded.

Nothing more needed to be said.

Daniel switched off the engine.

Sarah opened her door.

Emma remained seated.

Only for a moment.

“Mom?”

Sarah leaned back toward her.

“Yes?”

“What if I know immediately that it’s him?”

Sarah held her gaze.

“Then you say so.”

“And if I don’t know?”

“Then you say exactly that.”

Emma looked toward the station entrance.

“Okay.”

She got out.

Sarah waited beside her.

Together, they walked toward the automatic glass doors.

---

The doors opened.

Cold air met them.

The same receptionist from the day before sat behind the desk. She recognized Sarah immediately.

“Mrs. Weber.”

“Officer Daniels is expecting us.”

The woman glanced at Emma and Daniel.

Then she picked up the telephone.

“One moment, please.”

Emma did not sit down.

She stayed close beside Sarah, holding one of the hoodie’s overlong sleeves in her hand.

A door behind reception opened.

Daniels stepped into the waiting area.

He wore the same calm expression he had on Saturday.

But when his gaze reached Emma, he stopped for a moment.

“Mrs. Weber.”

“Officer Daniels.”

He nodded to Daniel.

Then looked back at Emma.

“Good morning, Emma.”

“Good morning.”

Her voice was more cautious than usual.

Daniels looked at Sarah.

“Could I speak with you alone for a moment first?”

Emma immediately looked at her mother.

“Why?”

Daniels answered her himself.

“Because I’d like to explain to your mother exactly what can be seen in the recording.”

“I can hear that too.”

“I know.”

He spoke calmly, without talking down to her.

“But first I need to make sure you don’t see something no one has prepared you for.”

Emma folded her arms.

The long sleeves hung over her hands.

“Is there something bad in it?”

“Not in the way you mean.”

“Then I can come too.”

Sarah placed a hand on Emma’s shoulder.

“Just a few minutes.”

Emma looked at her.

“You’ll come back out?”

“Of course.”

“Before you play the video?”

Sarah did not hesitate.

“Yes.”

Emma looked at Daniels.

“Promise?”

Daniels nodded.

“We won’t play it before your mother has spoken with you again.”

Emma studied him as though deciding whether she could trust him.

Then she sat beside Daniel.

“Okay.”

Sarah followed Daniels through the door.

Just before it closed, she turned around once more.

Emma sat on the edge of the chair.

Michael’s blue hoodie wrapped around her.

Her gaze fixed on her mother.

Then the door closed.

---

Daniels did not take Sarah to the same meeting room they had used on Saturday.

This time, they walked farther down the corridor.

He stopped at a closed door.

“Before Emma comes in, there are two things you need to know.”

Sarah felt her stomach tighten.

“What?”

“First, the footage is not conclusive.”

“You said that on the phone.”

“In a single still frame, the person looks very much like Michael.”

Daniels held her gaze.

“Once the recording is moving, it becomes harder.”

“Why?”

“Reflections. Motion blur. Parts of the face keep disappearing behind the A-pillar.”

Sarah looked at the closed door.

“And the second thing?”

“Emma cannot be made to feel that she has to decide whether this man is her father.”

Sarah said nothing.

Daniels continued.

“We can ask whether she notices anything.”

“How he sits. How he moves.”

“But the responsibility for identifying him does not belong to her.”

Sarah thought of Emma’s question in the car.

*What if I know immediately that it’s him?*

“She’ll still think it depends on her answer.”

“Then we have to make it clear to her together that it doesn’t.”

Daniels placed a hand on the door handle.

“Are you ready to look at it alone first?”

Sarah stared at the door.

A screen waited behind it.

A white SUV.

And perhaps Michael.

“No,” she said.

Then she took one deep breath.

“But show me.”

---

Daniels opened the door.

The room beyond was small.

A desk.

Two chairs.

A television mounted on the wall, switched off.

A computer monitor sat on the desk.

Daniels pulled out the second chair for Sarah.

“Have a seat.”

She did.

He sat beside her and moved the mouse.

The screen came to life.

A gray window.

A timeline.

A frozen image.

At first, Sarah saw only the road.

The edge of a warehouse.

A piece of sky.

Then the white SUV.

It was already halfway into the frame.

Large.

Wide.

Its front angled slightly toward the camera.

Daniels said nothing.

Sarah leaned closer.

Two people were visible behind the windshield.

The driver almost completely hidden by reflections.

The passenger clearer.

Not clear.

Only clearer.

A face behind glass.

The familiar shape of his head and hair.

A dark blue top.

His shoulders turned slightly toward the driver.

Sarah’s breath caught.

“That’s him.”

Daniels did not look away from the screen.

“Take your time.”

“I don’t need time.”

Her voice was quiet.

But certain.

She pointed at the passenger.

“That’s Michael.”

Daniels kept the image frozen at the same frame.

“What do you recognize?”

Sarah moved even closer to the monitor.

“The posture.”

She pointed at his left shoulder.

“When he talks to someone, he turns like that.”

Her finger moved slightly upward.

“And his head.”

“He always sits a little too far back when he isn’t driving.”

Daniels made a note.

“The shirt?”

“His company polo.”

“Could it be another dark blue shirt?”

Sarah looked at him.

“Yes.”

Then back at the screen.

“But it isn’t only the shirt.”

Daniels started the recording.

The SUV moved slowly through the frame.

The windshield reflected the sky.

For a moment, the passenger’s face almost disappeared entirely.

Then it became visible again.

Only for a few frames.

Sarah held her breath.

The person’s head moved slightly.

Not toward the camera.

Toward the driver.

Sarah placed one hand on the edge of the desk.

“Back.”

Daniels rewound several seconds.

“Again.”

He played the section once more.

Sarah watched only the passenger.

The slight turn of the upper body.

The angle of the head.

The right shoulder sitting a little lower than the left.

“That’s him,” she said again.

This time, it did not sound like a guess.

It sounded like something she had not wanted to know.

Daniels paused the recording.

“You’re certain?”

Sarah kept looking at the blurred face.

“As certain as I can be from this image.”

Daniels nodded slowly.

“That is an important qualification.”

“Maybe to you.”

Sarah leaned back.

“To me, it’s Michael.”

For several seconds, only the frozen image remained on the monitor.

Then Daniels pointed at the vehicle.

“Do you recognize this SUV?”

Sarah studied the white paint.

The large grille.

The shape of the headlights.

She searched her memory.

Michael’s friends.

Neighbors.

Coworkers.

Customers whose names she might have heard once.

Nothing.

“No.”

“Have you ever seen Michael in a vehicle like this?”

“No.”

“Do you know anyone who owns a white GMC Yukon or a similar model?”

Sarah shook her head.

“No one I can think of.”

“Family? Friends? Coworkers?”

“No.”

She looked at Daniels.

“I don’t know who this vehicle belongs to.”

Daniels wrote that down as well.

Sarah looked back at the monitor.

Michael sat there.

Beside someone she could not identify.

Inside a vehicle she did not know.

“Can you tell whether he got into it?”

“This camera shows only the access road.”

“So not where the SUV came from.”

“No.”

“And not where Michael entered it.”

“No.”

Sarah stared at the frozen image.

“But he was in that vehicle.”

Daniels set his pen down.

“That is now our most likely assessment too.”

Sarah swallowed.

Until that moment, Michael had vanished from a building.

Now she knew at least one thing.

He had left it.

Just not alone.

---

Daniels did not close the video window.

He left the still image on the screen.

The white SUV.

The reflective windshield.

The person in the passenger seat.

Sarah was still looking at it.

“Should Emma see this?” she asked.

Daniels folded his hands in front of him.

“That depends on what you hope it will accomplish.”

Sarah looked at him.

“She believes she may recognize him.”

“She may.”

“And if she notices something we missed?”

“Then we listen to her.”

Daniels continued in the same calm voice.

“But I won’t ask her to decide whether that man is her father.”

Sarah nodded slowly.

“I’ll tell her first that I believe it’s him.”

“That’s better than letting her see it without warning.”

Sarah stood.

She paused at the door.

“She’ll want to know what happens next.”

“Then we tell her the truth.”

“That you’re looking for a white SUV with no front plate.”

“That we’re reviewing other cameras.”

“That we don’t know who was driving.”

“Yes.”

Sarah looked back at the screen.

The shape of his head.

The posture.

The shoulder turned slightly toward the driver.

Michael.

She opened the door.

---

Emma was still sitting beside Daniel.

On the edge of the chair.

Her hands hidden inside the sleeves of the blue hoodie.

She jumped to her feet when Sarah appeared.

“And?”

Sarah walked over to her.

She did not sit down.

“I saw the recording.”

Emma waited.

“I believe it’s Dad.”

Emma’s face went completely still.

Not relieved.

Not frightened.

Only still.

“You believe it?”

“Yes.”

“Or you know?”

Sarah took her hands.

Even through the sleeves, she could feel how cold Emma’s fingers were.

“The image isn’t good enough to prove it with certainty.”

Emma looked past her toward the closed door.

“But you recognized him.”

“His posture.”

“The way he turns toward the driver.”

Sarah nodded.

“And the shape of his head and hair.”

Emma swallowed.

“Then he got out of the building.”

“It looks that way.”

“He wasn’t alone.”

“No.”

Emma looked at Daniels, who had appeared in the doorway.

“Can I see it?”

Daniels stepped into the waiting area.

“Yes.”

Emma pulled her hands out of the sleeves.

“Now?”

“Now.”

He stopped in front of her.

“But before we go in, I want you to understand something.”

Emma looked at him.

“You do not have to tell us whether that man is your father.”

“But maybe I’ll know.”

“Then you can tell us what you recognize.”

Daniels gestured toward the door.

“You can also say you don’t recognize anything.”

Emma gave the smallest nod.

“Either answer is okay.”

“Okay.”

Daniel stood as well.

“Should I wait here?”

Sarah looked at Emma.

Emma nodded.

“Just Mom.”

Daniel briefly rested a hand on her shoulder.

“I’m right here.”

Emma followed Sarah and Daniels through the door.

This time, it closed behind all three of them.

---

Emma remained standing directly beside Sarah.

Daniels pulled the empty chair back a little.

“You can sit down.”

Emma shook her head.

“I want to be closer.”

Daniels glanced at Sarah.

Then he started the recording again from the beginning.

The white SUV appeared at the edge of the frame.

Slowly.

Almost casually.

Emma leaned toward the monitor.

Her eyes did not follow the vehicle.

Only the passenger.

The reflection moved across the windshield.

For a moment, there was almost nothing to see but a silhouette.

Then the person turned his head slightly toward the driver.

Emma grabbed Sarah’s hand.

“Stop.”

Daniels paused the video.

“What did you notice?”

Emma pointed at the screen.

“That’s Dad.”

Her voice was quiet.

But there was no hesitation in it.

Sarah squeezed her hand.

Daniels remained matter-of-fact.

“What tells you that?”

Emma kept looking at the image.

“The way he turns.”

She moved her own right shoulder slightly forward.

“He always does that.”

“He almost never turns only his head.”

Sarah looked at her.

She had recognized the same thing.

Emma pointed to the frozen figure.

“And he’s sitting too far back.”

“When he isn’t driving, he always pushes the seat back.”

Daniels wrote it down.

“Do you recognize the vehicle?”

For the first time, Emma studied the SUV itself.

The white paint.

The wide front.

The large cabin behind the windshield.

She shook her head.

“No.”

“Has your father ever picked you up in a vehicle like this?”

“No.”

“Do you know anyone who drives one?”

Emma thought about it.

Longer than Sarah had.

Then she shook her head again.

“I don’t think so.”

Daniels played the recording once more.

The SUV continued through the frame.

Emma tracked Michael’s silhouette.

“Again.”

Daniels rewound it.

She said nothing during the second viewing.

Nothing during the third.

Only when the vehicle had nearly left the frame did she ask:

“Can you tell if he’s wearing his seat belt?”

Daniels enlarged the image.

The outlines became coarser.

Small square blocks replaced even more of the detail.

“Not with certainty.”

Emma stared at the area between Michael’s shoulder and the vehicle door.

“Dad buckles up immediately.”

Sarah looked at Daniels.

“Even when he’s the passenger,” Emma added.

“He won’t even start the engine if I’m not buckled in yet.”

“I believe you,” Daniels said.

“But we can’t reliably identify the seat belt in this recording.”

Emma nodded.

She looked disappointed.

Not in him.

In the image.

As though it had promised an answer and then refused to give it.

“Does he look afraid?”

Daniels paused the recording again.

“We can’t assess that.”

“But he’s just sitting there.”

“Yes.”

“He isn’t hitting the windshield.”

“No.”

“He isn’t trying to get out.”

“Not in the portion of the footage we can see.”

Emma lowered her eyes.

“Maybe he knew the driver.”

Sarah put an arm around her.

“Maybe.”

Emma looked back at the screen.

“Or maybe he wanted it to look that way.”

Daniels raised his head.

Sarah felt her hand tighten around Emma’s shoulder.

“What do you mean?” he asked.

Emma pulled the hoodie more tightly around herself.

“Dad stays calm when something goes wrong.”

“Always.”

She studied the blurred figure behind the windshield.

“Maybe he didn’t want the driver to know he was scared.”

Daniels did not write the sentence down immediately.

He looked at Emma for a moment.

Not like a child.

Like a witness.

“Has your father ever told you what he does when he feels uncertain?”

Emma thought.

“He looks at everything.”

“Everything?”

“Exits. Doors. Who is sitting where.”

She pointed at the screen.

“And he remembers things.”

Daniels nodded slowly.

“Did he tell you that directly?”

“Not exactly.”

Emma pulled the sleeves back over her hands.

“But he always does it.”

Sarah brushed her thumb across Emma’s shoulder.

“Michael observes.”

“Yes,” Emma said.

“When we go somewhere, he still knows later who was sitting near the door or which car was parked outside.”

Daniels wrote something down.

Emma noticed.

“Does that help?”

“Maybe.”

He set the pen down again.

“Thank you, Emma.”

She looked at him.

“For what?”

“For telling us what you noticed.”

Daniels closed his notebook.

“We don’t need to ask anything more of you right now.”

Emma looked back at the monitor.

The white SUV still filled the frozen frame.

Michael sat in the passenger seat.

Or someone who looked enough like him that neither Sarah nor Emma had any doubt.

“What happens now?” Sarah asked.

“We continue searching for the vehicle and reviewing other cameras along possible routes.”

“And if you find something?”

“I’ll call you immediately.”

Daniels looked first at Sarah and then at Emma.

“No matter what time it is.”

Sarah nodded.

“Thank you.”

Daniels stood and switched off the monitor.

The image disappeared.

For a moment, Sarah and Emma saw only their own reflections in the black screen.

Emma reached for her mother’s hand.

Together, they left the room.

Daniel was still waiting where they had left him.

When he saw their faces, he did not ask a question.

Sarah only shook her head slightly.

“Nothing certain yet.”

Emma pulled Michael’s blue hoodie more tightly around herself.

Then they walked together toward the automatic glass doors.

The bright Sunday morning waited outside.

And somewhere out there was a white SUV no one recognized.

---

The drive back was quieter than the journey to the station.

Daniel asked no questions.

Sarah said nothing.

Emma sat in the back seat looking out the window.

She had pulled the sleeves of Michael’s blue hoodie over her hands.

At every intersection, Sarah looked for white SUVs.

She knew it was pointless.

She did it anyway.

---

When Daniel turned into the Vances’ driveway, Laura was already standing behind the front door.

She opened it before the engine had fully stopped.

Her gaze moved immediately across their faces.

“Come inside.”

No one stopped in the hallway.

Daniel led them directly into the kitchen.

Laura set four glasses of water on the table, although no one had asked for one.

Emma sat beside Sarah.

Not across from her.

Daniel remained at the kitchen counter at first.

Laura took the chair on Sarah’s other side.

“Was it him?” she asked quietly.

Sarah glanced at Emma.

Then nodded.

“I think so.”

Emma corrected her immediately.

“It was him.”

Laura looked at her.

Emma held her gaze.

“Mom and I both recognized the way he sits.”

Sarah placed a hand on Emma’s arm.

“The police can’t confirm it beyond doubt yet.”

Daniel folded his arms.

“But they think it’s him too?”

“They believe it probably is.”

“When was it recorded?”

“Friday at 2:03.”

Laura looked at Sarah in confusion.

“After the last sighting inside the building.”

“Yes.”

Sarah exhaled slowly.

“A large white SUV drives along the rear access road. Probably a GMC Yukon.”

“And Michael is in the passenger seat,” Emma said.

Daniel pulled out the chair opposite them and sat down.

“Who’s driving?”

“They can’t tell.”

“License plate?”

Sarah shook her head.

“The camera shows only the front and part of the side. There was no front plate. It never captured the rear.”

Laura wrapped both hands around her glass.

“Did you recognize the vehicle?”

“No.”

“Emma?”

She shook her head too.

“Never seen it.”

For several seconds, no one spoke.

The refrigerator hummed.

Upstairs, Byte could be heard running in his wheel.

Laura looked at Sarah.

“Then Michael got out of the building.”

Sarah nodded slowly.

“Yes.”

“But not into his own car.”

“No.”

Daniel looked down at the table.

“And not alone.”

This time, no one answered.

Emma pulled the hoodie more tightly around herself.

Sarah noticed.

“Officer Daniels says they’re checking more cameras and looking for the vehicle.”

Laura reached across the table and placed her hand over Sarah’s.

“Then at least we know the trail doesn’t end inside the building.”

Sarah looked at her.

She wanted to agree.

Wanted to say that was good.

That a new lead meant hope.

But on the dark monitor at the police station, Michael had been sitting beside someone none of them knew.

“No,” she said quietly.

“Now we only know it continues somewhere else.”

---

Sarah waited until Emma had gone upstairs with Laura.

Daniel stayed in the kitchen but said nothing.

Sarah picked up her phone from the table and found Henry’s number.

He answered on the second ring.

“Sarah?”

He sounded as though he had been awake for some time.

“The police found a recording.”

The other end went silent.

“Of Michael?”

“Probably.”

Sarah looked at the empty chair beside her.

“A large white SUV leaves the rear access road Friday at 2:03.”

“And Michael?”

“He’s in the passenger seat.”

Henry drew an audible breath.

“Are you sure?”

“The image is poor.”

Sarah closed her eyes for a moment.

“But yes. I’m sure.”

Henry said nothing for several seconds.

“Alive?”

“He’s sitting upright.”

“Does he move?”

“A little. He turns toward the driver.”

Sarah heard Henry sit down somewhere.

“Who’s driving?”

“They can’t tell.”

“License plate?”

“No front plate. The rear wasn’t recorded.”

Henry swore under his breath.

Not loudly.

A single word.

Then his voice was calm again.

“What kind of vehicle exactly?”

“The police think it may be a GMC Yukon.”

“White?”

“Yes.”

Sarah waited.

She could almost hear Henry thinking.

Names.

Customers.

Vendors.

Former employees.

Everyone who had passed through Chesapeake over the years.

“Does that mean anything to you?” she asked.

“Not yet.”

“Do you know anyone with a vehicle like that?”

“Not for certain.”

Henry sounded dissatisfied with his own answer.

“Frank doesn’t drive a white Yukon. Neither does Steve. Brenda has a small car.”

“What about customers?”

“Too many.”

He exhaled slowly.

“Outside technicians. Vendors. People from client companies. Some of them I see once a year. Some for five minutes.”

“Could someone drive onto the back of the property without Brenda seeing them?”

“Yes.”

The answer came too quickly.

Sarah straightened.

“How?”

“Through the service entrance.”

Henry was quiet for a moment.

“But normally, they’d need a reason.”

“Normally.”

“Yes.”

Silence again.

Then Henry said:

“I’m going through every name I can remember.”

“The police are already doing that.”

“I know.”

His voice grew quieter.

“I still have to do it myself.”

Sarah understood.

She had done nothing else since Friday.

“Call me if you think of anything.”

“Even if it’s only a guess.”

“Even then.”

Henry was silent for another moment.

“Sarah?”

“Yes?”

“If that really is Michael, then at least he made it out of the building.”

Sarah looked toward the stairs.

Upstairs, Emma was speaking quietly with Laura.

“Yes.”

“That’s more than we knew yesterday.”

Sarah saw the white SUV again.

Michael in the passenger seat.

Beside someone without a face.

“I just don’t know whether it’s good news.”

Henry did not answer immediately.

“No,” he said at last.

“Neither do I.”

Sarah ended the call and placed the phone on the table in front of her.

Daniel waited another moment.

“Can Henry place the vehicle?”

Sarah shook her head.

“Not for certain.”

“But?”

“He wants to go through every customer, vendor and former employee he can remember.”

Daniel nodded slowly.

“I’d probably do the same.”

Footsteps came from the stairs.

Emma appeared first, Laura directly behind her.

“What did Henry say?”

Sarah pulled the chair beside her out a little.

Emma sat down.

“He doesn’t recognize the vehicle.”

“Not at all?”

“Not consciously, at least.”

Emma looked down at the table, disappointed.

“Then no one knows who it belongs to.”

“The police are looking.”

“I know.”

Sarah put an arm around her.

Emma leaned briefly against her shoulder.

Only briefly.

Then she looked through the patio doors.

The garden lay bright in the morning sun.

The pool was perfectly still.

“Are we staying here today?” she asked.

Laura answered immediately.

“You can stay as long as you want.”

Sarah looked at Emma.

Since Friday, they had waited in the Vances’ house.

Slept.

Or at least tried to.

Laura and Daniel had given them space without leaving them alone.

But Sarah knew they could not remain there forever.

“I thought we could go home this evening.”

“To the apartment?” Emma asked.

Sarah nodded.

“If that’s okay with you.”

Emma considered it.

“With Byte?”

“Of course with Byte.”

“And the big cage?”

Daniel smiled faintly.

“We’ll fit it in the car.”

Emma pulled Michael’s hoodie more tightly around herself.

“Can I take this too?”

Sarah looked at her.

“It belongs to you for now.”

Emma ran her fingers over the sleeve.

“Only until Dad comes back.”

Sarah felt Laura place her hand on the edge of the table.

Very close to hers.

Not touching.

Only there.

“Only until Dad comes back,” Sarah said.

Emma nodded.

Then she looked around the kitchen.

“Can we go after dinner?”

“Yes.”

“Then I don’t have to tell Chloe we’re leaving right away.”

Laura smiled sadly.

“She knows you have to go home eventually.”

Emma looked toward the stairs.

“I’ll tell her later anyway.”

Sarah pulled her a little closer.

“That’s a good idea.”

The phone vibrated on the table.

All four of them looked at it immediately.

Only a weather alert.

Possible thunderstorms that afternoon.

Sarah turned the screen off again.

For a moment, no one spoke.

Then Laura stood.

“Breakfast.”

She opened the refrigerator.

“And this time I’m not accepting ‘I’m not hungry’ from any of the six people in this house.”

Daniel raised one hand.

“I didn’t say anything.”

“Your face did.”

At that moment, Leo shuffled into the kitchen.

His hair stood in every direction, and he still had his pillow tucked beneath one arm.

“Why is everyone awake already?”

Then he saw Emma in Michael’s oversized hoodie.

His expression grew serious.

“Did you find her dad?”

Emma shook her head.

“Not yet.”

Leo thought for a moment.

Then he pulled out the empty chair beside her.

“Then I’ll wait with you again today.”

He sat down and placed the pillow in his lap.

Laura quickly turned back toward the refrigerator.

But Sarah saw her smile.

They would remain with the Vances until evening.

After that, they would go home.

Not because the waiting was over.

Because they had to learn how to endure it there too.

---

Sunday passed more slowly than Saturday.

In the early afternoon, a summer storm moved across the neighborhood.

Rain struck the patio doors.

The pool disappeared behind a gray curtain, and thunder rolled somewhere above the rooftops.

The children stayed inside.

Chloe and Emma sat on the floor of her room drawing.

Leo lay on his stomach between them and insisted that the next Major Tom page needed a “space-hamster commander.”

“Byte needs a helmet,” he said.

Emma looked toward the large cage.

Byte was asleep beneath his little shelter and showed no interest in his impending promotion.

“He’d eat the helmet,” she said.

“Then he needs an edible helmet.”

Chloe shook her head.

“That makes no sense at all.”

Leo sat up.

“Everything makes sense in space.”

Emma smiled.

“That isn’t scientifically accurate.”

“Yes, it is.”

“No.”

“Your dad would say no too,” Leo said.

The sentence came easily.

Almost casually.

Then Emma’s smile grew smaller.

Chloe put her pencil down beside the paper.

Leo looked between them.

“When he comes back, we can ask him.”

Emma looked at the unfinished drawing.

“Yeah.”

She picked up the pencil again.

“Then we’ll ask him.”

---

By evening, the rain had stopped.

Water dripped from the palms and collected in small puddles across the patio.

Sarah stood in the guest room folding the last pieces of clothing into the bag.

There was not much.

Still, it felt as though she were ending an entire stay.

Two nights.

That was all.

And yet Friday already seemed far away.

Laura leaned against the doorframe.

“You don’t have to go.”

Sarah placed Emma’s pajamas in the bag.

“I know.”

“The guest room stays available.”

“I know.”

Laura was quiet for a moment.

“Then tell me the real reason.”

Sarah closed the zipper.

“Emma needs something that feels like her own life again.”

“And you?”

Sarah looked at the packed bag.

“Probably me too.”

Laura stepped into the room.

“You don’t have to pretend everything is normal once you’re there.”

“I couldn’t.”

“Good.”

Laura picked up the smaller bag from the bed.

“Then let’s not pretend you’re going through this alone either.”

Sarah looked at her.

“We’re twenty minutes away.”

“I wasn’t talking about the distance.”

Sarah nodded.

“I know.”

---

Downstairs, Daniel and Leo were preparing Byte’s large cage for the drive.

“The wheel has to come out,” Daniel said.

Leo held it with both hands.

“But maybe he’ll want to run during the trip.”

“Then the wheel will fly across the cage at the first turn.”

Leo considered that.

“Byte would think that was funny.”

“Byte also thinks cardboard is food.”

Emma knelt beside them and opened the small side door.

“He’ll go back into the travel cage for the drive.”

Leo looked at Byte.

“But it’s smaller.”

“Only for twenty minutes.”

“Does he know that?”

Emma carefully lifted Byte from the large cage.

“I’ll explain it to him.”

She placed him inside the travel cage and closed the door.

Byte immediately began pushing the fresh bedding into one corner.

“He’s angry,” Leo said.

“He’s always angry when someone changes his apartment.”

Daniel lifted the large cage.

“Then we have something in common.”

Chloe stood beside Emma holding the case of colored pencils.

“Will you text me when you get there?”

“Yes.”

“Even if it’s late.”

“It’s twenty minutes.”

“Still.”

Emma took the pencils from her.

“I’ll text.”

Chloe nodded.

Then she hugged Emma.

Not for long.

But more tightly than usual.

“See you tomorrow?”

Emma glanced at Sarah.

Sarah answered for her.

“We’ll see how she feels in the morning.”

Chloe released her.

“Then maybe see you tomorrow.”

Emma nodded.

“Maybe see you tomorrow.”

Leo came from the kitchen carrying Byte’s food container.

“You would’ve forgotten this.”

Emma took it.

“Thanks.”

“I can look after Grissom if you have to go to the beach again.”

Emma smiled.

“Grissom lives at Dad’s house.”

Leo stopped.

“Oh.”

Then his expression became serious.

“Then someone has to go there.”

“Mom will take care of him.”

“Good.”

He nodded, as though at least one problem had been solved.

Daniel carried the large cage toward the front door.

Outside, the street still shone with rain.

Sarah picked up the overnight bag.

Emma lifted Byte’s travel cage and pulled Michael’s blue hoodie more tightly around herself.

For the first time since Friday, they were returning somewhere.

Just not where they truly wanted to go.

---

Laura stood beneath the porch roof with her arms folded.

Daniel had already placed Byte’s large cage in the cargo area and wedged a blanket around it to keep it from sliding.

The bags lay beside it.

Emma’s sketchbook and pencils stood upright between the seats.

Sarah checked everything a second time.

Then a third.

Not because she thought they had forgotten something.

Because she was not ready to get in.

Laura pulled her into a hug.

“Call me.”

“I will.”

“Not only if something happens.”

Sarah held her a moment longer.

“Okay.”

Daniel stepped toward her.

“If you need anything tonight, we’ll come.”

“We’ll be fine.”

He looked at her.

Sarah corrected herself.

“I’ll call.”

“Better.”

Beside them, Leo crouched in front of Byte’s travel cage.

“You’ll be back in your big apartment soon.”

Byte pushed bedding against the clear wall.

Leo pointed at him.

“That means yes.”

Emma held the cage with both hands.

“That probably means he wants out.”

Chloe stood directly in front of her.

Neither of them seemed to know what else to say.

“Text me,” Chloe said at last.

“I will.”

“Right away.”

“After twenty minutes.”

“Twenty-one still counts as right away.”

Emma smiled.

“Okay.”

Chloe hugged her once more.

This time, Emma held on just as tightly.

When they separated, Chloe looked at Michael’s hoodie.

“It looks good on you.”

Emma looked down at herself.

“It’s way too big.”

“Still.”

Emma ran a hand over the fabric.

“Dad always says clothes have to work. They don’t have to look good.”

“Then it works.”

Emma nodded.

“Yeah.”

---

Sarah opened the rear door.

Emma carefully placed Byte’s travel cage on the seat beside her and fastened the seat belt around it.

Daniel watched.

“Your dad would approve.”

Emma checked the belt again.

“He’d probably test it twice.”

Daniel gave it a gentle pull.

“Now it’s been tested twice.”

Emma looked at him.

“Thanks.”

Sarah got behind the wheel.

Laura remained beside the open door.

“Drive carefully.”

“We will.”

Sarah started the engine.

The windshield wipers pushed the last drops of rain from the glass.

As she slowly backed out of the driveway, the Vances remained in front of the house.

Laura with one hand on Leo’s shoulder.

Daniel beside her.

Chloe barefoot on the wet path.

Emma turned and waved until the house disappeared around a corner.

Then she faced forward again.

For several minutes, neither of them spoke.

Only Byte moved softly through the bedding.

Sarah drove more slowly than usual.

The roads shone in the light of the setting sun.

At every major intersection, she looked for white SUVs.

Emma noticed.

“It could be anywhere.”

Sarah kept her eyes on the road.

“I know.”

“You’re looking anyway.”

“Yes.”

Emma drew her legs a little closer.

“Me too.”

Sarah glanced into the rearview mirror.

Emma looked out the window.

Michael’s hoodie lay around her shoulders.

The travel cage beside her was safely buckled in.

Behind them, the Vances’ house receded.

Ahead waited Sarah’s apartment.

A place where everything was still exactly as it had been on Friday.

And nothing would ever feel the same again.

---

The entrance to Azure Palms Residences was almost empty.

Sarah drove between the low apartment buildings and parked in front of Building Three.

For a moment, she remained in the car.

Her hands still on the steering wheel.

The engine still running.

Emma looked up through the window.

“Our light is on.”

Sarah followed her gaze.

The small lamp in the third-floor living room was still burning.

She had left it on Friday.

Back then, she had believed she would return that evening.

“Yes,” she said.

Then she switched off the engine.

---

It took two trips.

Sarah carried the overnight bags and Emma’s drawing supplies upstairs first.

Emma took Byte in the travel cage.

She was still wearing Michael’s blue hoodie.

When Sarah unlocked the apartment door, Emma stopped directly behind her.

Sarah opened it.

Warm, stale air met them.

The apartment smelled familiar.

Laundry detergent.

Coffee.

Home.

Sarah’s mail from Friday morning still lay on the small table beside the door.

An advertisement.

A bill.

A letter she had not opened.

Everything waited exactly where she had left it.

Sarah switched on the hallway light.

“We’re home.”

The words sounded strange.

As though she had to inform the apartment.

Emma stepped inside.

She carefully placed Byte’s travel cage on the floor.

Then she looked around.

“It’s quiet.”

“Yes.”

Sarah closed the door behind them.

Emma pulled the hoodie more tightly around herself.

“At Chloe’s, someone was always there.”

Sarah placed her keys in the bowl beside the door.

“Someone’s here too.”

Emma looked at her.

Sarah briefly touched her shoulder.

“We’re here.”

Emma nodded.

It was not the same.

But for the moment, it would have to be enough.

---

Daniel helped them with the second trip.

He carried Byte’s large cage up the stairs while Sarah brought the blanket and the remaining parts.

“Where does it go?” he asked.

Emma led him directly into her room.

“Against the wall beside the desk.”

Daniel set the cage down.

“Here?”

Emma took a step back.

“A little farther left.”

He moved it.

“Like this?”

“A tiny bit more.”

Daniel shifted it almost imperceptibly.

“Perfect.”

Sarah smiled for the first time since the drive.

“Exactly like your father.”

Emma looked at her.

Then at the cage.

“Dad would’ve used a level.”

Daniel studied the top edge.

“Good thing I didn’t bring one.”

Emma gave a faint smile.

Together, they reattached the wheel, water bottle and Byte’s little shelter.

When Emma opened the travel cage, Byte did not wait a second.

He climbed into his larger home and immediately began rearranging the fresh bedding.

Daniel watched him.

“Apparently, he already has improvement requests.”

“Lots,” Emma said.

“And all of them urgent.”

Daniel nodded solemnly.

“Also like your father.”

This time, Emma’s smile lasted a little longer.

---

Daniel stayed until everything was in place.

Then he stood in the apartment doorway, looking between Sarah and Emma.

“You have our number.”

Sarah nodded.

“Yes.”

“And Laura never sleeps deeply anyway.”

“That isn’t true,” Sarah said.

Daniel raised one eyebrow.

“Please don’t tell her I said that.”

Emma held the door halfway open.

“Thanks for driving us.”

“Anytime.”

He gestured toward her room.

“And tell Byte his complaints have been formally recorded.”

“I will.”

Daniel smiled.

Then his expression grew serious again.

“Good night, you two.”

“Good night.”

Sarah closed the door behind him.

His footsteps receded down the stairwell.

A moment later, they heard the main entrance below.

Then they were alone.

---

Sarah opened the refrigerator.

Milk.

Yogurt.

Half a jar of tomato sauce.

Vegetables left from the previous week’s shopping.

She looked inside without really seeing anything.

“Pasta?”

Emma stood behind her.

“Okay.”

“With sauce?”

“Okay.”

Sarah took a pot from the cupboard.

Emma sat at the kitchen table and placed her phone beside her.

Screen facing up.

Volume all the way up.

Sarah noticed.

She said nothing.

Soon, the water was boiling.

The tomato sauce warmed slowly in a smaller pot.

The clatter of dishes and silverware filled the apartment.

Ordinary sounds.

Sarah was grateful for every one of them.

---

They ate at the kitchen table.

Emma wound pasta around her fork without eating much of it.

“Mom?”

“Yes?”

“Do you think Dad knew the camera was there?”

Sarah set down her fork.

“I don’t know.”

“If he knew the area, maybe he did.”

“Maybe.”

Emma looked at her phone.

“Then maybe he sat that way on purpose so someone would recognize him.”

Sarah thought of the recording.

Michael’s slightly turned shoulder.

His familiar posture.

“That’s possible.”

“Or maybe he didn’t know about it at all.”

“That’s possible too.”

Emma sighed.

“I hate those answers.”

“So do I.”

Sarah reached across the table and took her hand.

“But I promise I won’t pretend something is certain when it isn’t.”

Emma squeezed her fingers.

“Dad wouldn’t either.”

“No.”

Sarah looked at the phone between them.

“He wouldn’t.”

---

After dinner, Sarah cleared the plates.

Emma remained at the table for a while.

Her phone still lay in front of her.

Silent.

No message.

No call.

When Sarah returned from the kitchen, Emma had already stood up.

“I’m going to see Byte.”

“Okay.”

Sarah followed a few minutes later.

Emma sat on the floor beside the cage.

She had taken off Michael’s hoodie and folded it neatly over the back of her desk chair.

Byte ran in his wheel.

Steadily.

The same circle again and again.

“He’s happy to be home,” Sarah said.

Emma watched him.

“How do you know?”

“He isn’t complaining anymore.”

Emma looked up at her.

“He always complains.”

“Then he must really feel at home.”

A small smile appeared.

Sarah sat on the floor beside her.

For several minutes, they listened only to the quiet turning of the wheel.

“Do you have to go to work tomorrow?” Emma asked at last.

Sarah shook her head.

“No.”

“Are we staying here?”

“Yes.”

Emma rested her head against Sarah’s shoulder.

“Until Officer Daniels calls.”

Sarah put an arm around her.

“Until Officer Daniels calls.”

---

Later, Emma lay in her own bed.

The blue hoodie was beside her.

Not over the chair.

Not on the floor.

Directly beside her pillow.

Sarah left the bedroom door slightly open.

“Mom?”

“Yes?”

“If the phone rings, will you wake me?”

Sarah paused in the doorway.

“Yes.”

“Right away?”

“Right away.”

Emma nodded.

“Good night.”

“Good night, sweetheart.”

Sarah switched off the light.

---

In the living room, she placed her phone on the coffee table.

Volume at maximum.

Directly beside her.

She sat on the sofa and looked at the dark television screen.

The small hallway light reflected in it.

Nothing else.

No white SUV.

No Michael.

Only a silent apartment and a door left slightly open.

Eventually, Sarah lay down.

The phone remained in her hand.

Sunday ended without another call.

But for the first time since Friday, she knew Michael had probably left the company grounds alive.

The question was no longer only where he had disappeared.

It was who he had left with.

---

*Monday, August 3, 2026*

Monday morning did not feel like a Monday.

Sarah did not set an alarm.

She woke early anyway.

Her phone lay on the pillow beside her.

No missed calls.

No new messages.

She remained there for another moment and listened to the apartment.

The steady sound of Byte’s wheel came from Emma’s room.

Nothing else.

---

Emma was already sitting at the kitchen table in her pajamas when Sarah came in.

Michael’s blue hoodie hung over the back of her chair.

A bowl of cereal sat in front of her, barely touched.

“You’re up early.”

Emma shrugged.

“Byte was loud.”

Sarah started the coffee maker.

“He’s happy to be home.”

“Or he’s planning his escape.”

“Then apparently he learned something from us.”

Emma looked at her.

For a moment, she smiled.

Then she looked back at her phone.

---

The morning passed in small tasks.

Sarah did laundry.

Emma continued working on the Major Tom pages at the kitchen table.

Every so often, she fed Byte.

Then checked her phone.

Sarah did the same.

Neither of them spoke about it.

Around noon, Laura sent a message.

> **How are you both doing?**

Sarah replied:

> **We’re getting through it. Still waiting.**

She studied the words.

*We’re getting through it.*

It was not true.

But it was shorter than the truth.

---

In the early afternoon, Emma moved her drawings into the living room.

Sarah sat at the dining table with her laptop and tried to answer several messages from work.

After ten minutes, she had read the same sentence three times.

She closed the screen.

“Mom?”

“Yes?”

Emma held up a sheet of paper.

Major Tom floated beside a small hamster in an oversized spacesuit.

“Leo wanted the helmet to be edible.”

Sarah studied the drawing.

“Is it?”

“Emergency rations.”

“Sounds sensible.”

Emma placed the sheet beside her.

“Dad would say the helmet loses its structural integrity.”

Sarah smiled.

“He would.”

---

Her phone vibrated.

They both froze.

Sarah looked at the screen.

**Officer Andrew Daniels**

Emma was already on her feet.

Sarah answered.

“Officer Daniels?”

“Mrs. Weber.”

His voice sounded different from Sunday morning.

Not excited.

But firmer.

“We found the SUV a second time.”

Sarah stood.

“Where?”

Emma stepped closer.

Daniels answered immediately.

“At the intersection of Glades Cut-Off Road and Midway Road.”

Sarah looked at Emma.

“When?”

“Friday at 2:09:34 PM.”

Six minutes and seventeen seconds after the first recording.

“Is it the same vehicle?”

“We believe so.”

“Another white Yukon?”

“A large white SUV. The identifying features match the first recording.”

Sarah gripped the phone more tightly.

“And Michael?”

There was a short pause on the other end.

Not the same cautious pause as Sunday.

This time, Daniels sounded certain.

“The second recording is much clearer.”

Emma heard every word.

“What does much clearer mean?” Sarah asked.

“The SUV turns left at the intersection.”

Daniels spoke slowly and precisely.

“As it turns, the angle to the camera changes. For several frames, most of the reflection disappears from the windshield.”

Sarah sat on the edge of the table.

“You can see his face.”

“Yes.”

Emma reached for her hand.

“Are you sure it’s him?”

Daniels answered without hesitation.

“We’re nearly certain.”

Sarah closed her eyes.

Until that moment, she had believed she was prepared to hear those words.

She was not.

“How nearly?”

“The recording is sufficient to identify the passenger as Michael Weber with a very high degree of confidence.”

“Ninety-nine percent,” Emma whispered.

Sarah looked at her.

Daniels had apparently heard.

“That isn’t a figure I would put in an official report.”

His voice remained calm.

“But yes. Approximately.”

Emma tightened her grip on Sarah’s hand.

“Then it’s Dad.”

“Yes,” Daniels said.

This time, no one contradicted her.

Sarah looked at the closed laptop on the table.

At the drawings.

At Major Tom and the hamster with the edible helmet.

“Where is the vehicle going?”

“Westbound on Midway Road.”

“Toward I-95?”

“Yes.”

Sarah straightened.

“And after that?”

“We don’t know yet.”

“North or south?”

“Not yet.”

Those words again.

But this time, a direction came before them.

West.

A road.

A vehicle.

And Michael.

“Do we need to come back to the station?”

“No,” Daniels said. “This time, I don’t need another identification from you. The footage is clear enough.”

“Can you send me a picture?”

“Not at this time. It is part of an active investigation. As soon as I’m able to show you or release the material to you, I’ll let you know.”

Sarah looked at Emma.

“What happens now?”

“We’re checking additional cameras along Midway Road and at the interstate entrances.”

“Call me as soon as you know anything.”

“I will.”

Daniels’ voice remained steady.

“I’ll contact you immediately when we have something reliable. No matter what time it is.”

Sarah closed her eyes for a moment.

“Thank you.”

“For now, don’t go anywhere. Stay reachable.”

“We will.”

“Good.”

A brief pause.

“And Mrs. Weber?”

“Yes?”

“This is the clearest lead we’ve had on Michael.”

Sarah looked at Emma.

“I know.”

Then the call ended.

For the first time, the police knew with near certainty that Michael had left Chesapeake alive.

But no one knew who he had left with.

Or where they were going.

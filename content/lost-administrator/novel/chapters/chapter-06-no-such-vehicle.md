# Chapter 06 — No Such Vehicle

*Tuesday, August 4, 2026*

**08:12 AM**

Sarah already had the phone in her hand before it rang.

Not because she had expected the call.

Because she had barely put it down since waking up.

She stood at the kitchen window looking down at the parking lot beneath the apartment. Sunlight already lay bright across the roofs of the parked cars. A delivery van stopped in front of the neighboring building. Two people walked along the sidewalk carrying coffee cups.

An ordinary Tuesday morning.

Sarah no longer remembered what an ordinary morning felt like.

Behind her, Emma sat at the kitchen table.

Michael's blue hoodie hung around her shoulders. A bowl of cereal stood in front of her, untouched for almost ten minutes.

Byte was running in his wheel.

The steady sound came from Emma's open bedroom and carried through the quiet apartment.

Sarah looked at the screen again.

No missed calls.

No message.

Daniels had not contacted her since their conversation Monday afternoon.

She knew that did not have to mean anything.

He had said he would call only when they had something reliable.

Reliable.

The word had remained in her head since yesterday.

Not probable.

Not possible.

Not maybe.

Reliable.

"Mom."

Sarah looked up.

Emma was watching her from the table.

"You're checking the phone even though you've been looking at it the entire time."

Sarah looked back at the screen.

"I know."

"It would still ring if you put it down."

"I know."

Emma picked up her spoon.

Then put it down again.

"Maybe they won't find anything else."

Sarah was about to answer.

At that moment, the phone vibrated.

Only once.

But both of them froze.

A name appeared on the screen.

**Officer Andrew Daniels**

Sarah answered.

"Officer Daniels?"

Emma was already on her feet.

Daniels' voice was calm.

Not relieved.

Not excited.

But different from Monday.

"Mrs. Weber, we've been able to confirm another part of the route."

Sarah gripped the edge of the kitchen counter with her free hand.

"Where?"

A brief pause.

Only long enough for Daniels to choose his words carefully.

"The white SUV entered Interstate 95."

Emma stepped directly beside her mother.

Sarah looked at her.

"Which direction?"

This time, Daniels answered without hesitation.

"North."

Sarah closed her eyes.

North.

The word gave the vehicle's movement a direction.

But still no destination.

"How do you know?"

"A traffic camera near the entrance ramp captured a vehicle consistent with the earlier footage."

"Consistent means probable again."

"In this case, more than that."

Daniels spoke slowly.

"The vehicle's size, body shape, color and several visible features match. The timing also fits the recording on Midway Road."

Sarah looked at Emma.

She stood so close that her shoulder touched Sarah's arm.

"Can you see Michael?" Emma asked.

Daniels was silent for a moment.

"Not clearly enough to identify him again in this recording."

Emma pulled Michael's hoodie more tightly around herself.

"Then it could be another white SUV."

"Viewed by itself, yes."

Daniels remained calm.

"But we are not viewing it by itself. We're comparing the time, direction of travel, vehicle shape and the order of the cameras."

Sarah looked out the kitchen window again.

Below, a white car moved slowly through the parking lot.

Smaller than the Yukon.

Not the same one.

She watched it anyway until it disappeared behind the next building.

"How far did the vehicle travel on the interstate?"

A pause formed at the other end of the line.

Longer than before.

Sarah turned away from the window.

"Officer Daniels?"

"Not far."

Something in his voice made her tighten her grip on the counter.

"What does that mean?"

"We have another recording."

Emma raised her head.

Sarah said nothing.

Daniels continued.

"The SUV left the interstate again at Exit 129."

Sarah tried to place the number.

"Which road is that?"

"Okeechobee Road."

Emma looked at her mother.

"SR 70," she said quietly.

Sarah nodded.

She knew the road.

Wide near the city.

Emptier the farther west it went.

Daniels confirmed it.

"State Road 70."

"And after that?"

"A camera near the exit captures the vehicle turning west."

Sarah felt her breath catch.

"So it keeps going inland."

"Yes."

Only that one word.

No maybe.

No probably.

Emma slowly sat down on one of the kitchen chairs.

Sarah remained standing.

"How long was the SUV on I-95?"

"Only a few minutes."

"Why would someone get on the interstate if they were going to leave it again almost immediately?"

"We don't know yet."

"You could reach the westbound roads from Midway without doing that."

"Yes."

Daniels did not disagree.

Sarah heard paper rustling at the other end of the line.

"That is exactly why we're looking very closely at this part of the route."

Emma stared at the untouched bowl of cereal.

"Maybe the driver wanted it to look like they were going to stay on the interstate."

Sarah looked at her.

Daniels had heard her too.

"That is one possibility."

"Or he knew where the cameras were," Emma said.

"That is also possible."

Daniels paused briefly.

"But at the moment, none of that is confirmed."

Sarah went to the table and placed a hand on Emma's shoulder.

"How far can you follow the vehicle?"

Daniels did not answer immediately.

When he did, his voice had become more cautious again.

"Onto SR 70."

"How far west?"

"Not far enough yet."

Sarah closed her eyes.

Another boundary.

A road opening in front of them.

And then darkness again.

"You lose it after that."

"For now, yes."

Emma looked up.

"Because there are fewer cameras out there."

Daniels said:

"The farther west you go, the greater the distance between usable recordings."

Sarah sat down beside her daughter.

"What's out there?"

"Many different things."

Daniels stayed deliberately general.

"Residential areas. Agricultural land. Commercial properties. Workshops. Warehouses. Long undeveloped stretches."

Each place appeared immediately in Sarah's mind.

A building behind a closed gate.

A windowless warehouse.

A dirt road.

The white Yukon hidden behind palms and brush.

She forced the images away again.

"Are you searching there?"

"We're working to establish the next reliable point on the route."

"That wasn't my question."

Daniels was silent.

Sarah waited.

"The new direction of travel is already being distributed to the involved units," he said at last.

"And the vehicle?"

"The alert information is being updated as well."

"As far as Okeechobee?"

"We are not limiting the search to the city limits of Port St. Lucie."

Sarah looked at Emma.

She sat completely still.

Only her fingers moved.

They passed again and again over the sleeve of Michael's hoodie.

"Do you believe he's still in that vehicle?" Sarah asked.

Daniels answered honestly.

"We can't confirm that."

"But he was still inside on the second recording at Midway Road."

"Yes."

"And at the entrance ramp?"

"The passenger seat is visible, but not clearly enough."

"Is someone sitting there?"

Another pause.

"It appears so."

Emma closed her hand around the sleeve.

Sarah leaned slightly toward her.

"And at the SR 70 exit?"

"The passenger seat appears occupied there as well."

Sarah held her breath.

"Appears?"

"The cameras were not installed to identify people inside moving vehicles."

Daniels' voice remained calm.

"I'm not going to tell you it's Michael when the recording cannot establish that reliably."

Sarah lowered her head.

"But the SUV continues west."

"Yes."

This time, he did not hesitate.

"The SUV is traveling west on SR 70."

Sarah looked toward the window.

Outside, a garbage truck moved slowly through the apartment complex.

A man pulled two empty bins beneath a carport.

The world continued moving as though a road had not just opened in front of them, carrying Michael farther and farther away from the city.

"What are we supposed to do now?" she asked.

"Remain reachable."

Daniels answered immediately.

Sarah pressed her lips together.

"We've been doing that since Friday."

"I know."

"Should we come somewhere? The station?"

"Not at the moment."

"Do you need another identification?"

"No."

"Then maybe a list of places Michael might know out there."

Daniels was silent for a moment.

"His professional connections are already being reviewed."

Sarah straightened.

"By whom?"

"By the investigators working with Chesapeake."

"Henry knows the customers better than any list does."

"We're speaking with him."

Emma lifted her head.

"And Frank?"

Sarah looked at her.

Daniels answered calmly.

"Everyone who may help us identify possible professional destinations."

Emma looked back at the table.

"Dad knows old radio sites."

Daniels said nothing.

She continued.

"And places where companies used to keep equipment."

Sarah placed a hand on her arm.

"Emma."

"It's true."

"Yes."

Sarah turned her attention back to the phone.

"Michael has worked with legacy systems, backups and communications equipment for years."

"We know that," Daniels said.

"Then there are probably dozens of places."

"Possibly more."

The answer settled heavily into the room.

Sarah thought of Henry's list.

Customers.

Former customers.

Vendors.

Technicians.

Buildings where Michael had once replaced a server, inspected a radio link or collected an old backup.

Places that meant nothing to everyone else.

But not to him.

"How are you supposed to search all of that?"

"Not all at once."

Daniels spoke calmly.

"First, we look for another point on the route. After that, the possibilities become narrower."

"And if there isn't another point?"

"Then we work with what we have."

Sarah stood again.

She could not remain seated.

"SR 70 goes a long way west."

"Yes."

"To Okeechobee."

"Among other places."

"And beyond it."

"Yes."

Sarah walked to the window.

Her reflection lay pale in the glass.

"Then the vehicle could be anywhere by now."

Daniels did not answer immediately.

"It has had enough time since Friday to travel a considerable distance."

Sarah closed her eyes.

She had not asked that question.

Not really.

But now the answer was there.

Emma stood as well.

"Maybe it didn't go very far."

Sarah turned toward her.

Emma pulled the sleeves of the hoodie over her hands.

"Maybe it turned off somewhere almost immediately."

Daniels said:

"That's possible."

"Or it stopped."

"Also possible."

"Or Dad got into another vehicle."

Sarah felt something tighten inside her.

"Emma."

"We don't know."

Her voice was quiet.

Not panicked.

Only factual.

Almost like Michael.

"All we know is that the Yukon turned onto SR 70."

Daniels was silent for a moment.

"That is correct."

Emma looked at her mother.

"Then we can't act like he went all the way to Okeechobee."

Sarah held her gaze.

She did not know whether Emma was trying to calm herself.

Or her mother.

Probably both.

"No," Sarah said.

"We can't."

Daniels' voice came through the phone again.

"Emma is right. At the moment, only the westbound direction is confirmed."

Sarah nodded even though he could not see her.

"How quickly can you review the next recordings?"

"As soon as we receive them."

"And when will that be?"

"Some systems can be accessed directly. Other recordings have to be requested, exported or secured in person."

"So hours."

"Possibly."

"Or longer."

Daniels did not answer.

Sarah understood that too.

"Call me as soon as you have anything."

"As soon as we have something reliable."

That word again.

Sarah looked at the phone.

At the name on the screen.

"Officer Daniels."

"Yes?"

"If you find a place."

She had to stop for a moment.

"If you learn where the Yukon stopped."

Emma stood completely still beside her.

"Tell me immediately."

"I will."

"Not after someone decides how much you're allowed to tell me."

Daniels' voice remained calm.

"I will tell you everything I can as soon as I can confirm it."

It was not the promise Sarah wanted.

But it was the only one he could give her.

"All right."

"Please remain in the apartment or at least reachable by phone."

Sarah looked at Emma.

"We're staying here."

"And do not drive west yourselves to look for the vehicle."

Sarah said nothing.

Emma looked at her immediately.

Daniels continued.

"The route is too large. You don't know exactly what you're looking for, and we don't know who is inside that vehicle."

"I wasn't planning to—"

Sarah stopped.

Because the thought had already been there.

Not as a plan.

Only as an image.

Sarah behind the wheel.

Emma beside her.

SR 70 in front of them.

Every white SUV a possible match.

"Don't," Daniels said.

This time, he did not sound like an officer issuing an instruction.

He sounded like someone who understood that desperation could set people in motion.

Sarah placed her free hand on the kitchen counter.

"We'll stay here."

"I'll contact you."

"Today?"

A brief pause.

"As soon as there is something."

Then the call ended.

Sarah still held the phone to her ear.

Emma waited.

The screen went dark.

Only Byte could be heard in the apartment.

The soft, steady turning of his wheel.

Sarah slowly lowered her hand.

"They lost him," Emma said.

Sarah looked at her.

"The SUV."

Emma looked at the bowl of cereal.

"After SR 70."

Sarah wanted to disagree.

To say that the police were still searching.

That lost was not the same as vanished.

But once again, they had only a point on a map.

And beyond it, a road continuing farther and farther west.

"For now," Sarah said.

Emma nodded.

"For now."

Sarah placed the phone on the table.

Neither of them sat down again.

In front of them was a breakfast that had gone cold.

Behind them, Byte continued running in circles.

And somewhere west of the city, the last confirmed trail ended.

---
Henry had already read the list three times.

It did not grow shorter on the fourth.

He sat in a small conference room at Chesapeake Signal Tech. Several printed spreadsheets, handwritten notes and a road map Daniels had spread across the table lay in front of him.

The air conditioning was too cold.

Henry barely noticed.

"Again," Daniels said.

He stood on the opposite side of the table and pointed to the westbound line of SR 70.

"What professional connections did Michael have in this direction?"

Henry removed his glasses and rubbed his eyes.

"How far west?"

"We don't know."

"Then the answer is too many."

Daniels waited.

Henry put his glasses back on.

"Chesapeake doesn't work only with companies inside the city. We've had customers outside the denser areas. Agricultural operations. Small workshops. Utilities. Private radio sites. Companies with remote facilities."

"Current customers?"

"Some."

Henry pulled a printout closer.

"Others aren't anymore."

"How far back do the records go?"

Henry looked at him.

"Which records?"

Daniels said nothing.

Henry exhaled slowly.

"That's exactly the problem."

He tapped the spreadsheet with one finger.

"The current customer list is orderly. Contracts, contacts, open work, recent service calls."

His finger moved to a second stack.

"The older material is less orderly."

"How much less?"

"The older it gets, the worse it gets."

Henry leaned back.

"Some companies no longer exist. Some were sold. Some changed their names. Others had one system installed fifteen years ago and we never went back."

"But Michael might remember them."

Henry gave a short laugh.

It was not a happy sound.

"Michael remembers a defective network card he removed from a computer in 2009."

Daniels looked at him.

Henry gestured toward the papers.

"He remembers sites that no longer appear on any current list."

"For example?"

Henry opened his mouth.

Closed it again.

Then looked down at the map.

"Old radio relays, for example."

Daniels picked up his pen.

"Where?"

"Not one specific place."

Henry raised a hand immediately.

"Don't write that down as though I've named a destination."

Daniels stopped.

"Then explain it."

Henry leaned over the map.

"Customers used to rely more often on their own radio links between buildings or remote locations. Storage yards. Pump stations. Workshops. Properties where a normal connection was unreliable or too expensive."

"And Chesapeake serviced those systems."

"Installed them, repaired them or eventually replaced them."

"Michael personally?"

"Often."

Henry studied the road stretching west across the map.

"He's been here long enough to have been almost everywhere at least once."

Daniels continued writing.

"Which of those sites are on or near SR 70?"

Henry pushed the first stack aside and took the next one.

"That's what I'm trying to determine."

Company names covered the pages.

Some he recognized immediately.

Others only after staring at them long enough.

With some, he remembered faces but not roads.

With others, buildings but no longer the people inside them.

Michael would have known.

Michael would have pointed to a name and told them which antenna stood on the roof, which server ran in the back room and which owner kept his passwords taped beneath the keyboard.

Henry looked at the empty place beside his chair.

Since Friday, part of him had still expected Michael to be sitting there.

Impatient.

A coffee mug in his hand.

Ready to correct every error in the lists.

"What about former customers?" Daniels asked.

Henry continued turning pages.

"That becomes harder."

"Why?"

"Because a former customer doesn't automatically become irrelevant."

He pulled another page from the stack.

"Some systems kept running after the contract ended. Some companies retained old equipment even though no one was being paid to support it anymore. And sometimes, years later, they called Michael directly because he was the only person who still knew how everything had been built."

Daniels looked up from his notes.

"Directly on his private phone?"

"Sometimes."

Henry stopped.

Michael's phone was now in police custody.

So was the Leatherman they had found in the old archive and backup room.

The ThinkPad T43 and Michael's running workstation had been forensically secured and imaged. Perhaps their data would eventually show who had called or written to Michael.

But they could not tell Henry which casual conversation he was failing to remember now.

"Or by email," he said. "Or they called here and asked for him."

"Are there records of that?"

"Not complete ones."

"Why not?"

Henry looked at him.

"Because people don't document every conversation they believe is unimportant."

Daniels accepted the answer.

Henry ran a finger across several names.

None of them triggered anything.

No white Yukon.

No driver.

No reason Michael would willingly sit in the passenger seat of a stranger's vehicle.

"Has Michael mentioned any of these old sites recently?" Daniels asked.

"Not to me."

"Anything about SR 70? Okeechobee? A customer outside the city?"

Henry thought.

Not only about the last few days.

Weeks.

Passing comments over coffee.

Complaints about old hardware.

Michael's habit of mentioning a technical location in the middle of a conversation without explaining why it had crossed his mind.

Nothing.

Or nothing Henry had considered important enough at the time to retain.

"No," he said at last.

The word felt like a failure.

Daniels pointed to another printout.

"What's this?"

Henry pulled the page toward him.

"Old service categories."

"Backup and archive."

"Yes."

"Communications systems."

"Yes."

"Remote sites."

Henry nodded.

Daniels placed the tip of his pen against the paper.

"Then we start there."

Henry looked at the long lists.

"That's still dozens."

"Dozens are fewer than an entire highway stretching into the interior."

Henry looked at the map again.

The line of SR 70 continued west beyond the edge of the city map.

Daniels was right.

Dozens were fewer than everything.

But Michael had been missing since Friday.

And every hour made even a shorter list too large.

Henry pulled the first stack toward him.

"All right."

He picked up a pen.

Not Michael's Timberline Lodge pen.

A cheap black pen from the conference room.

"Active sites first."

He made a mark beside the first name.

"Then former customers with old communications equipment."

A second mark.

"After that, everything Michael installed himself or later supported alone."

Daniels sat across from him.

"And if you remember a vehicle?"

Henry did not look up.

"You'll be the first to know."

He marked the next entry.

Then the next.

On the map, the road continued west.

On the paper, it slowly began to dissolve into names.

Henry made another mark.

Then stopped.

His pen hovered over an entry without touching the paper.

Daniels noticed immediately.

"What is it?"

Henry did not answer.

He read the line again.

Then the note beside it.

A former customer.

The contract had ended almost two years earlier.

Remote technical site.

Older radio link.

Multiple service visits Michael had handled personally.

Daniels leaned slightly over the table.

"You know the name?"

"Yes."

Henry set down the pen.

"And I probably wouldn't have mentioned him if you hadn't asked for everything west of the city."

"Why?"

Henry pulled the page closer.

"Because there was trouble."

Daniels picked up his pen again.

"What kind of trouble?"

"Technical problems. Repeatedly."

Henry flipped through the pages behind it as though an older note might appear among the printouts.

"The system was outdated. Parts of it should have been replaced years earlier. But the customer always wanted Michael to make it work one more time."

"Did Michael?"

"As long as he considered it responsible."

Henry looked at the entry.

"Eventually, he refused to keep patching it together."

"And the customer did not agree."

"Not at all."

Henry remembered more clearly now.

Not every word.

But the tone.

A conversation in the hallway loud enough to carry into his office.

Michael's voice, calm as always.

The other voice growing louder with every sentence.

"He blamed Michael," Henry said.

"For what?"

"For outages. Lost time. Probably everything that had been wrong at that site for years."

"Were there threats?"

Henry thought.

"None I can remember word for word."

Daniels held his gaze.

"That wasn't an answer."

"He was angry."

Henry moved a finger along the line.

"He said Michael was making it impossible for his business to operate. Michael told him a repair was not a substitute for modernization."

"When was that?"

"The last major argument? About two years ago."

"No contact since then?"

"Not officially."

"And unofficially?"

Henry slowly shook his head.

"I don't know."

Daniels wrote something down.

"Why did this customer stand out now?"

Henry looked back at the map.

The site was not directly on SR 70.

But close enough to reach from it.

"Because he drove a large white SUV."

Daniels' pen stopped.

"What kind?"

Henry briefly closed his eyes.

He saw the vehicle in the parking lot.

Large.

White.

Wide enough that Brenda regularly complained when it occupied two spaces.

"Not a Yukon," he said.

"I think it was a Ford Expedition."

"You think?"

"I'm a system administrator, not a car dealer."

Daniels did not let that distract him.

"White. Large SUV. Former customer with a conflict involving Michael. A site west of the city."

Henry looked at him.

"I'm not saying he had anything to do with this."

"I didn't say you were."

"The man had problems with Michael. That doesn't mean he abducted him."

"No."

Daniels placed his pen on the map.

"It means we check him."

Henry leaned back.

The memory suddenly seemed too small.

A white vehicle.

An argument two years earlier.

A system no one wanted to replace.

Maybe it was nothing.

It was probably nothing.

But since Friday, Henry had learned that meaningless things became meaningless only after someone checked them.

"The Expedition had damage," he said.

Daniels looked up.

"Where?"

Henry tried to hold on to the image.

"Rear right."

He moved his hand as though drawing the location in the air.

"Near the taillight. A dark scrape or a dent."

"Are you certain?"

"No."

Henry looked directly at him.

"But I remember Michael once saying the vehicle looked as though it had lost an argument with a concrete post."

Daniels did not write down the sentence.

Only the location of the possible damage.

"Do you have an address?"

Henry pointed to the entry.

"The old business address."

"A phone number?"

"Probably in the customer file."

Daniels took the printout and photographed the relevant entry with his department phone.

"I'll have the residential address checked."

Henry looked at the mark beside the name.

"And the Expedition."

"If it's still registered to the same owner."

Daniels stepped away from the table and began making a call.

Henry listened as he described the former customer, the old dispute and the large white Ford Expedition.

That was all they had at the moment.

No confirmation.

No alibi.

Only a name and a vehicle that might not have carried the same license plate in years.

When Daniels ended the call, he sat down again.

"A patrol unit is going by."

"How long will that take?"

"As long as it takes."

Henry pushed the printout aside.

He did not like the answer.

But it was honest.

"Then we continue."

They worked through more entries.

Active customers first.

Then former customers with old radio and communications systems.

Henry marked workshops, pump stations, storage yards and remote sites where Michael had worked personally.

Some still existed.

Others had been sold.

At two addresses, no one immediately knew whether a business remained there at all.

The coffee beside Henry's papers went cold.

Daniels left the room twice to take calls.

Neither concerned the white Expedition.

When he returned, Henry was reaching for the next stack.

Daniels' department phone vibrated again.

He looked at the screen and stopped.

"Daniels."

Henry set down his pen.

Daniels listened.

"He's home?"

A pause.

"He confirmed the connection to Chesapeake?"

Daniels opened his notebook.

"And Michael Weber?"

Henry watched his face.

No visible surprise.

Only concentration.

"Does he have an explanation for Friday?"

Daniels wrote several lines.

"Good. Get the names and exact times."

Another pause.

"And the vehicle?"

Henry sat straighter.

Daniels glanced briefly at him.

"Which repair shop?"

He wrote down an address.

"How long does he say it's been there?"

Another pause.

"Verify it independently. Vehicle identification number, intake time and current repair condition."

Daniels ended the call.

Henry waited.

"The patrol unit found him," Daniels said.

"At home."

"And?"

"He recognized Chesapeake Signal Tech immediately. Michael too."

"The argument as well?"

"He doesn't deny it."

Daniels sat again.

"He says Michael refused to make another temporary repair to the old system. He says he became angry at the time."

Henry looked at the name.

"When did he last see Michael?"

"According to him, not in person for almost two years."

"And Friday?"

"He provided an alibi."

"What kind?"

"A medical appointment that afternoon. Afterward, he says he was at his sister's house."

Henry folded his arms.

"Says."

"The information is being verified."

"And the Expedition?"

Daniels looked at his notes.

"Supposedly at a repair shop since Thursday."

"Supposedly."

"Transmission failure."

Henry looked at the map.

Nothing had been ruled out yet.

The owner could be lying.

The shop could belong to him.

The vehicle could have been brought in later.

Daniels did not return the page to the normal stack.

He placed it separately beside his phone.

"We wait for confirmation."

The confirmation came later.

By then, Henry had marked several more locations and requested two old customer files from the archive.

Daniels stood at the conference-room window when his phone rang again.

He listened longer than before.

"The vehicle identification number matches?"

Henry set down the pen.

"It's been there since Thursday morning?"

Daniels stopped writing.

"The transmission has been removed?"

A pause.

"Photograph the vehicle and the intake paperwork. Confirm the timeline separately with the shop manager and the mechanic."

He ended the call.

"The Expedition is in the repair shop," he said.

Henry looked at him.

"Since Thursday morning."

"Could it have been driven?"

"The transmission has already been partially removed. The vehicle is not operational."

Henry leaned back.

"And the alibi?"

"The appointment has been confirmed. The remaining details are still being fully checked, but the known times fit."

Daniels picked up the page he had kept separate.

He made a small mark beside the name.

Not crossed out.

But significantly downgraded.

"Then it wasn't his vehicle," Henry said.

"No."

For a while, a white SUV had belonged to a name.

To a man with an old conflict.

To a site west of the city.

Now that SUV stood motionless inside a repair shop.

A false lead.

But a checked one.

Henry pulled the next printout toward him.

"Continue."

Daniels sat across from him.

Henry read the next entry.

This time, he remembered no vehicle.

Only an old radio tower, a remote pump station and Michael once saying that even the cellular signal was unreliable out there.

---
In the early afternoon, Daniels was back at his desk.

The printouts from Chesapeake lay in a folder beside him.

Customers.

Remote sites.

Old radio systems.

A white Ford Expedition that had turned out to be a false lead.

A preliminary report from digital forensics waited on his screen.

Daniels opened it.

The first section concerned Michael's workstation.

The computer had not been shut down.

Not restarted.

Not logged out.

Before the forensic team changed anything, they had preserved the complete state of the system.

Memory.

Drives.

System logs.

Connected devices.

Active processes.

The visible console had remained unchanged afterward.

Still the same line.

Still the same blinking cursor.

Daniels continued reading.

The local login at 1:41:26 PM had been confirmed.

The account had been used at the physical console.

The entry proved nothing more.

It did not prove who had been sitting in front of the keyboard.

It did not prove who had entered the password.

Inventory label.

Ports.

The examination was being conducted only on a working copy.

The case showed clear signs of wear.

**Should have.**

Deleted files.

Old user profiles.

System logs.

Timestamps whose reliability first had to be established.

The T43 was not an empty old laptop.

But that did not mean anything on it was connected to Michael's disappearance.

A device could contain years of data without anyone having powered it on for years.

The forensic team had found several user profiles on the drive.

Not even this year.

Not reliably preserved in the available log.

Unknown.

Correct.

Correct.

GOG Galaxy Is Coming to Linux and Wing Commander: Prophecy Is Back

GOG plans to bring Galaxy to Linux in 2027. Here’s how to play GOG games now with Heroic or offline installers, plus a Steam Deck tip for the restored cutscenes in Wing Commander: Prophecy.

When I tried the Epic Games Launcher on Cthulhu, I got as far as the castle in Castlevania and stayed there. A few notes were enough to bring back the NES theme I remembered. The cockpit in Wing Commander: Prophecy makes me want to sit down and see what still feels familiar. My Jane’s Longbow Pentium III belongs to that era. This time I’m looking at how much of a late-’90s PC game is within reach on a Linux machine today.

Status as of 16 November 2026: GOG is planning GOG Galaxy for Linux in 2027. Wing Commander: Prophecy – 30th Anniversary Edition came out on 5 November and officially supports Windows. The developers’ FAQ now has a Steam Deck launch option for the restored cutscenes. I still have to test the Anniversary Edition on Cthulhu.

When is GOG Galaxy coming to Linux?

GOG announced the Linux client at its first showcase on 5 November 2026. Its anniversary page lists the next version of Galaxy under 2027 and offers a waitlist for updates. The announcement gives no specific release date, supported distribution list or SteamOS details. GOG has yet to offer a Linux client download through the announcement.

Galaxy is getting a redesign on Windows and macOS too. GOG is promising a faster interface and more ways to organise the library. For my older games, its commitment to offline installers is at least as interesting. Galaxy stays optional. I can download a game through my browser, keep the setup files and dig them out again years later. That feels about right for something that first came in a big box.

How can you install GOG games on Linux today?

Start with the supported operating systems on the GOG store page. If a title has a Linux build, look for its Linux installer in your library. If it lists Windows, as Prophecy does, the Windows build needs a compatibility layer such as Wine to run on Linux. A launcher manages the games and starts them. In-game rendering, video playback and input each need to work for the game to be playable.

Your gameWhere to start
GOG title with a Linux versionChoose the Linux installer from your GOG library or install the native Linux build in Heroic if it is offered.
Windows game from GOGSign in to GOG in Heroic and start the Windows release with a Wine runner. You can also run the GOG offline installer in a separate Wine prefix.
Windows game from SteamInstall the Steam release through Steam. Use Proton for the Windows build on Linux or Steam Deck.

Here’s how I’d start with the GOG release on Cthulhu. I’d check Heroic for the Anniversary Edition and Legacy Edition and start with Anniversary. If I went with the offline installer, I’d download every part of the setup from my GOG library and keep them together. Giving the game its own Wine prefix would let me retry with a different runner or settings without touching another game’s environment.

Before switching Wine versions, I’d back up the save and note which version I had been using. Start with one runner and the default graphics settings. A black cutscene after the game has started and a launcher that crashes before the menu are two different symptoms. That’s why I’d check the video option before swapping runners. My guide to Wine prefixes for Linux gaming shows how the pieces fit together.

What’s in Wing Commander: Prophecy – 30th Anniversary Edition?

Origin Systems released the original in 1997. The new edition includes the original campaign and the Secret Ops episodes. GOG restored it with EA and contributors from the modding community. GOG lists an updated OpenGL renderer and gamepad support, and EA describes how the community’s DVD and OpenGL work found its way into the release.

The cutscenes are the part I keep coming back to. EA found master tapes in its own archive, digitised them and edited the scenes back into the game. The launcher offers three ways to watch them. Original gives you the videos from the original release, Master Source uses the restored tape footage, and Master Upscaled adds optional AI upscaling. The same footage the actors originally shot is still there, and you can still see how it looked in the game at the time.

On GOG, the bundle includes both the Anniversary and Legacy editions. If you already own the earlier GOG release, GOG says the Anniversary Edition will be added to your library at no extra cost. There are two places you can encounter the word “Original”. The original game build is a separate entry in your GOG library. The original cutscenes are a setting inside the Anniversary Edition’s launcher.

Wing Commander: Prophecy on Steam Deck – how do you fix black cutscenes?

In their 5 November FAQ, the developers said the missions in the Enhanced edition should be playable on Steam Deck. The updated FAQ provides a launch option for the restored videos. On Deck, open the game’s gear icon → Properties → General → Launch Options and enter this line.

WINEDLLOVERRIDES="ddraw.dll=n,b;dinput.dll=n,b" %command%

This tells Wine to prefer the native DirectDraw and DirectInput DLLs and fall back to its built-in versions. Launch Enhanced in Steam, select Master Source or Master Upscaled in the game’s launcher, then check the intro and first briefing with sound. If the videos still fail, remove the launch option and select Original in the launcher. That was the developers’ initial recommendation at release. After flying a mission, save manually at the next terminal on the TCS Midway. The FAQ says the game has no autosave.

The installation’s Extras folder contains a controller setup PDF with a section on Steam Deck. The FAQ also flags places where you may need the on-screen keyboard, such as entering a player name or saving a game. These details concern the Steam release on Deck. How the GOG release behaves with Heroic and a specific runner on my Arch desktop remains open.

The 6 November Steam hotfix addressed subtitles and an instant failure in Hrissith Mission 5. The Deck video launch option is documented separately in the FAQ.

What stayed with me after the GOG Showcase

For me, the more interesting story is on those old tapes. A few people in the community had figured out years ago how to get better video and a modern renderer into the game. Now that work is part of a regular release and the old version is still available. It’s a balance I recognise from my story about the big green Jane’s Longbow box. Sometimes I want to sit at the old PC. Sometimes I just want to get back into the cockpit on my current one. I can keep a copy of GOG’s installation files today. I’m still looking forward to Galaxy for Linux.

My builds – and your own paladin

Dual spec: Protection stays, experiments with Holy and Reckoning

On November 26, after buying dual spec, I tried Retribution and then Holy. I liked Retribution barely more than I did back in The Burning Crusade. The Holy preset (31/5/15), including Holy Shock and Light’s Vigil, remains as a record of that stage.

Later that day I experimented with Reckoning. In my tests, four stored extra attacks survived swapping from a one-handed weapon and shield to a two-hander, logging out and in, mounting and dismounting, and taking a flight path. Changing specialization removed the stacks. My latest experimental level-60 target was 0/26/25. These are personal observations, not a guarantee for every situation or PvP. Protection remains my main spec (planned 0/35/16).

Level 43 – From AoE paladin to yeti skinner, at my own pace

Why Protection really clicked for me after level 40

Since level 40, I have felt the talents and my own mount coming together. I am now at level 43. The strong self-healing and emergency abilities are exactly what I enjoy about this character. On my hunter in Classic Era, I could defeat enemies well above my level too, but sometimes had to kite them. Now my paladin can take on six melee mobs at once, even when they are one to three levels above me. Casters that cannot be gathered together, frequent stuns and poisons are trickier. My favourite targets are melee mobs that do not even run away. :D

I know many corners and tricks from Vanilla and Classic Era, but experiencing them as a Horde paladin is new to me. Horde paladins were already available in The Burning Crusade, of course, but the old world was no longer really the focus then.

From herbalist to professional yeti skinner

This morning I dropped Herbalism at skill 1 and learned Skinning instead. When several players rush towards the same flower from different directions, I fight the mob and someone else skips away with the herb, it is simply not my favourite pastime. I already have the professions distributed among my various Classic Era characters anyway.

Skinning had already reached 256/300 this morning, while Alchemy stays at 225 for now. AoE farming the yetis now gives me something extra to collect from each pull. Later I want to solo farm elite dragons in Dustwallow Marsh, the Sunken Temple and the cave in Winterspring again. Whether I replace Alchemy with Mining or another profession after getting my fast level-60 mount will depend on what I learn about the profession changes.

Feralas, a missed sandstorm and familiar places

Yesterday was my first visit to Feralas in Forever. The old feeling returned immediately: I know the area and its caves, but I look forward to seeing these places in the updated graphics. In Classic Era I farmed the high-level elite chimaeras on my hunter. With the right talents and pet resistances it was possible, although challenging, and very profitable. I am also curious to see the new look of Dire Maul.

There was a very brief sandstorm in Gadgetzan. I was so delighted that I completely forgot to take a screenshot. :D The atmosphere that has stayed with me most so far is the nighttime lighting and fog in Tirisfal and Brill, as well as the thunderstorm in Grom'gol. It feels to me as though it rains a little less than two weeks ago, although that is a personal impression rather than a confirmed change.

What I like about Forever – and what I still want to discover

To me, Forever feels like a game into which people who love Classic have put a lot of care and work. Some elements, such as the outpost near the Crossroads, feel a bit like Retail, though I have not explored that particular content in detail. I find the new dungeons beautifully designed and surprisingly demanding. A familiar BRD or UBRS run feels more relaxed to me than Krol'Dok or the Sunken City. That is probably also because I have played the old dungeons countless times and know exactly where a knockback in UBRS can send me into the whelp eggs.

I am looking forward to Silithus, the Plaguelands and Tyr's Hand, to Onyxia and Zul'Gurub, and especially to my first view inside Blackrock Mountain. Less frequently shown corners, such as the troll village in Winterspring and my beloved Alterac Valley, are on the list too. Regardless of loot, I am particularly excited about Zul'Farrak and its potential for paladin AoE farming. Eventually I want to farm the Scarlet Monastery Cathedral and Zul'Farrak solo and improve my gold reserves along the way.

A new gear goal: books instead of drop luck

Since the shield I want from the monastery just will not drop, I am now setting out to collect the library books. My next target is Wappen der Aufklärung, followed by Antikes Bollwerk (German in-game item names). These are goals, not items I have already obtained. The Wowhead library guide helps me find the books.

The in-game rewards panel lists 25 books for the crest and 35 for the bulwark. The crest requires level 40; the bulwark tooltip shows no level requirement. Whether it could be claimed at level 43 was still open this morning; the answer is in the travel diary below. Its 1,994 armour, 36 block value, +13 Stamina and +8 Defense are especially appealing compared with my current shield’s 738 armour and 16 block value. A Thorium Shield Spike would also be worth considering.

Level 60? I will get there – at my own pace

Ostfriesen Raid will deliberately remain my personal guild, as in Classic Era. I would still like to build a reputation so other players invite me along to Onyxia, AQ20, AQ40, Zul'Gurub and BWL. Outside that, I love solo content, farming old dungeons and, of course, PvP.

The anticipation reminds me of the early days, even after all my time in Classic Era. What I especially enjoy compared with Retail is that it hardly matters whether I play today or wait until next weekend. Other players may already be level 60 by then. I will get there too, and I no longer mind if my first tier set piece or a PvP rank comes a month later. On Saturday, for example, I want to spend some time on nerd projects outside WoW, and might only play a little in the evening. I am enjoying the visuals, the world and the journey. WoW should remain a hobby, not a schedule.

Operation Bookworm – the Ostfriesen Raid company outing

All 24 new original screenshots from this journey are also available in the screenshot gallery. Open the pictures in the diary at full resolution.

This morning's little idea turned into a tour of Azeroth. At level 43 I collected 35 of the 38 books my library addon currently lists, all in one day. I really enjoy this quest, even with the long distances. As a Horde player I rarely have a reason to visit so many Alliance areas, and along the way I unlocked numerous flight paths for future dungeons and farming trips.

Felwood, Winterspring and memories of my hunter

In Felwood I loved the poisonous green water. Seeing the ancient trees immediately brought back memories of my hunter class quest in Vanilla and Classic Era, and how proud I was of Rhok'delar and Lok'delar! In Timbermaw Hold, one book was right next to a skull-level furbolg with 4,312 health. Divine Shield did not let me collect the book, so I fought the Timbermaw mob solo. Towards the end I had just 12 mana left, but my self-healing kept me alive. Book number six was mine, and I reached Winterspring alive.

Like putting on new glasses: water and familiar roads

In Darkshore I particularly noticed how alive the water looks: little eddies and visible flow patterns around rocks and narrow passages. To me the water used to just sit there; now it looks as though it really flows. I cannot tell from my observations what technology produces the effect. I stood here more than 20 years ago and found it beautiful then. Now it feels like I have been given a pair of glasses. :D

Smaller memories away from the major landmarks returned too. In Classic Era I once stood among the Darkshore moonkins in druid form until a low-level Alliance player mistook me for a mob and attacked. At the Ruins of Mathystra I tamed my translucent Ghost Saber and was often asked where I had found it. These are places I would probably skip on a normal Horde leveling route.

From Kalimdor to the Eastern Kingdoms

After 14 books on Kalimdor I moved on. The Tanaris pirates were easy to fight in groups, but I still prefer the yetis for experience farming: extra leather and no fleeing mobs. My route took me past Booty Bay and through the Blasted Lands, Duskwood, Westfall and Elwynn. I enjoyed the greenish lighting in the Raven Hill catacombs and even spotted a little fox against Duskwood's blue fog.

At Sentinel Hill in Westfall I had to fight several Alliance guards while low-level players watched. Later I encountered a PvP-flagged level-12 hunter who used /cry; I simply rode on. I was here for the books, after all. Amusingly, his guild was called Thalia, also the name of a German bookshop chain.

The Plaguelands: reading is supposed to be relaxing

The Plaguelands were more demanding. At Felstone Field I initially died, but resurrected inside the building and defeated a single level-50 ghoul. The book was safe. Instead of dying on the way out as planned, I pulled mobs into the inn, jumped down and escaped using Divine Shield and Blessing of Freedom. I also unlocked the flight path at Light's Hope Chapel. My familiarity with the areas helps enormously: I know safe routes, graveyards and spots with no mobs.

My journey continued through Silverpine, Alterac, the Hinterlands, Arathi, Wetlands, Loch Modan and the Badlands. Mo'grosh Stronghold reminded me of my old WoW joke: once you know ten caves, you know them all – at least their floor plans. As a Horde player, I used to visit Wetlands mainly for the Onyxia quest chain or the red dragon whelps. The library quest and new content now give me another reason to look around.

Blackrock Mountain: the grand finale

With 33 books I headed to Blackrock Mountain. I had already picked up a flight path there on foot at level 36, but this time I had a mount. I reached the bridge without dying. The first view inside was exactly what I had been waiting for: enormous chains, dragons, red-lit rocks and a bubbling lava lake with visible liquid effects. My old Classic Era guild was called “Molten Core Swim Team”, incidentally. How fitting! :D I remembered the tomb on the central rock from the old key quest, and finally found the last book on a platform outside Blackrock Depths. 35 collected! Naturally my hearthstone was set to Undercity.

35 books handed in – the bulwark will have to wait until level 50

At Owen Thadd's, the sheer number of quests even overflowed the dialogue window. I particularly liked seeing the previously empty bookshelf visibly fill up with my donations. For the 25-book reward I chose the bow rather than the crest for its higher vendor value, because I expected to receive the bulwark.

Then came the surprise: According to the quest data, the final reward quest for Antikes Bollwerk requires level 50, even though the item tooltip itself shows no level requirement. I could not claim the shield at level 43. The 35 books have been collected and handed in; the reward is still pending. I hope to be able to claim it directly at level 50. Eventually I will collect the remaining three books too.

Do I regret the trip? Not at all. I came back with new flight paths, beautiful landscapes and a pile of memories. The three intermediate rewards even covered my repair bills. The flight paths in Winterspring, the Hinterlands and at Light's Hope Chapel are particularly useful to me. The journey was part of the reward. My old round shield stays in service for another seven levels before I add four legs and sell it as a coffee table with a history. The murloc tooth is part of the original equipment. :D

Another idea for my secondary build

These fights also inspired a potential 21/30/0 Holy-Protection hybrid. For long solo fights I like the idea of Holy Shock for instant self-healing, Divine Favor for a critical heal, and protection against spell pushback through Spiritual Focus and Concentration Aura. Several fast melee enemies make cast-time healing particularly difficult. This is a future experiment, not an implemented build; Protection remains my main spec. I prefer building for a specific purpose rather than blindly following the meta. In Era, for example, my rogue had demon-slaying enchants on both weapons when farming demons and Felcloth in Azshara.

Level 40 – My First Mount, Two Talent Builds and a New Chapter of the Journey

Level 40! My first mount is here, and the paladin is starting to feel like the character I wanted to play. After more than 20 years of WoW, these milestones still get me. Dennis Hilk has come a fair way since the undead paladin with a round shield who started this diary at level 14. :D

Arena quest complete, Ogre Horn Ring collected

The arena quest is done. Two of us defeated the final arena opponent, and I received the Ogre Horn Ring. Together with the first mount, that feels like a proper milestone. Something to remember beyond another number going up.

Why I like “of the Eagle” gear while leveling

Tanking and AoE farming have made me appreciate a balance of Stamina and Intellect. My personal recommendation at this stage is therefore “of the Eagle” gear. A bigger health bar alone does little for me if two Consecrations leave me out of mana. I want to survive the hits and keep using my abilities.

That is my experience with this paladin, rather than a universal best-in-slot list. Armour and the other stats still need to suit the job. I am deliberately building out Protection first and adding Retribution later. The complete calculator presets show my level-60 goal; at level 40 I only have the normal 31 talent points.

Today’s world update and my continuing frustration with disappearing players and mobs are covered in the updated layering section, including the original notification. I am enjoying this paladin, but that still bothers me.

25 November 2026: A world update warning – and layering still bothers me

Even at level 40, the issue has not gone away. Today the game displayed a warning that the world around me would update in ten minutes, asking players to move out of combat to a safe area or click to update immediately.

“The world around you will be updated in 10 minutes. Make sure you are out of combat and in a safe area, or click here to update immediately.”

English translation of the German in-game message captured in my own screenshot on 25 November 2026.

A warning about a scheduled update is at least understandable. What still bothers me are the abrupt disappearances during ordinary gameplay: A player walks towards me, I target them – and suddenly they vanish. The same happens with mobs: I select an enemy and run towards it, only for it to disappear just before I arrive, or for me to seemingly end up in a different world layer. It makes encounters feel unreliable and breaks immersion. I would much rather see stable layering, especially while targeting, approaching enemies or fighting. I cannot verify whether these incidents are technically caused by the same process as the scheduled world update; these are my observations as a player.

27 November 2026: Am I on the right language realm?

At level 43, just before my fourth Cathedral run, I notice something again: although I play on the German-language Normal PvE realm, most of the character and guild names around me are written in Cyrillic. We can still make ourselves understood with a little English and some improvisation, of course – that is not a problem for me.

As a new player, however, the first thing I would do is check my settings. Did I select the right realm? Is my language setting correct? Someone unfamiliar with WoW Forever and its layering might easily become confused. I would therefore appreciate more transparent grouping of language communities and greater continuity in chance encounters. This is not criticism of the other players, but of the lack of transparency in how the world is divided. I cannot tell from this observation whether I was actually assigned to a Russian-speaking layer or whether the grouping was coincidental.

After the Graveyard, the Library is another chapter completed in my journey as a tank through Azeroth. :D Watch my first Library tank run on YouTube.

One star off: languages, invisible players and disappearing mobs

As much as I have grown to love WoW Forever's graphics, one aspect has now cost it one star in my personal assessment. I selected German as my preferred language, yet I still keep encountering Cyrillic writing in the game. I can understand English, but not everyone can – and I would like a selected language preference to make a more noticeable difference.

The open world can feel odd too: some players are online according to /who, but I cannot see them around me. At the same time, I keep meeting newcomers, and familiar Classic Era-style questions have already returned: “Hey Dennis, could you tank for us?” The sense of community is there; sometimes the world just feels strangely divided.

I now recognise what looks like layering even in the middle of combat. Several times I have spotted a mob, targeted it and started running over – only for it to vanish just before I arrived. Stranger still: I am already fighting a mob when an Alliance player apparently appears from another layer and attacks it too. The mob turns grey for me, then red again – and the Alliance player disappears just as suddenly. Having played WoW for so many years, I recognise the visual pattern and am convinced this is layering. I cannot see exactly which server-side rule is responsible, of course. But watching things switch back and forth during combat is confusing and breaks my sense of being in one shared world. Hopefully it becomes less common as the initial crowds settle down.

On the other hand, tanking Scarlet Monastery Library at level 34 was great fun again – and for the first time my paladin had Windfury during the run. That makes me look forward to the higher levels even more! I'm still enthusiastic overall, but I'm keeping an eye on these early rough edges. ❤️

What I really miss: When your name mattered more than your Gearscore

Perhaps this is what I miss most as someone who has played WoW since the very beginning: Back then, people knew one another.

On Destromath, and later on Noggenfogger in Classic Era, friendships and familiar faces lasted well beyond a single dungeon. You might venture into Ragefire Chasm with four other players, meet some of them again weeks later in Wailing Caverns, and eventually find yourselves together in Blackrock Depths or at Onyxia. Sometimes a simple “Moin” even grew into a friendship that still exists outside World of Warcraft today.

And I didn't even need a proper guild for that.

My Molten Core Swim Team guild on Noggenfogger consists entirely of my own characters. People still invited me to tank on my druid or warrior, or took my healer Desponia along. I didn't have to show anyone my Gearscore, a collection of points or a long list of achievements first.

People knew me. They knew I could tank, that I knew the dungeons, or that Desponia could heal them through Naxxramas. My name was my recommendation.

That's what I increasingly lost in Retail with its anonymous Dungeon Finder. Press a button, join a random group, rush through, collect loot and leave. Sometimes not a “Moin”, a “Thanks” or a “Bye”. Above all, no “Hey Dennis, weren't we adventuring together the other day?”

In WoW Forever I've founded my Ostfriesen Raid guild, although for now it only contains my bank character. I enjoy meeting new players, and the enthusiasm of people discovering Azeroth for the first time is wonderful. But I still miss the familiarity that comes from a long-established realm community.

I had already feared this might happen when Forever was announced. And, to some extent, it has.

Maybe that will change. Forever is still young, the initial rush will eventually ease, and perhaps friendships will grow here too, across many shared adventures.

I don't want a closed-off world for veterans. I want a world where strangers become familiar faces, and familiar faces eventually become friends.

Because visually I think WoW Forever is magnificent, I like the new quests, and I still have enormous fun in its dungeons.

But after all these years, my wish is actually very simple:

I would love Classic Era with WoW Forever's graphics.

Perhaps that sums up my personal verdict on WoW:

WoW Forever delights my eyes. Classic Era still holds a piece of my heart. ❤️

15.11.2026Level 28: solo DeadminesStranglethorn thunderstorms, Westfall and a memorable solo run.5 min read+

15 November 2026: Stranglethorn thunderstorms, Westfall and a level-28 solo Deadmines run

I only wanted to do some more levelling, take a few pictures along the way and eventually pick up a legacy point through a dungeon challenge. Instead, I ended up exploring three completely different sides of Azeroth – and fighting a boss battle I won't forget any time soon. :D

“Bro, rain again?” – and then I noticed the waves

When my zeppelin arrived in Stranglethorn, my first thought was honestly: “Bro, rain again?” Then I realised it wasn't just raining. It was a proper thunderstorm. I stood by the water, watching the rain, the mist and especially those waves and reflections. My quest log suddenly stopped mattering. Alt+Y, hide the UI, take a screenshot, show the UI again – almost like switching between old and remastered graphics in Monkey Island. :DDD

Westfall was the complete opposite: warm golden morning light, mist over the shoreline, calm water and that lonely lighthouse. Two new original screenshots from Stranglethorn and Westfall are now part of the gallery above, ready to view and save at full resolution.

The Deadmines solo – grey enemies, real trouble

At level 28 I took my undead Horde paladin into the Deadmines alone. I didn't want to interfere with lower-level players' experience gains by joining their group. The “Novice: Dungeon Explorer” legacy challenge still needed the Deadmines and the new Wetlands Excavation Site. Most of the mobs were already grey, but that didn't mean a solo run was safe.

Sneed and his shredder went down, followed by Gilnid in the foundry. Things got properly unpleasant on the pirate ship: endless packs, Mr. Smite and his stuns and adds, and Captain Greenskin with four guards, two of them elite. Not exactly a relaxed stroll! Whenever an enemy tried to flee, I made sure to stop it before it brought the whole instance running after me. :D

Alchemy 225, two attempts and a memorable finale

Having Alchemy at 225 proved incredibly useful. Mana potions, big healing potions and ways to briefly control enemies saved me more than once. Lay on Hands was still on cooldown for my first attempt against Edwin VanCleef. With two elite adds joining him, I ended up at the spirit healer.

On the second attempt, VanCleef wasn't any kinder: he called in more reinforcements, more than once. After 2 minutes and 27.48 seconds, VanCleef was finally dead – and so was I. A genuine double KO on the ship's deck! OBS captured the fight for YouTube. The boss frame said “Dead”, and most importantly, the Deadmines got their green check mark in the legacy challenge. :DDDD

That's five out of six dungeons complete; my legacy score stays at 3 out of 65 for now. The extra point will have to wait until I finish the excavation dungeon. There was also some BoE loot for my bank alt Baron Gorthak. And after all that inventory Tetris, I finally replaced my four six-slot bags with four 12-slot bags: 48 slots instead of 24. Room for loot and potions at last!

A changing opinion: Forever is more than “Classic Era Redux”

At first I liked to call Forever “Classic Era with a Redux graphics mod”. Some colours still seem a little too saturated, and the lighting can feel overdone. But the longer I play, the more often I stop just to look around. First the forest sunlight, then the underwater ruins, the Stranglethorn thunderstorm and waves, the golden Westfall morning and now the eerie turquoise glow inside the Deadmines.

I thought I knew this world after almost twenty years. Forever keeps making me stop at places I would once have run straight past. My old Redux comparison is starting to sell the atmosphere short. And I'm only level 28 – I can't wait to see Blackrock, Stratholme, Un'Goro and Silithus. ❤️

Stranglethorn side by side: WoW Forever versus WoW Classic

Katharina wanted to see what I meant about the new graphics. So I photographed the Stranglethorn coastline from a similar angle in both versions. In Forever, the sea and waves have noticeably more texture and the atmosphere feels denser. Classic shows the same place with its familiar, softer look – and a beautiful sunset. The weather and position of the sun are not identical, so this is not a controlled graphics benchmark. Even so, seeing the two together explains why I'm increasingly moving away from my original “Classic Era Redux” verdict. ❤️

Forever vs Vanilla and Classic Era: familiar Azeroth, very different light

A walk through the forest at sunset captures the visual changes perfectly. Vanilla's simple textures, fog and fairly even lighting gave it an unmistakable atmosphere, but the scenery could look comparatively flat. Classic Era preserves much of that original look. Forever retains the familiar shapes and Warcraft art style while transforming the way light feels: golden sunlight, richer atmosphere and stronger contrasts make an old forest feel newly staged.

It's more than a colour filter. Blizzard has described a custom raytracer for global illumination that replaces Vanilla's baked ambient lighting. Sun position and shadows matter more, with volumetric fog and reworked water adding to the scene. One important distinction: this custom lighting system isn't necessarily the same as the separate DXR-style Ray Traced Shadows graphics toggle, which was temporarily removed during beta. New ambient lighting does not automatically mean that a separate hardware ray-tracing setting is enabled.

I love the water and sunsets in particular, even though some colours and highlights feel a little too intense for my taste. My shorthand remains Classic Era with a Redux-like visual makeover: lovely, occasionally over the top, but recognisably Azeroth. Most importantly, after 27½ levels, I still want to keep playing.

WoW Forever after 22 levels: Classic Era with a Redux filter?

Update: 11 November 2026. Yesterday I set up a Debian server to keep track of my undead paladin. Today Dennis Hilk is level 22, has Consecration, has tanked four dungeons and belongs to a guild called M O I N. I'm from East Frisia. The guild recruitment interview was effectively over after “Moin.”

The old world with fresh paint — perhaps a little too much

WoW Forever feels astonishingly close to Vanilla, and there's an incredible amount happening in the world. It reminds me of “WoW Classic Remastered” — or Classic Era with one of those GTA V Redux-style graphics filters. The water looks excellent. It also rains rather often, although living in Wiesmoor, East Frisia, I probably shouldn't complain about rain. Sometimes the colours are a bit too saturated for me, so I adjust them myself.

There are new quests and stories worth discovering. Seeing the new interpretation of Dalaran near Silverpine particularly fascinates me: in Vanilla and Classic Era, I used to wonder what it would look like. Not every addition wins me over, though. The enormous merchant camp beside the Crossroads — Wegekreuz, thank you very much! — is too much for my taste. And why is everyone setting up tents around campfires? I miss the special feeling of finding the last five people still awake by a campfire at five in the morning, chatting nonsense. Now it can feel more like grabbing a buff and leaving.

The Retail-style interface is another adjustment. I've arranged it so I can work with it, and learning a fresh paladin makes that easier. In Classic Era, when changes broke half my addons, I couldn't face setting up things like Clique on my priest all over again. On Forever I also don't yet bump into the same familiar player names as often as I did on Noggenfogger. Perhaps that will change after the initial rush.

Four dungeons as a tank: Ragefire, Undercity, Wailing Caverns and Shadowfang Keep

I've already tanked Ragefire Chasm, the new Undercity dungeon, Wailing Caverns and Shadowfang Keep. Every run went smoothly. I know the old routes by heart, even the twists of Wailing Caverns. The Undercity dungeon looks beautiful, but after just one run I found it pretty confusing. Then again, new players must wonder what on earth is happening when they first enter Razorfen Kraul or Wailing Caverns.

In Wailing Caverns someone needed an escort quest. “We'll clear the route first, then do that.” Somebody else asked where the fellow with the port wine was. “Outside the instance portal. I'll show you on the way out.” “Super, thanks!” “np.” Small conversations like that are part of what I love about these games.

In Shadowfang Keep one player said they were completely new. I told everyone not to worry: I'd pull the enemies back to us. So I worked with the corners and line of sight, brought casters into the group and delivered manageable packs. The damage dealers waited for the pull. We flew through the castle; I forgot to record it; and I came home with the Baron's Scepter as my new main-hand weapon. Arugal has been standing around in that castle for twenty years. He can wait.

Years of tanking as Bearforceone the druid and Arganosh the warrior still help. I watch enemy positioning and the healer's mana. A healer in Wailing Caverns even said, “Dennis, you don't have to keep looking at my mana. I'll tell you.” Fair enough. Old habit: I always keep the healer as a focus target. Having healed extensively myself on Desponia, I know both sides of the group.

And I genuinely enjoy tanking as a paladin. This was the missing Vanilla class for a Horde player; I only played one once The Burning Crusade arrived. Consecration, Retribution Aura, shield spikes, a cloak that damages attackers — the plan is to make every mob regret hitting me. Best of all, we can taunt. That's worth gold. Levels 1 to 20 on a warrior used to be miserable; on this paladin they were pleasant. There's also no shortage of undead to blast.

Relaxed groups, newcomers welcome — and instant tank invitations

I set the group finder to “Entspannt” (relaxed), welcome newcomers and let people know I can lead the run. That combination gets my tank invited almost instantly. The tool makes it easier to meet people, but it doesn't automatically teleport me into a randomly assembled dungeon party like Retail. I like that distinction.

So far every group has been friendly, patient and curious. Many players openly say this is their first time playing WoW at all, not simply their first time outside Retail. At the end people say, “Thanks, have fun!” or “See you next time!” In Retail I was used to the opposite: queue, rush, next queue. Here we're actually playing together. A good group isn't necessarily the fastest; it's the one where everyone can have fun and the newcomers get to learn.

Difficulty, music and being poor again

At level 22 the difficulty feels quite close to Classic Era — not Retail. A couple of fights have been tight, but I'm not going to pronounce judgment before I've tackled things like Onyxia, Zul'Gurub, Upper Blackrock Spire and Ahn'Qiraj. I do not want achievements and glittering rewards just for walking a few steps, though.

Recently I put on Subway to Sally and went questing through Silverpine. That felt like a little time machine: the same old forest, but this time with a class I couldn't have played as undead Horde in Vanilla. And I have to think about gold again. In Classic Era, new alts got 250 gold and four large bags from my established level-60 characters. Now every purchase counts. Baron Gorthak has work to do.

My verdict: Classic Era in Redux, not another Retail

After 22 levels I'm having a wonderful time. My biggest surprise is the friendliness of the community. I hope the developers don't make everything progressively easier. No automatic dungeon queues, no solo groups filled with bots, no achievement pop-ups for every trivial action, and nothing beyond cosmetic upgrades in a shop. New quests and good-looking water? Lovely. I just want the core of Classic to stay intact.

For someone completely new to WoW, I'd actually suggest playing Classic Era first, perhaps up to level 20, then trying Forever and comparing. If an old Vanilla veteran asks me whether to switch, my first question would be: “Have you played Classic Era?” It is still lively: I logged in today for an hour and was immediately asked to heal Molten Core on Desponia. I couldn't — there was chili con carne to cook.

My plan is one paladin to level 60, earn Quel'Serrar myself, and raid with him. When he's on a raid cooldown, I'll take a break or play Classic Era instead. Nebukatneza, Desponia, Bearforceone, Arganosh, Onenightstab and the rest stay there. Including Kurtcowbain, my usually friendly healing shaman, who sometimes gets angry, grabs his two-handed Nightfall and hopes for that priceless Windfury proc in a battleground. I'm not taking another eight characters to level 60. And I'm never farming the rogue T0.5 set again. Thirty-nine Stratholme runs and about seventeen UBRS runs were plenty for one lifetime.

WoW Forever: After 20 Years as Nebukatneza, I’m Dennis Hilk

For roughly twenty years, I went by Nebukatneza in Azeroth. Then, in November 2026, I walked into the world with a different name above my head for the first time. It said Dennis Hilk. Two decades to come up with a new character name, and I went with the one I already had. The fellow wearing it is an undead paladin.

How Nebukatneza became nebu

My first World of Warcraft home was Destromath, a German EU PvP realm. Horde, undead warlock, guild Klinge der Macht. At some point Nebukatneza became nebu, and the name followed me well beyond WoW. I’ve written separately about how I ended up being called nebu.

My warlock’s story carried on after level 60. I played every expansion from The Burning Crusade through Dragonflight, and Nebukatneza stayed my main. Sometimes I took a break and came back halfway through an expansion. The continents, talent trees and rules kept changing. Whenever I came back, I still had my warlock.

From Vanilla to Dragonflight, one expansion at a time

At first, what I noticed was how much bigger the world got. There was Outland on the other side of the Dark Portal, and then there was Northrend. Over time, WoW also changed how I spent an evening. Dungeon Finder took care of finding a group. Garrisons, artifact weapons and Covenants gave their respective expansions their own progression systems. Mythic+ gave dungeon groups a challenge that kept scaling with higher keystones. Some parts of the game got easier to get into while others gave players more to master.

Dragonflight brought back branching talent trees and gave professions more depth through specializations and crafting orders. Every few years, the developers made a fresh decision about where to ask for more of a player’s time and where to give some back.

Why Dragonflight was the last expansion I bought

“Candy Crush meets World of Warcraft — too much bling-bling everywhere.” That’s how I jokingly put it. Retail had started to feel too bright and too loud for me. Effects, achievement banners and rewards called for my attention so often that I ended up noticing them less. When the next progress bar was already filling before I’d enjoyed the last one, even a proper achievement started to feel a bit ordinary.

In the parts of the game I played, I often felt I needed less patience or skill to earn a reward than I remembered. High Mythic+ keys and Mythic raids still demand practice and coordination. Plenty of players enjoy the more accessible starting points, the bold look and the modern systems. I missed a quieter atmosphere, slower leveling and the satisfaction of getting something I’d spent a while working toward.

Dragonflight was where my own retail journey ended. I didn’t buy the retail expansions that followed it.

Back to Classic Era and eight level-60 characters

Classic Era felt right again. A trip across the world could take up an evening, and if I skipped a week, the dungeon set I’d worked for was still good enough for whatever I planned to do next. Over time I built up eight level-60 characters on Noggenfogger, plus a level-one auction-house orc. He’s the one with the loudest axe in the picture. You have to have priorities.

The Nebukatneza in this list carries on the name of my old Destromath warlock. I can’t find that very first character anymore. I look at this roster and still know exactly where “nebu” came from.

WoW Forever and a paladin named Dennis Hilk

World of Warcraft: Forever launched on 5 November 2026 here in Germany, on the same global launch that fell on 4 November in North America. Blizzard is building it as a permanent version alongside retail and Classic. The original continents are once again the setting for levels 1 through 60, with new quests and reworked zones. The two changes that mattered most to my fresh start were first names and surnames, and the chance to play a Forsaken paladin.

In Vanilla, paladins were Alliance-only. Blood elves brought the class to the Horde in The Burning Crusade. I played a Horde paladin back then and kept the warlock as my main. In Forever I wanted to see how the world felt if I started with the paladin this time. I did at least stick with the undead and the Horde.

I play in the EU in German on the Normal (PvE) ruleset. The public profile also showed level 14 when I checked it on 10 November. That number stays in the screenshot as the beginning of this story, even as the profile eventually moves on.

How the character card works

How the WoW Forever Observer works

The independent Armory describes two delays. Characters appear from level 10 after logging out, and the Armory can still serve an older saved snapshot. A future sync can turn higher levels and newly reported achievements into dated milestones. The moment it happened in-game remains unknown. If a character gains several levels between checks, the history can record only the level actually observed.

Blizzard’s official API returns my Forever character’s level, experience, equipment and professions. On 10 November 2026, I successfully ran the Python collector on my Debian 13 server, Worldnode, and published its first real JSON snapshot. It contains only selected display data: no credentials and no permanent gameplay history. The website shows the last successful sync time; the hourly automated run is configured separately. An older snapshot is not live telemetry.
